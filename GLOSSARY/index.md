Terse definitions as the book uses the terms. Each heading links to a full concept file with locations, related terms, secondary glosses and disagreements.

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| [**Spirit** (*esprit*, *valeur esprit*)](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/spirit/index.md>) | Noetic life (attention, memory, judgement, sublimated desire), psychic and collective and technically supported. Valéry's "value" that can fall like a commodity price. |
| [**Industrial populism**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/industrial-populism/index.md>) | Populism produced by the economy: culture industries and marketing levelling attention to drives and selling "available brain time". Political populism follows from it. |
| [**Libidinal economy**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/libidinal-economy/index.md>) | The organization of desire. Consumer capitalism captured libido as its energy and, by capturing it, destroys it, reducing desire to drives. |
| [**Symbolic misery**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/symbolic-misery/index.md>) | Loss of individuation through losing a part in producing symbols. Leads to "spiritual misery". |
| [**Hyperindustrial society**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/hyperindustrial-society/index.md>) | Not post-industrial. Industrialization extended to services, culture and cognition. Service capitalism controls how ways of life are adopted. |
| [**Tertiary retention**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/tertiary-retention/index.md>) | Memory held in artefacts. It conditions perception (primary retention) and recollection (secondary retention). |
| [**Grammatization**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/grammatization/index.md>) | Discretization of flows (speech, gesture, perception) into reproducible marks, from the alphabet to the digital. Each stage both enables knowledge and threatens it. |
| [**Hypomnemata**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/hypomnemata/index.md>) | Memory supports. Writing and all mnemotechnics. They can serve care of the self or replace memory. |
| [**Pharmakon** / pharmacology](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/pharmakon/index.md>) | Every technics is remedy and poison. A therapeutic (care) decides which. Explicit already in Ch. 1 (pp. 47–51) and systematized in the 2010 Manifesto. |
| [**Proletarianization**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/proletarianization/index.md>) | Loss of knowledge to apparatuses: workers' *savoir-faire*, consumers' *savoir-vivre*, thinkers' theoretical knowledge. |
| [**Otium / negotium**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/otium-negotium/index.md>) | Studious leisure for existence and self-cultivation, against business for subsistence. Service capitalism folds otium into negotium. |
| [**Care** (*soin*)](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/care/index.md>) | Attention to self, others and world, sustained by techniques of the self. "To economize means… to take care." Its opposite is carelessness (*incurie*). |
| [**Short-termism**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/short-termism/index.md>) | The drive's temporality in finance, management and media. It destroys investment, both financial and libidinal. |
| [**Economy of contribution**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/economy-of-contribution/index.md>) | Industrial model without a producer/consumer split. Positive externalities, capabilities, de-proletarianization. Free software is the model. |
| [**Associated / dissociated milieu**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/associated-milieu/index.md>) | Simondon. A milieu its participants co-individuate (associated), against one individuated by specialists at others' expense (dissociated). |
| [**Individuation / transindividuation / disindividuation**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/transindividuation/index.md>) | Simondon. Becoming who one is, psychically and collectively. The co-production of a "we". Its breakdown through short-circuits. |
| [**Disenchantment / re-enchantment**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/disenchantment/index.md>) | Weber's rationalization, radicalized into the destruction of desire and of capitalism's "spirit". Medef's slogan, taken at its word. |
| [**Technologies of spirit**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/technologies-of-spirit/index.md>) | The converged culture, media, telecom and cognitive industries. Psychotechnologies (control) against nootechnologies (the life of the mind). |
| [**Knowledge vs information**](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/knowledge-vs-information/index.md>) | Information as a commodity is entropic (value decays). Knowledge is negentropic (lasts, intensifies). Confusing them yields a "reign of ignorance". |

## Other terms you will meet

- **R technologies**: relational technologies (Ch. 1, p. 29): telecom and broadcast networks, RFID, chip cards, wi-fi, Bluetooth, biometrics, nanotech, used as technologies of control. See [technologies of spirit](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/technologies-of-spirit/index.md>).
- **Dissociated milieu**: a milieu in which production and consumption are separated, so participants lose their knowledge and cannot individuate themselves (Ch. 1, pp. 34–37). See [associated milieu](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/associated-milieu/index.md>).
- **Plane of consistencies / motifs ("the plane that enchants me")**: the incalculable objects of desire (works, the beloved, idealities) which "do not exist" but *consist*, and must be cared for (Ch. 1, pp. 58–60).
- **Discharge**: services relieve the consumer of tasks and so of his existence (Ch. 1, pp. 30–31).
- **Meta-transformation**: the capacity to transform the way ways of life are transformed (Ch. 1, pp. 44–45).
- ***Savoir-faire / savoir-vivre***: know-how / knowing how to live. Left in French by the translator. See [proletarianization](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/proletarianization/index.md>).
- **Available brain time** (*temps de cerveau humain disponible*): Patrick Le Lay (TF1), 2004. See [context](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/context-le-lay-temps-de-cerveau-2004/index.md>).
- **Medef**: French employers' federation. Its 2005 summer university was titled "Réenchanter le monde". See [context](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/context-medef-universite-ete-2005/index.md>).
- **Program industries / program institutions**: culture and media industries against schools and universities. See [Ch. 2](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/chapters/05-ch2-against-the-reign-of-ignorance/index.md>) p. 90.
- **Adoption**: the process by which ways of life are taken up. Individuation is adoption. See [Ch. 1](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/chapters/04-ch1-re-foundation-of-society/index.md>) §3.
- **General organology**: the co-evolution of bodily organs, technical organs and social organizations. See [grammatization](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/grammatization/index.md>).
- **Desublimation**: loss of the capacity to sublimate drives into desire for singular objects. See [libidinal economy](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/concepts/libidinal-economy/index.md>).
- ***Sursaut*** ("jumpstart", also rendered "sudden surge", Introduction p. 5): a leap and rupture that re-founds the "human enterprise" (*entreprendre de fonder*). See [Ch. 1](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/chapters/04-ch1-re-foundation-of-society/index.md>) pp. 44–47.
