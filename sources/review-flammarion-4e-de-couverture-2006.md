# Flammarion back-cover text (2006) and booksellers' summaries

The Flammarion site rejected automated access, so I read the blurb only as reproduced by third parties: Babelio (truncated), leslibraires.ca and SensCritique (short summary), homocoques.com (full blurb plus dedication and an extract, 19 April 2012), and the Philharmonie de Paris catalogue (summary). Below is a paraphrase, not the text.

## Condensed account
1. **Opening:** Paul Valéry, foreseeing where Nazism was leading, noted as early as 1939 a "baisse de la valeur esprit". That was a time when radio was little listened to and television did not yet exist. Today communicating devices capture "available brain time" (*temps de cerveau disponible*, Le Lay's phrase).
2. **Occasion:** the 2005 Medef summer university took "re-enchanting the world" as its theme. The book takes this at its word and argues that re-enchantment requires rethinking the place of spirit (*esprit*) in organising the economy.
3. **Thesis (leslibraires.ca and SensCritique):** Bernard Stiegler, president of Ars Industrialis, and the association's co-founders argue that the fall in the "valeur esprit" has become the very principle of capitalism. Facing coming civilisational and economic crises, they call for fighting the reign of ignorance and for an industrial policy of the "technologies of spirit", including media and television.
4. **Diagnosis (homocoques):** contemporary hyperindustrial capitalism treats the mind as a mere organ of reflexes, undermining thought and culture.
5. **Credits:** co-founders Georges Collins, Marc Crépon, Catherine Perret and Caroline Stiegler. The Philharmonie record notes that the volume includes the Ars Industrialis Manifesto.
6. **Dedication (homocoques transcription):** "A la mémoire de Simon Nora." / "A Laurence Parisot". Nora co-wrote the 1978 Nora–Minc report on the informatisation of society, cf. EN Ch.2 §"from the informatization of society…", p. 61. Parisot was then president of the Medef. The dedication is presumably pointed, given the Medef framing.

## Reader reception (same pages)
- Babelio: 3.5/5 from 24 ratings. Short reader notes stress the Medef riposte, the critique of "temps de cerveau disponible", and the call for an "industrial ecology of spirit".
- SensCritique (reader "Denaya", 19 Aug 2011): the writing is "souvent inintelligible", with sentences that are too long, badly built and obscure.

## Notes for evaluation
- The blurb confirms that the **Introduction** starts from Valéry and the Medef slogan. That the Manifesto is included in the 2006 edition is confirmed.
- The dedication (Nora; Parisot) is not mentioned in any EN source seen. Check whether the Bloomsbury edition keeps it.
- Promotional text. Reliable for framing, not for content.
