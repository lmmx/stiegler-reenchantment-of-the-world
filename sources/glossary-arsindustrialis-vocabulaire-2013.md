**Status: primary-adjacent.** Written inside the association (the page states it was drafted by Victor Petit and published in *Pharmacologie du Front National*, 2013). It is the association's own house glossary of 48 entries, so it reproduces Stiegler's positions rather than evaluating them. Read in full from the single-page version (`/vocabulaire`); individual entries live at `arsindustrialis.org/vocabulaire-<slug>`. No page numbers (web text); the entry name is given instead.

## Condensed account (by concept)

- **Spirit (entry "Esprit").** Opens with Valéry (*La liberté de l'esprit*, 1939): life is a power of reciprocal transformation with a milieu, and human life also creates a "supplement" of values, the *valeur esprit*. Note 24 quotes Valéry's comparison of a value called "spirit" with the values of oil, wheat or gold. This spiritual capital is made of material things (books, paintings, instruments), but those things count as capital only if people know how to use them. When people can no longer use the technologies of spirit imposed on them, spirit loses its capital and capitalism loses its spirit. Note 26 says capitalism *suffers from having no spirit* and contrasts this with Boltanski and Chiapello's "new spirit". Spirit is neither an immaterial soul nor brain matter. It is what brain activity becomes when it turns external things into memory supports (tertiary retentions), so it is psychic, social and technical at once. Spirit is finally defined as the dynamic of technically mediated **transindividuation**: the "and" in Simondon's "psychic and collective individuation".
- **Libidinal economy ("Économie libidinale").** Libido, a Freudian concept, is the socialised and desexualised energy of the drives, turned into objects of investment through sublimation. It is always channelled through artefacts (fetishism), which is why it can be industrialised. Twentieth-century capitalism made libido its main energy and channels it towards consumer goods so that overproduction is absorbed. Unlimited managerial exploitation of libido destroys desire, so a "renewable" libidinal energy is needed, which makes this an *ecological* problem. A healthy market economy combines investment with sublimation. Related entries: "Désir/Pulsion" (consumerism reduces desire to drive) and "Investissement" (*Besetzung* means both financial and libidinal investment).
- **Industrial populism ("Populisme industriel et télécratie").** Today's populism is "of a new kind" because it comes from economic organisation rather than from properly political causes. When consumerist societies reach their limits, life becomes drive-based and addictive, and the resulting regression produces political populism *secondarily*. "Telecracy" is when mass audiovisual media short-circuit political transindividuation, with television acting as marketing's "secular arm". The entry adds claims about children's screen exposure and synaptogenesis.
- **Symbolic misery ("Misère symbolique").** This is the loss of individuation that comes from losing participation in the production of symbols. It has grown since culture's and politics' "machinic turn". The National Front is to be fought by fighting symbolic misery, which in turn means fighting industrial populism.
- **Proletarianization ("Prolétarisation").** It means depriving a subject (producer, consumer or designer) of savoir-faire, savoir-vivre, or the knowledge to conceive and theorise. The entry reads Marx as saying the working class was only the *first* class to be proletarianised. In the 20th century the consumer's savoir-vivre was proletarianised too: marketing turns "mode de vie" into "mode d'emploi". After 2008, designers and decision-makers are proletarianised by automated decision systems. The process is "not a fatality" but a pharmacological question, and generalised de-proletarianisation is the stake of the economy of contribution. The proletarian is "the employee of a dissociated milieu" (Simondon).
- **Associated vs dissociated milieu ("Milieu").** "Associated milieu" comes from Simondon (technical individuation, the Guimbal turbine). "Dissociated milieu" is explicitly credited to Stiegler. A milieu is associated when individuals contribute to individuating it, and dissociated when it is individuated by experts at their expense. Culture industries dissociated symbolic milieus by splitting senders from receivers, and service economies deepened this. The internet is "not structurally dissociated", which is what makes contributory economy possible.
- **Pharmakon.** The word means remedy, poison and scapegoat. Every technical object is pharmacological. The pharmakon is both what enables care and what must be cared for. The entry cites Derrida's "Pharmacie de Platon" (note 55) and develops a "positive pharmacology", which may nonetheless prohibit some pharmaka (the example is nuclear power). Economising means taking care.
- **Psychotechnics / nootechnics.** This is a normative opposition, "assumed as such". Psychotechnics act on or against the mind in order to control it, whereas nootechnics foster the mind's individuation. The same technology can be either (pharmacology). What makes a technology a nootechnics is reciprocity, where the reader can also write, together with long circuits of transindividuation.
- **Otium/negotium.** Negotium is the calculable sphere of business and subsistence, and otium the sphere of leisure and existence. They must stay distinct without being metaphysically opposed. Both use hypomnemata. Otium aims at "consistences" (ideal objects, sublimation) and involves disciplines or techniques of self (Foucault's writing of the self). Service industries now short-circuit otium and fold it into negotium. Related entries: "Skholè" and "Subsister, exister, consister".
- **Short-termism ("Financiarisation").** Finance is a "commerce of promises". The entry quotes Stiegler (*Pour une nouvelle critique de l'économie politique*, 2009): fully realised short-termism destroys the retentions (knowledge) and protentions (investments) of time.
- **Grammatization.** This is the discretisation of temporal continua (gestures, speech, calculation) into reproducible spatial "grammes". Stiegler borrows and diverts Sylvain Auroux's term (note 33) and links it to Derrida's grammatology. The entry gives three stages: literal, analog and digital. The digital stage is that of "hyperindustrial society".
- **Tertiary retention ("Attention, rétention, protention").** Primary and secondary retention are Husserlian. Tertiary retentions are hypomnesic sedimentations in artefacts, and they overdetermine secondary retentions, which in turn overdetermine primary ones.
- **Economy of contribution.** It has three traits: producer and consumer are no longer separated; the value produced is not fully monetisable (it is a positive externality); and it is an economy of existences as much as of subsistences. The contributor is neither consumer, taxpayer nor gift-giver. The amateur is its ideal type ("Amateur"). The entry rejects the "creative economy" as hyperconsumerist.
- **Transindividuation.** The term is derived from Simondon's "transindividual". In Stiegler's version, the diachronising co-individuation of "I"s produces the synchronising transindividuation of a "we". The preindividual milieu is "intrinsically artefactual", and technics is the "third strand" beside the psychic and the social. When technologies serve proletarianisation they short-circuit transindividuation.
- **Le Lay.** In the "Marketing" entry (note 44), Le Lay's "temps de cerveau disponible" remark illustrates the "cynical lucidity" of the culture industries.

## Short quotations
- "Lorsque les hommes ne savent plus se servir des technologies de l'esprit qui leur sont imposées, c'est alors l'esprit qui a perdu son capital, c'est aussi bien le capitalisme qui a perdu son esprit" (entry "Esprit").
- "La misère symbolique est la perte d'individuation qui résulte de la perte de participation à la production de symboles" (entry "Misère").
- "ce populisme est, en sa nature profonde, d'un genre nouveau, en ceci qu'il procède d'une organisation économique bien plus que d'une causalité proprement politique" (entry "Populisme industriel").
- The consumer's way of life is turned by marketing "en mode d'emploi" (entry "Prolétarisation").

## Notes for evaluation
- **Reliability:** high as a statement of the association's vocabulary. It is not neutral, since it is advocacy and self-description. It postdates *Réenchanter* (2006) and includes later developments (pharmacology, 2008 crisis, telecracy, "mécroissance"), so it should be used carefully as a gloss on 2006 usage. In particular, the systematic "pharmacology" vocabulary was developed mainly from c. 2007–2010 (*Ce qui fait que la vie vaut la peine d'être vécue*, 2010), so its prominence here may over-systematise the 2006 book (to verify against the text).
- **Stance:** a Simondon–Freud–Derrida–Valéry–Foucault synthesis, presented normatively (the psycho/nootechnics opposition is openly "normative").
- **Weak points:** empirical claims about screens and synaptogenesis are asserted without discussion. The economic entries (contribution, finance) lean on French heterodox economists (Orléan, Giraud, Moulier-Boutang, Azaïs et al.).
- **Usefulness:** it gives the best compact French definitions for nearly every slug. The "Esprit" entry reconstructs the Valéry "valeur esprit" source that gives the book its title.
