## Condensed account
- **Three capitalisms.** Stiegler distinguishes pre-industrial, industrial (producers proletarianised) and 20th-century consumerist capitalism, which was designed to defer Marx's contradictions. Ars Industrialis departs from Marx: capitalism postpones its contradictions rather than overcoming them. The same three-phase schema appears in the Ars Industrialis "Capitalisme(s)" glossary entry.
- **Proletarianization.** Producers lose savoir-faire, and in the 20th century "ce sont les consommateurs qui perdent leur savoir-vivre". The result is generalised proletarianisation, reaching even Greenspan, who did not understand the system he ran, and engineers trained in skills without the history of their discipline.
- **Contribution.** Free software is "un modèle industriel déprolétarisant". Contribution must replace the oppositions between producer and consumer and between capital and labour. Contributors individuate themselves by helping others individuate (co-individuation).
- **Motivation and care.** Workplace motivation has collapsed; Stiegler cites Weber on capitalism's need for motivation. Jérôme Kerviel's trading is described as addiction rather than investment. The opposite of curiosity is *incurie*, the absence of care, which characterises contemporary society.
- **Digital pharmakon.** Digital technology can produce stupidity or be cared for, and it can serve surveillance (UK cameras).

- A popular, oral restatement. It is useful mainly as evidence of continuity between the 2005 manifesto's economy of contribution and the free-software model, and for the *cure/incurie* (care) theme.
