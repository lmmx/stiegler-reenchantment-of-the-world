## Condensed account
- **Proletarianization.** Manche treats it as a "noetic" process, a generalised loss of knowledge that amounts to "becoming stupid". Stiegler introduces the term in *Technics and Time 3* and develops it from *Symbolic Misery* (2004) onward into a three-part loss: savoir-faire, savoir-vivre and savoir-théoriser. She lists *Réenchanter le monde* (2006) among the works that develop it. The three losses map onto three eras (19th-century industrial capitalism, 20th-century Fordist consumerism, and today's digital/automatic paradigm) and are cumulative. Proletarianisation occurs when the cycle of exteriorising and reinteriorising knowledge breaks (note 30 quotes *Automatic Society*: "exteriorization without return").
- **Tertiary retention / pharmakon.** She gives a standard account: Husserl's primary and secondary retentions are extended to tertiary retentions (externalised, intergenerational memory), and Plato's *Phaedrus* is read via Derrida, with technics as pharmakon and hypomnēmata.
- **Economy of contribution.** It is meant to counter automation's effects on individuation by rewarding contributions to collective knowledge, through a conditional "contributive income". Manche traces this to Gorz's "second cheque" and to the French *intermittents du spectacle* regime (citing Corsani and Lazzarato). Stiegler also wants an unconditional minimum income for "fragile" people. The Plaine Commune "contributive learning territory" involves corporate partners (Orange, Dassault Systèmes).
- **Short-termism.** It is mentioned only within the automation and investment debate, not as a stand-alone concept.

## Short quotations
- "Proletarianisation is the problem, not capitalism." (Manche, final section; this is her reconstruction of Stiegler's stance)
- Stiegler, cited by Manche (note 1, *Ballast* 2019): "what I'm interested in is to put down capitalism, for good. Or to do something else in the meanwhile."
- Stiegler, cited (note 28, *States of Shock*): "the proletariat is not the working class, but the non-working class".

## Notes for evaluation
- **Stance:** Marxist critique. Manche holds that Stiegler's Marx comes via the Franco-Italian (post-)operaist reading of the *Grundrisse* "Fragment on Machines". In her view this produces a teleological view of technology and automation-unemployment forecasts that the economic evidence does not bear out. It also sidelines the profit motive and capital accumulation and yields a "good vs bad capitalism" reformism, which risks self-exploitation and co-optation by corporate partners.
- **Value:** the most rigorous critical piece found on proletarianisation and contribution. It is a useful counterweight to sympathetic readings (Ross, Lemmens, Ars Industrialis). It bears on the book's 2005/2010 manifestos, which explicitly seek to "save capitalism from itself" by re-founding its spirit (check the manifesto text).
- **Accuracy:** its exposition of Stiegler's concepts is careful and conventional. The critique targets the later work (*Automatic Society*, *L'emploi est mort*) more than the 2006 book.
