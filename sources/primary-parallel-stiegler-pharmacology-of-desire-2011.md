## Condensed account
- **Libidinal economy and psychopower (pp. 150–151).** The starting point is Edward Bernays, Freud's "double nephew" and the inventor of public relations and so of marketing. Twentieth-century libidinal economy was transformed by marketing into a **psychopower**, the counterpart of Foucault's biopower. Psychopower channels consumers' libido toward commodities so that industrial investment can return a profit.
- **Subsistence, existence and consistence (p. 151).** The "historicity of the unconscious" lies in the relation between an economy of subsistence (needs), an economy of existence (desires), and "consistences" (objects of idealisation). The present crisis is the collapse of the consumerist arrangement between these, through the liquidation of consistences, that is, of sublimation.
- **Disposability (p. 151).** Consumerism is an economy of disposability and "infidelity": objects must be disinvested as soon as they are invested. Its counterpart is speculative finance (hedge funds, LBOs), which is structural disinvestment. This drive-based consumption links to **short-termism**.
- **Organology (pp. 152–155).** Freud's "organic repression" (upright stance, olfaction) is reread with Leroi-Gourhan as prosthetic de- and re-functionalisation. There is "no libido without aesthetics", and the libidinal aesthetic is "pros-thesic". Political, symbolic and libidinal economy form three levels of general organology.
- **Transindividuation (pp. 156–158).** Superegoic and sublimatory "retentional systems" depend on intergenerational long circuits of transindividuation, which psychotechnologies short-circuit. The result is regressive identification (Freud's *Massenpsychologie*) and "generalised proletarianisation", that is, loss of savoir-vivre and systemic "dis-apprenticeship". Long circuits are modelled on the Socratic dialogue and anamnesis.
- **Associated vs dissociated milieus (p. 158).** Associated milieus are transitional milieus (Winnicott) in which participants co-individuate their milieu. The opposition between producers and consumers of symbols "de-symbolises" consumers, who no longer take part in shaping their milieu. Dissociated milieus are therefore toxic and dis-individuating. This is the conceptual core of **symbolic misery**, although the term itself is not used.
- **Le Lay (p. 159).** Psychotechnologies standardise secondary retentions. That is what Le Lay called selling "available brain time" to Coca-Cola.
- **Libidinal ecology (p. 159).** Drive-based capitalism is "addictogenic": consumers' addiction forms a system with speculators' "ultra-short-termist" behaviour.
- **Investment and money (pp. 159–160).** Investment is both libidinal (binding drives over time) and capitalist (a system of protentions). Money is a tertiary retention and protention, and a pharmakon: it turns belief into trust (calculability), and that calculability ruins trust.
- **Disenchantment (p. 160).** Weber's secularisation and disenchantment has reached "extreme disenchantment". In this limit, three limits of capitalism potentise one another: the falling profit rate, falling libidinal energy, and negative externalities. A new pharmacology (digital) opens the possibility of reconstituting associated milieus.
- **Bêtise / Marcuse (pp. 160–161).** Generalised proletarianisation leads to "systemic stupidity", or *bêtise systémique*. The difference from Marcuse is that Marcuse wants to "liberate the instincts", whereas Stiegler holds that the artefact (pharmakon) conditions desire itself. Fuller treatment is referred to *Mécréance et discrédit 3: L'esprit perdu du capitalisme* (2006).

## Notes for evaluation
- **Relation to the book:** this is the most compact free English statement of the libidinal-economy thesis that runs through *Réenchanter* (desire as capitalism's energy, the exhaustion of libido, Le Lay, the need for investment rather than speculation). It explicitly links that thesis to Weber's disenchantment ("extreme disenchantment"), so it is valuable for the re-enchantment slug. Its companion volume, *L'esprit perdu du capitalisme*, appeared in the same year as *Réenchanter*.
- **Vocabulary drift:** the systematic pharmacology and "dis-economy" language is post-2008, as in *Ce qui fait que la vie vaut la peine d'être vécue* (2010).
- **Reliability:** authorial and authoritative, with a peer-reviewed venue and a careful Ross translation that keeps the French glosses.
