# Stiegler, "The Uncontrollable Societies of Disaffected Individuals" (2006; *Nordic Journal of Aesthetics*, 2013)

The *Nordic Journal of Aesthetics* text on "the lowering of the spirit value". It is a 2006 text, written at the same time as *Réenchanter le monde*, and it names Medef's 2005 summer university.

## Condensed account
- **Spirit (p. 129).** The text opens: "Symbolic misery leads irresistibly to spiritual misery." *Esprit* is a "noetic process that is both psychic and collective (cerebral and social)". It is what connects the I to the we, the "and" of psychic and collective individuation, "just as is, moreover, technics". It is not "vapour" or pure form. It passes through the organisation of matter. Knowledge (*connaissance*) is spirit's refined fruit, resting on savoir-faire and savoir-vivre. The translator's note (n. 1) says *esprit* means both mind and spirit, with no "vaporously 'spiritual'" sense.
- **Proletarianization (pp. 129–130).** Simondon held that the worker-become-proletarian is disindividuated. Stiegler extends this to consumers' savoir-vivre, which gives "generalized proletarianization". He doubts that industrialised cognitive technologies still yield individuation even in science.
- **Spiritual misery (p. 130).** It is the blockage of the psychic and social circuits that constitute objects of spirit (objects of admiration, sublimation and love). It produces anxiety, regression to drives and addictions, "panic" (herd reactions) and the "reign of stupidity". This is "the lowering of the spirit value, *la valeur esprit*" (Valéry), and ultimately despair (*dés-espoir*), the destruction of Kant's kingdom of ends.
- **Disenchantment (pp. 130–131).** Weber's disenchantment carries a becoming-reactive and stupid, which is Nietzsche's nihilism. It also carries "the potential for an unprecedented trans-formation", a "new epoch of the spirit". Carried to extremes, it lets capitalism conquer the world but leads to "the loss of the capitalist spirit". Against Boltanski and Chiapello, there is no "new spirit of capitalism": capitalism is threatened by itself. That is why **Medef took "the re-enchantment of the world" as the theme of its 2005 summer school** (p. 131).
- **Industrial populism (p. 132).** The loss of spirit "massively" takes the form of industrial populism: the exploitation of "available brain time", targeting drive-based and regressive mechanisms, against spirit understood as care for the objects and subjects of desire, including children. It destroys shame (*aidos*), the superego and sublimation, and leads to cynicism, acting out and "negative sublimation" (terrorism). This is cross-referenced to *Constituer l'Europe 1*.
- **Hyper-industrial society (pp. 132–133).** "Absolute disenchantment" is the loss of every horizon of expectation, of belief and of *philia*, especially for those who expect nothing from "hyper-industrial society". The hyper-power of the technical system depends on trust and turns into hyper-vulnerability once trust is lost.
- **Attention and control (p. 133).** Industrial populism arises from the destruction of attention through control technologies, above all TV (Deleuze, Daney). Control of primary and secondary identification by industrial temporal objects leads to dis-identification and collective disindividuation. The result is "disaffected" individuals, in both senses: affect is ruined (*désaffection*) and their place, their ethos, is lost (*désaffectation*).

## Short quotations (Stiegler, trans. Ross)
- "Symbolic misery leads irresistibly to spiritual misery." (p. 129)
- "spirit is what exceeds the I and connects it to the we, the condition of the 'and' of psychic and collective individuation" (p. 129)
- "Disenchantment, when carried to extremes, may have allowed capitalism to conquer the entire world, but it eventually leads to the loss of the capitalist spirit." (p. 131)
- "This loss of spirit above all takes the form, massively, of industrial populism, the exploitation of 'available brain time'" (p. 132)

## Notes for evaluation
- **Relevance:** very high. It is contemporaneous (2006) and runs the exact chain of the book: symbolic misery, then spiritual misery and the fall in the value of spirit, then disenchantment, the loss of capitalism's spirit, Medef's "re-enchantment", and industrial populism and "available brain time". It is the best free primary text for checking how the book uses *esprit* and *populisme industriel*.
- **Translation:** Ross's translation and note on *esprit* (mind and spirit) are useful to compare with Trevor Arthur's choices in the Bloomsbury book.
- **Reliability:** authorial and published, though only an excerpt (pp. 129–134).
