# Cherrier, lelitteraire.com (2012)

## Condensed account

A short general-audience review. Starting from Valéry's 1939 remark, it asks where "valeur esprit" stands in a consumer society saturated with communication. Its main thesis: today's technology serves communication more than thought, so people end up **informed but poorly educated**. This is the reviewer's rendering of ch. 2's information/knowledge distinction. The review worries about the loss of individuality and the standardisation of mass culture, with reality TV as the example. It notes that the essay draws on Hannah Arendt and on Ars Industrialis's work. Its verdict: a pertinent and ambitious essay that is hard going for non-specialists because of its vocabulary (hypomnémata, pharmaka…).

## Short quotations

- "une bible pour les philosophes".
- "l'ouvrage est peu accessible aux non-initiés".

## Notes for evaluation

- Accurate but thin. It does not go chapter by chapter and does not mention the Medef or Le Lay context, the manifestos or the Tunis motion. The Arendt reference appears mainly in the manifesto's "vie de l'esprit", so it is fair but marginal to the chapters.
- The bibliographic data (Flammarion, Oct 2006, 172 pp., €12) and the list of co-founders (Crépon, Collins, Perret, Caroline Stiegler) are correct. The reviewer writes "Georges" Collins.
- It contains a terminology slip ("épokhal" for *épokhè*), which suggests a non-specialist reviewer.
