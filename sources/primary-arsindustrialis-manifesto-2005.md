# Ars Industrialis 2005 Manifesto (web version)

**Status:** primary text. The book reprints it at pp. 11–16 of the English edition. We read the association's own English web version, which is **not** Trevor Arthur's translation. Wording therefore differs from the book, but the content should match. Confirm against the printed text.

## Condensed account

1. **Threat.** The "life of the mind/spirit" (Arendt) risks total subjection to the market. Firms exploiting the culture industries, program industries, media, telecoms and finally cognitive technologies drive this. Digitization makes these sectors **converge**, and the converged sector is named the **technologies of spirit**. Thesis: convergence has so far intensified *control*, but these technologies can and must open a new age of spirit.
2. **Industrial politics = industrial ecology of spirit.** Under market criteria alone the technologies of spirit serve **societies of control** (Burroughs → Deleuze). That means short-term financial return, and new social practices are blocked. Properly used, they could found a new epoch of civilization and avert a widely sensed chaos.
3. **Desire destroyed.** These technologies of "soul" and "consciousness", now joined by bio-technologies, aim to control modes of existence at every stage of life. Control of desire destroys the *singularity* that existing requires, and it destroys **libidinal energy** (Freud). Twentieth-century capitalism made libido its main energy source, channelled into commodities to absorb excess production. The capture of libido has ended up **destroying** it, which leads "inevitably" to an unprecedented global crisis.
4. **Super-ego and sublimation.** The ruin of desire is the ruin of **sublimation** and of the constitution of the super-ego. It brings geopolitical, political, social and psychic disorders on top of economic ones, under a model that sets production and consumption against each other.
5. **Singularity vs calculation.** Desire forms in symbolic practices supported by techniques. Objects of desire are singular. Industrial fabrication of desire *categorizes* singularities and makes the incalculable calculable. Yet singularity is not outside technics; art aims at "a number that averts counting" (Claudel). ICTs are **hypomnemata** (Foucault's "writing of the self"; Plato's *hypomnesis*). Like ancient hypomnemata they call for new practices and new social organization, not just user manuals and marketing for 15%-plus shareholder returns.
6. **Self-destructive capitalism.** "This capitalism is self-destructive." The association will fight the tendency by inventing practices of spiritual technologies that rebuild objects of desire and experiences of singularity.
7. **Boost desire, not consumption.** The political-economic task is to revive desire, not consumption. The general interest is a question of the symbolic, so a **new form of public power** is needed. It would join firms, public bodies, foundations, associations, economists, artists, scientists, philosophers and investors.
8. **Scope.** Based in Paris, European first, international in aim.
9. **Objectives and programme.** Symposia, publications, motions and studies. A Paris meeting series: European policy on industries of spirit; Google's digital-library initiative; research; the **Tunis WSIS (Nov 2005)**; marketing and advertising; art in industrial society; European languages and idiomatic difference; psychological disorders and public health; intellectual property; US, Latin American, Chinese and Japanese perspectives.

## Short quotations

- "This capitalism is self-destructive."
- The goal is "giving a boost to desire, and not simply giving a boost to consumption".

## Notes for evaluation

- The text is primary, so its accuracy is not in question. The interest is in its **predictive claim** (an "unprecedented global crisis"), which the 2010 Manifesto cites as vindicated by 2007–8.
- **Stance:** Freudian libidinal economy plus Foucault's hypomnemata and Deleuze's control societies. It is reformist and Europeanist ("new public power", with no anti-capitalist programme). This is the root of later charges of "technoreformism" (see [reception.md](../reception.md)).
- The web English is rough, with typos ("instrinsically", "Japn"). Quote from the Bloomsbury text rather than this version.
