# The Tunis motion (French original)

**Status:** primary. **Excerpt only**: per the book's Introduction (p. 9) and the motion's n. 1 (p. 112), *Libération* published part of it and the book prints the complete motion (pp. 95–100; FR p. 163). The printed version adds the intellectual-property argument, numbered points, three conditions, Porto Alegre, the FCC model, Reding and the French government; see [chapters/06](../chapters/06-tunis-motion.md). The book's subheading "The true stakes of the Tunis summit" (p. 95) translates this page's title. It was published two days before the WSIS second phase (Tunis, 16–18 Nov 2005). The book version may differ slightly. Perlego's preview of p. 93 shows the book's closing phrases, close to this text's call for "a new summit" preceded by "a far-reaching debate" and its ending on "the spirit of Europe". Those phrases suggest the printed version is somewhat expanded.

## Condensed account

1. The real stake of the "information society" is UNESCO's **knowledge societies**. The future of capitalism, and of the planet, depends on *raising* intelligence and knowledge. Knowledge is by essence shared and shareable. Digital cognitive and cultural technologies are reshaping the world's whole **public space**. Knowledge societies can arise there only if that space is regulated to serve people's capacity to discern, criticize, create, transmit and receive knowledge.
2. **Tunis is a missed appointment.** The UN summit will not ask the main question: how information, knowledge, technology, industry and society relate, as the object of an international policy to transform a **consumption capitalism** that now yields only **desublimation** and mental, moral, intellectual and aesthetic regression.
3. **Spirit is not (only) religious.** Faced with **spiritual misery**, people feel they need spirit. But spirit should not be placed only in religion and salvation. The life of spirit happens in daily work, relationships and communication tools, above all those reaching mass TV and mobile audiences.
4. **Call for another summit** built on an **industrial politics of technologies of spirit**. Spirit here means both the "available brain time" TV sells and the *œuvres de l'esprit* (works of mind). Valéry is quoted: a world transformed by spirit poses spirit entirely new problems.
5. **Means.** A new form of **public power** working with economic actors should turn ICTs into instruments for a new industrial model aimed at *raising* individual and collective intelligence. If the information society is to be a knowledge society, **television must become an instrument of knowledge**, not of **industrial populism**. Digital technology makes a different TV possible.
6. **Europe.** Europe can build itself on a forward-looking project in the knowledge and culture industries. Public and private funds that do not demand short-term returns should support real research. The model is medicine or physics. Spirit is now the first industrial resource after matter, so new solvencies and new fields of externalities are needed.
7. **New spirit of capitalism.** Planetary problems will force deep behavioural change, which requires sharper intelligences. The question of a "new spirit of capitalism" is posed. Social standard of living is measured by the quality of spiritual life, not protein consumed.
8. **Policy targets.** It welcomes the presidential commission to Jean-Louis Beffa for an industrial-policy report but says the report ignores technologies of spirit. The Barroso Lisbon strategy does not go in this direction. "Cultural diversity" is not enough: what is needed is industrial policy aimed at *raising* social life against **levelling**.
9. **Closing.** The state, the EU and industry must give Europeans hope in their culture again. What is singular to Europe since Greece is a **public space of knowledge**, now no longer only written but made of cognitive and cultural industries. "That is the stake of Tunis."

## Short quotations (translated by us)

- Television must become "an instrument of knowledge, and no longer the instrument of industrial populism that it has become".
- The rise in social living standards "is not measured by the quantity of protein consumed, but by the quality of the life of its spirit".

## Notes for evaluation

- **Primary.** It is the most compact statement of the 2005 position and shows that **industrial populism** was coined with **television** foremost in mind.
- It is strongly **Europeanist** and **state-plus-industry** (Beffa, Lisbon). There is no talk yet of contributory economy or pharmacology.
- On publication: the English book calls it "a motion adopted by Ars Industrialis", while the website says it was "published in Libération". An Ars Industrialis session on 24 Sept 2005 was explicitly held to draft it (see [primary-parallel-stiegler-colline-2005.md](primary-parallel-stiegler-colline-2005.md)).
