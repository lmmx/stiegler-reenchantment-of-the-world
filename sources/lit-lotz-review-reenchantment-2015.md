I read the full text from the author-hosted PDF capture of the review. The original page was at marxandphilosophy.org.uk/reviewofbooks/reviews/2015/1754. Lotz gives page references to the **Bloomsbury 2014 English edition**, and these are kept below in parentheses as "RW n".

## Condensed account
- **Book and project.** Lotz describes the book as presenting the motivations and assumptions of Ars Industrialis (founded in Paris c. 2005). It contains the 2005 manifesto and its 2010 update, two essays, and the Tunis WSIS motion. Lotz judges the **Introduction** to be "the most telling and most transparent text" because it outlines the book's central claims. In his reading, Stiegler "no longer" conceives capitalism in Marxist language. He builds instead on prostheticity (technics and humanity belong together) and seeks new "technologies of spirit" and a politics drawn from the analysis of consciousness industries.
- **Industrial populism and brain time.** The core claim is that contemporary capitalism centres on controlling and producing "brain time". Psychic desires are channelled toward consumption (RW 1) and the consumer is "manufactured" (RW 2). The result is stupidity and "total absence of thought" (RW 68). Knowledge is reduced to information, a calculable form of knowing (RW 23). Memory and imagination become objects of financial investment (RW 88). Own and critical judgement becomes harder (RW 70, 72). Capitalism replaces intelligence with "the time of gregarious audiences, of brains without consciousness … systems of drives" (RW 4).
- **Proletarianization.** Mental life becomes "proletarian" through the externalisation of memory.
- **Transindividuation and disindividuation.** Lotz quotes RW 57: hyper-solicitation of attention short-circuits transindividuation and leads to dis-identification and disindividuation, insensibility, and loss of civility. The young have "lost the feeling of existing" (RW 58).
- **Value of spirit.** The crisis amounts to "demotivation of producers, as well as consumers, which is the concretization of the 'drop in the value of spirit' – … the loss of the spirit of capitalism" (RW 67). Spirit means the capacity "of imagining and concretizing alternatives" (RW 68). The positive programme involves a "battle against regression" (RW 71), "collective intelligence" (RW 75), new "instrumentation" (RW 73), a "new industrial model" and a "new spirit of capitalism" (RW 6), "new, qualitative growth" (RW 3) and a "politics of decentralization" (RW 63).
- **Short-termism / consumerism.** The consumerist economy, "as the organization of an innovation founded upon disposability, waste, carelessness, and blindness, … is in contradiction with the future" (RW 23).
- **Contribution.** The only example given of "cooperative instruments of forms of knowledge" (RW 92) is Wikipedia (RW 87).

## Short quotations
- Lotz: "Overall, Stiegler's reflections remain extremely abstract and empty of any concrete content."
- Lotz: "The position that Stiegler puts forward is at its core idealistic and ethical".
- Stiegler as quoted by Lotz (RW 67): "the concretization of the 'drop in the value of spirit' – which is just as much to say, the loss of the spirit of capitalism".

## Notes for evaluation
- **Stance:** Marxist, from the Marx & Philosophy Society, critical. Stiegler lacks analysis of social and power relations and of the capital/labour relation. Control of labour "evaporates" into control of consumption. There is no dialectic of production and consumption and no account of money or capital. Stiegler returns to Kant and the Enlightenment with a social-democratic reformism. The positive notions are "empty placeholders". Lotz suggests Benkler's *Wealth of Networks* says more concretely what a network economy entails.
- **Value:** the most useful English review for this project. It cites the English edition with page numbers, which makes it a ready-made concordance to key passages (RW 1–4, 23, 57–58, 63, 67–75, 87–92), and it gives a clear statement of the Marxist objection. Its positive point is that the book extends the culture-industry critique to industrial technologies and media systems.
- **Possible slips:** "no longer conceives in a Marxist language" overstates the case, since the book heavily uses Marx's proletarianisation and the 2010 manifesto calls for a new critique of political economy. The charge of abstraction is fair for the essays but less fair for the manifestos' concrete proposals (media policy, contribution).
