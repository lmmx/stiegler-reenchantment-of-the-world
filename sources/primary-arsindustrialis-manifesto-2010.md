# Ars Industrialis 2010 Manifesto (web version)

**Status:** primary text. It is in the English edition at pp. 17–28 but post-dates the French book of 2006 (see [STRUCTURE.md](../STRUCTURE.md)). The English web version was read. Its footnotes are unfinished placeholders ("Cf. Vocabulaire, p…"), so it is a working draft of the translation.

## Condensed account (by numbered section)

1. **Vindication and diagnosis.** The 2005 claim that capitalism had become self-destructive is confirmed by the 2007 subprime crisis. That crisis is not only financial. It is a crisis of the **consumerist model** founded (via Edward Bernays, Freud's nephew) on instrumentalizing desire, which ends by destroying desire. Destroyed desire means destroyed **investment** of every kind. The speculator, a capitalist who no longer invests, and the consumer are both **drive-based**, and their behaviour is systemically linked. **Disinvestment** is the effect of neoliberal **short-termism**. The result is "systemic stupidity" (*bêtise*).
2. **Disposability and addiction.** Consumer objects are made to be thrown away (Schumpeter's "creative destruction"). The waste is ecological, and it is also psychic: people who dispose of things feel disposable themselves. Consumerist society is **addictogenic** (the 2009 French addiction congress is cited). Addicts no longer take care of self, others or world, which produces a society of **carelessness** (*incurie*), a **dissociation**. Hence the axiom: *to economize means first of all to take care* (**care**).
3. **The economy of contribution.** The consumption-based model (early twentieth century, devised to overcome nineteenth-century productivism) has hit its limits through negative externalities: toxic assets, pollution, attention disorders, incivility, "the becoming-mafia of capital". It must give way to the **economy of contribution**, which produces **positive externalities** (care for self and others; Sen's **capabilities**). Free/open-source software is an example. On the net there are no longer producers versus consumers but **contributors** forming an **associated milieu** (Simondon). This restores sublimation and fights addiction.
4. **Pharmacology.** Social networks show hyper-consumerist behaviour that is even more mimetic. Reasons:
   - 4.1. Digital technologies are **hypomnemata**, and therefore **pharmaka** (Plato): poison and remedy.
   - 4.2. All technics is pharmacological. Without a therapeutic (*melete*, *epimeleia*: discipline, care, technique of the self) a pharmakon becomes toxic. Politics, and so political economy, is a **system of care**. Culture is what cultivates a caring relation to pharmaka.
   - 4.3. For two millennia *savoir-vivre* rested on **writing** as the pharmakon of reference (Scripture, humanities, Republic of Letters, press). It spread globally, including via Jesuit missions ahead of industrial markets.
   - 4.4. The culture industries (radio, TV) are new hypomnemata (Benjamin understood this; the Frankfurt School did not). They compete with **program institutions** (schools and universities). Reason is replaced by **rationalization** (Weber, Adorno, Marcuse, Habermas).
   - 4.5. Digital hypomnemata could end this, but marketing seized them first as **relational technologies**.
   - 4.6. Since Thatcher and Reagan, **public power** has renounced industrial policy, which is its proper role: turning "a technical becoming into a social future".
   - 4.7. This failure is "carelessness", with a risk of politico-military-ecological catastrophe.
5. **Political project.** Public power should support the emerging contributory model. Consumerism is hegemonic exactly at its limit. Responsible politics must secure the short term ("keep the wheels turning") *in order to* reach and reverse it in the long term. Each person is both a dependent consumer and a citizen. Consumerism is "in contradiction with the future" (100 million births a year). The goal is a **new industrial economy founded on care**, not "green consumerism". The passage runs from **disapprenticeship** (destroyed *savoir-faire*, *savoir-vivre*, theoretical knowledge; Madoff as symptom) to development of **capabilities**. The digital is a pharmakon that can deepen or end generalized **proletarianization**. Facebook had 500 million members in July 2010.
6. **Proletarianization defined.** It is the process by which knowledge formalized in a technique, machine or apparatus escapes the individual. Smith and Marx: workers lose *savoir-faire*. The twentieth century: consumers lose *savoir-vivre*, replaced by apparatus (the TV set) and services ("available brain time"). Now cognitive workers lose theoretical and critical knowledge, as in Greenspan's admission that he did not understand the system he ran. Free software is exemplary of **de-proletarianization**. **Psychotechnologies** must become **nootechnologies** (technologies of spirit).
7. **Generalization.** The model applies beyond the digital. "Light time" replaces "carbon time". **Smart grids** dissolve the producer/consumer split in energy, and AMAPs (community-supported agriculture) do the same for food. The ethics of contributory business is **care as political economy**. Local authorities should prioritize a pharmacology of networks and Sen-style "capacitation".
8. **Territory.** Relational technologies are localizable and serve **re-territorialization**. The end of consumerism is the end of globalization, giving **post-globalization**. Territory is the ground for political de-proletarianization (Pierre Veltz on learning regions).
9. **National policy (six planks in this web draft; the printed book has seven, adding a plank on rebuilding financial systems against speculation; see [chapters/03](../chapters/03-manifesto-2010.md)):** (9.1) scientific, technological and industrial policy for the new digital system; (9.2) education policy rearranging written knowledge with digital hypomnemata; (9.3) fiscal policy rewarding positive externalities, with work distinct from "employment"; (9.4) cultural policy as social investment and capacitation; (9.5) health policy on psychotechnological toxicity and non-drug addictions, read "pharmacologically" in Plato's sense; (9.6) media policy against **industrial populism**, restoring the press and program industries to forming public space.
10. **Programme.** Working groups on "techniques of the self"; contributive tools such as *Lignes de temps* (IRI software); work with Nantes Métropole and Nord-Pas-de-Calais; a research model likened to the early Frankfurt School.

## Short quotations

- "to economize means first of all and before anything else to take care" (§2).
- Consumerism, "as an organization of an innovation founded on disposability, waste, carelessness and blindness, … is in contradiction with the future" (§5; Lotz cites the Bloomsbury rendering at p. 23).

## Notes for evaluation

- **Primary.** It updates the 2005 text in the vocabulary of Stiegler's 2008–2010 books: pharmacology (*Ce qui fait que la vie vaut la peine d'être vécue*, 2010), care (*Prendre soin*, 2008) and the contributory economy (*Pour une nouvelle critique de l'économie politique*, 2009). Readers should not project these 2010 concepts back onto the 2006 chapters, where **pharmakon** and **economy of contribution** are much less prominent, and in places absent. See [STRUCTURE.md](../STRUCTURE.md).
- The web draft's footnotes are incomplete. Use the printed Bloomsbury text for citations.
