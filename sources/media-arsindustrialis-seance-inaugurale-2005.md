# Ars Industrialis inaugural session (18 June 2005): minutes

**Nature of source.** These are written minutes (*compte rendu*) of the founding public meeting. They are not a transcript: Stiegler's words are paraphrased by the note-taker.

## Condensed account

The heading names the five founders: "Georges" [sic] Collins, Marc Crépon, Bernard Stiegler, Caroline Stiegler and Catherine Perret.

- **Why the association exists.** Stiegler gives three reasons. First, the founders were already preparing a joint seminar at the Collège international de philosophie, "Trouver de nouvelles armes. Pour une polémologie de l'esprit" (from October 2005), and felt that theory had to be joined to political work. Second, they share a diagnosis of industrial societies. Third, the association aims to widen that sharing to other "singularities". The urgency comes from a first deadline: the Tunis summit on "information societies" that autumn, where Ars Industrialis wants to be a "force de proposition".
- **The manifesto.** It is presented as **provisional**, a conceptual base and "platform for discussion" rather than a doctrine. Its concepts are "weapons" for thinking a new epoch of capitalism defined by generalised digitisation. The association wants to be a place of "translation" between disciplines and languages, and a process of psychic and collective individuation and transindividuation, that is, a new "discipline".
- **The diagnosis.** Capitalism is in a crisis that is **moral and psychic** and concerns the **libido**. To understand it, one has to understand the libidinal organisation of society under generalised digitisation and the techniques that manipulate desire. Having lost its symbolic efficacy, capitalism now targets the **drive (pulsion) rather than the libido**. It substitutes a "drive-based economy" (économie pulsionnelle) for a libidinal economy. This is a dangerous passage to the limit and produces new pathologies, felt as an explosive suffering and malaise. The idea anticipates ch. 1's "capitalism of drives". The resulting suffering is described as **"désublimation généralisée"**. Every society is a process of sublimation, offering something beyond the calculable that makes life worth living. That possibility is now compromised, which raises the question of a new "belief" affirming that there is a "high and low".
- **Questions from the floor** dealt with desire vs drive and the status of the manifesto. Crépon spoke on the association as a space of sharing.
- **Second part.** Knowledge, information and media societies were announced as the topic of the Tunis summit and of a future meeting. The speakers noted, "documents à l'appui", the "langue de bois" of knowledge management discourse. The floor asked about free software, which is not in the manifesto. The answer: the association has no fixed view yet but may open a working group.
- **Third part.** Philippe Aigrain presented the website as the association's "organe moteur", built around members' personal pages. Catherine Perret set out practical rules. Stiegler mentioned contacts in England, Italy, Spain, Belgium, Japan, the US and Taiwan.

## Short quotations

- The manifesto's concepts "sont en même temps des armes pour penser notre temps et tenter d'agir sur lui".
- On the crisis: its "caractère moral et psychique qui a à voir avec la libido".
- "A une économie libidinale, il substitue une économie pulsionnelle."
- Suffering understood as the effects of a "désublimation généralisée".

## Notes for evaluation

- Valuable for dating. The core vocabulary of ch. 1 (libidinal economy, desire vs drives, desublimation, belief) and the knowledge-society theme of ch. 2 were already in place on the founding day.
- It confirms the **five founders**: four philosophers and a jurist, according to the site's "qui sommes-nous" page (see [context-ars-industrialis-association.md](context-ars-industrialis-association.md)). This contradicts accounts listing only three co-authors or co-founders. The spelling "Georges" Collins varies across sources; the motion and most sources spell it "George".
- The minutes are unsigned. Paraphrase is not Stiegler's own wording.
