# Cohen, "Dare to Care" (2017)

I read the author-posted PDF of the published version, mainly the opening sections (pp. 149–153) and the conclusion (pp. 164–166), and skimmed the middle on Foucault's *epimeleia*.

## Condensed account
- **Spirit / Valéry (pp. 150–152).** Cohen reads the phrase "the life of the spirit" as a sign of Stiegler's admiration for Valéry, especially "La liberté de l'esprit" (1939), "to which Stiegler regularly returns". In Valéry, spirit is a "bizarrerie" that estranges humans from the "initial and normal conditions of life". Cohen recasts this in Simondonian and Leroi-Gourhanian terms: the life of the spirit emerges transductively when technical exteriorisation opens the possibility of a psychic *and* collective individuation. He cites the **Ars Industrialis glossary entry "Esprit"** (as "Stiegler 2014b") for the claim that the "and" designates spirit. He adds Simondon's definition of "spirituality" as respect for the relation between the individual and the preindividual (*L'individuation psychique et collective*, pp. 105–6).
- **Subsistence, existence, consistence (pp. 151–152, nn. 3–4).** Desire infinitises its object and so opens the plane of consistence. Stiegler's tropes of raising and lowering map onto movement between subsistence, existence and consistence. This is useful for "valeur esprit" as rising or falling.
- **Care / epimeleia (pp. 158–162).** Stiegler's *Taking Care* and Foucault's late lectures both turn on *epimeleia*, care of self. Stiegler's notion of the philosophical pharmakon is a practice in which repetitions "take care of me" (the *soin* motif).
- **Critique (p. 165).** Stiegler's "general organology" rarely attends to lived experience. It privileges the technological and "relegates vitality to subsistence". His reading of Foucault is "unnecessarily limited", missing the late "aesthetics of existence". Cohen proposes the motto "Curare Aude, Dare to Care".

## Notes for evaluation
- **Stance:** Foucauldian, biopolitical and medical humanities. Sympathetic but corrective.
- **Value:** the best secondary gloss found on the *Valéry genealogy of "esprit"* and the subsistence/existence/consistence scheme, both of which are central to the subtitle "la valeur esprit". It also gives a good account of how *care* (soin, epimeleia) links Stiegler and Foucault.
- **Limits:** it focuses on the later "mystagogy" lectures (2014) and does not discuss industrial populism or *Réenchanter*.
- **Reliability:** good, as a peer-reviewed article in a major journal with careful citations.
