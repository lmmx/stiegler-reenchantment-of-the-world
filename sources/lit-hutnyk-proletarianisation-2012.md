# Hutnyk, "Proletarianisation" (2012)

I read the full text of the author's eprint PDF. Page numbers below follow the *New Formations* pagination printed in the eprint.

## Condensed account
- **Proletarianization (pp. 127–129).** Hutnyk opens with Stiegler's "Within the limits of capitalism, economizing means taking care" (Ars Industrialis, n.d.). He calls Stiegler's Marxism "unorthodox" and notes that Stiegler calls proletarianisation Marx's greatest contribution. He quotes the **Ars Industrialis Manifesto 2010** definition at length (p. 128): knowledge, once formalised in a technique, machine or apparatus, "escapes" the individual. Workers lose savoir-faire. In the 20th century consumers lose savoir-vivre to devices such as the television set, which creates "available brain time" (an allusion to Le Lay). Intellectual workers of "cognitive capitalism" lose the "life of the spirit" as critical, self-formalising authority. Hutnyk observes that Stiegler's notion is both narrower than Marx and Engels's (focused on knowledge loss) and more generalised (nearly the whole middle class).
- **Television, attention and care (pp. 129–137).** He reconstructs the "battle for intelligence" in *Taking Care of Youth and the Generations*: the audiovisual industries destroy the intergenerational transmission of attention, and the result is "care-less-ness". He calls this rendering "alarmist and patronising" (p. 130), resting on thin statistics, "Euro-" centred, and close to a familiar conservative complaint about declining standards and youth.
- **General intellect (pp. 136–137).** He contrasts Stiegler's account of retentional devices with Marx's General Intellect, which is both deskilling and the basis of the "composite worker".
- **Critique (pp. 145–148).** In focusing on loss of skill and knowledge, Stiegler and Ars Industrialis underplay the two further moments of Marx's concept: immiseration through sinking into wage labour (now brain labour), and the class mobilisation that follows. Stiegler has "very little to say about class or social conflict" (p. 147) and ignores reproductive labour. He condemns "delinquents" rather than seeing stupidity itself as possibly pharmacological. Transindividuation can also be carried by "ruinous" media and may be proto-revolutionary. His model of time (long vs short circuits) is too undifferentiated.

## Short quotations
- Ars Industrialis Manifesto 2010, quoted p. 128: proletarianization is "the process through which an individual or collective knowledge, being formalized through a technique, a machine, or an apparatus, can escape the individual".
- Hutnyk, p. 130: "this rendering of the problem is alarmist and patronising."
- Hutnyk, p. 147: Stiegler "has very little to say about class or social conflict".

## Notes for evaluation
- **Stance:** Marxist and autonomist (Plan C), sympathetic but sharply critical, much like Manche (RP 2021) but earlier and more polemical.
- **Accuracy:** quotations are carefully sourced. The critique of Stiegler's empirical claims about children and television is fair. Reading Stiegler as a conservative "decline" theorist arguably neglects his pharmacological insistence on positive potentials and on technics as originary.
- **Relevance to the book:** it does not discuss *Réenchanter*, but it quotes the 2010 Ars Industrialis manifesto, which is reprinted in the English edition, and so serves as a critical gloss on that manifesto's proletarianisation paragraph and its "available brain time" motif.
- **Reliability:** good on sources. The interpretation is strongly partisan.
