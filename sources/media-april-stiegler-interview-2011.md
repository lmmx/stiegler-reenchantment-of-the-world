# Stiegler interviewed for April (2011)

**Nature of source.** A 12 min 19 s video. I read the **transcript** published by April, not the video.

## Condensed account

Stiegler presents the Ars Industrialis analysis five years after the book:
- 20th-century **consumerism** was a response to the contradictions of productivist capitalism that Marx analysed. Workers lost their *savoir-faire*. Consumers now lose their *savoir-vivre*.
- Generalised **proletarianisation**: people operate systems without understanding them. Examples: Alan Greenspan's testimony after 2008, engineers ignorant of their discipline's history, Fukushima.
- **Free software** and hackers are a "deproletarianising" industrial model that has effectively won.
- Simondonian individuation and co-individuation through shared knowledge.
- Employees work from fear and from reward or punishment, not motivation. Traders such as Kerviel act from addiction.
- Ars Industrialis proposes **contributive economies** as a therapeutics of the digital **pharmakon**, which could otherwise serve a police state (British surveillance; states' worries about the internet after Tunisia).
- Participation is distinguished from contribution.

## Short quotations

- On free software: "c'est un modèle industriel déprolétarisant".
- "ce sont les consommateurs qui perdent leur savoir-vivre".
- "Ils ne connaissent plus de choses, ils savent faire des choses."

## Notes for evaluation

- It does not discuss the book, the Medef or "valeur esprit". It is useful as Stiegler's own **plain-language statement of ch. 1's consumer and proletarianisation thesis** (loss of savoir-vivre). It also shows the later shift to "contribution" and free software, which the 2005 founding minutes had left open ("pas de point de vue arrêté sur le libre"). The "Tunisia" mention here refers to the 2011 revolution, not the 2005 summit.
- It is self-presentation for a sympathetic audience. The transcript is reliable as a record.
