# Le Lay: "Ce que nous vendons à Coca-Cola, c'est du temps de cerveau humain disponible"

## Established facts

- **Speaker.** Patrick Le Lay, then chairman and CEO (PDG) of the TF1 television channel. He died in March 2020.
- **Publication.** The remark appeared in an interview printed in ***Les Dirigeants face au changement*** (Paris: Éditions du Huitième Jour, 2004). This volume is a "baromètre 2004" by the partners of the consultancy **EIM (Executive Interim Management)**. It mostly contains interviews with French business leaders (the catalogue record says 21) and has a **preface by Ernest-Antoine Seillière**, then Medef president. French Wikipedia also gives the title as *Les Dirigeants français et le changement* (June 2004). The page is inconsistent on this point, and the form used by Acrimed, Stiegler and the Swiss teaching sheet is *Les Dirigeants face au changement*.
- **How it became public.** Acrimed reports an **AFP dispatch of 9 July 2004**, followed by a *Libération* item (10–11 July 2004) headlined "Patrick Le Lay, décerveleur". French Wikipedia cites *L'Expansion/L'Express* of 9 July 2004 as the earliest dated coverage, and *Le Nouvel Observateur*. In a September 2004 *Télérama* interview, reported by *Le Monde* on 8 Sept 2004, Le Lay said the Medef had invited him to a discussion about change, that it was not an official interview, and that the phrase was "un peu caricaturale et étroite".
- **Exact wording.** The passage was checked against Acrimed's reproduction. Key sentences:
  - "à la base, le métier de TF1, c'est d'aider Coca-Cola, par exemple, à vendre son produit"
  - "Or pour qu'un message publicitaire soit perçu, il faut que le cerveau du téléspectateur soit disponible."
  - "Nos émissions ont pour vocation de le rendre disponible"
  - "Ce que nous vendons à Coca-Cola, c'est du temps de cerveau humain disponible."
  - "Rien n'est plus difficile que d'obtenir cette disponibilité."
  French Wikipedia also gives his remarks that audience share "se joue chaque jour" and that television is "une activité sans mémoire". Stiegler's 2005 talk, the precursor of ch. 2, quotes further lines from the same interview on reactivity (*Star Academy*). I have not checked whether the printed book keeps all of them.

## Stiegler's use

In the 24 Sept 2005 Ars Industrialis talk (see [primary-parallel-stiegler-colline-2005.md](primary-parallel-stiegler-colline-2005.md)), Stiegler quotes Le Lay at length. He calls this business model **"industrial populism"** and says selling "available brain time" is the very opposite of a knowledge society. He also cites Le Lay on knowing audiences "to the second" and on *Star Academy* as "réactivité maîtrisée", and he links this to Bradbury and Truffaut's *Fahrenheit 451*. The Tunis motion (14 Nov 2005) defines "esprit" as both the "temps de cerveau disponible" that TV channels want and the source of "œuvres de l'esprit". French Wikipedia also says Stiegler called the remarks "effroyables de cynisme et de vulgarité" in *Le Monde diplomatique* (June 2005, "De la misère symbolique 2. La 'catastrophe' du sensible"). **I did not open that article.** Stiegler also appears in the 2010 France 3 documentary *Le Temps de cerveau disponible* (Jean-Robert Viallet and Christophe Nick; see [media-plandetudes-fiche-temps-de-cerveau-2010.md](media-plandetudes-fiche-temps-de-cerveau-2010.md)).

## Notes for evaluation

- The **date is often given loosely as "2004"**, and the publication varies between "a book" and "an interview". The precise form is an interview in an EIM book, publicised by AFP on 9 July 2004. Some English sources misdescribe it as a TV statement or a speech.
- The Medef link is real but is often missed: the volume was prefaced by Seillière, and Le Lay said the Medef asked him to take part. This is why Stiegler can join "re-enchantment" (Medef 2005) and "available brain time" (Medef-prefaced book, 2004) into one critique.
- Wikipedia's sourcing for the book title is internally inconsistent, as noted above.
