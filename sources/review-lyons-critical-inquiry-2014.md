# Lyons, Critical Inquiry (2014)

A short review in *Critical Inquiry*, online first and then in print (CI 41.4, 2015, pp. 900–902).

## Condensed account

1. **Thesis.** In the Western capitalist world, the "value of spirit" (mind, intelligence, attention, care for oneself and others) is shrinking. Stiegler blames television, social media and "communicational technologies" for an "industrial populism".
2. **Status of the book.** It is the first collective publication of Ars Industrialis, the group founded in 2005 by Stiegler, his partner Caroline and colleagues.
3. **Structure.** As Lyons describes it: two manifestos (the 2005 original and its 2010 revision), then two long diagnostic essays by Stiegler on hyperindustrial neoliberalism, the loss of *savoir-vivre* and a "reign of ignorance", then a transcribed group motion from the Tunis Summit on the crisis of information societies. He gives no page numbers apart from the total of 136 pp.
4. **Lineage.** The diagnosis starts from Paul Valéry's pre-war reflections. It echoes Weber's "disenchantment", but argues that neo-capitalist consumption now produces a new and "poisonous" enchantment.
5. **Libidinal economy.** Desire has given way to instant gratification, and productive sublimation has given way to manipulated libidinal economies.
6. **Pharmacology.** Following Plato, Stiegler treats technology as a *pharmakon*, both poison and remedy. Harmful technologies can therefore be reclaimed, for example by turning social media toward real community. This is why Lyons calls the book hopeful rather than paranoid.
7. **Other sources.** Simondon, Freud and Marx are drawn on, often without explanation.
8. **Translator's preface.** Lyons calls it a useful key. It explains that "esprit" covers personal and collective individuation, memory, and reflective and critical faculties.
9. **Verdict.** The range of topics overwhelms the reader. The prose drifts by association and relies on "slippery" outward reference and philosophical shorthand. Lyons doubts it suits newcomers. The essays "barely graze the surface", and the manifestos start discussion rather than supply solutions. Even so, the collected texts are "invaluable" as critical stimulation.

## Short quotations
- "The value of spirit in the Western capitalist world is dwindling, fading, nearly lost."
- "Not content to mourn and wallow, Stiegler's is a philosophy of hope."
- "these texts are collectively invaluable as critical stimulation, wary yet optimistic"

## Notes for evaluation
- **Accuracy.** The structural description matches the verified EN contents: preface, introduction, two manifestos, two chapters, Tunis motion. Lyons does not mention the Introduction ("What Is to Be Done?") separately. The translator's first name is spelled "Trever" in one place.
- **Depth.** This is a short notice-length review, thematic rather than section by section. It gives no page references, so it is of little use for section summaries apart from the general themes (Valéry, Weber, pharmakon, libidinal economy).
- **Stance.** Sympathetic and literary/media-theoretical. The pharmakon reading is foregrounded. Lyons offers no political-economic critique, which contrasts with Lotz (Marx & Philosophy), who finds the book abstract and reformist. Both reviewers agree that it lacks concrete proposals.
- **Reliability.** Accurate but general. Best used for the book's reception tone and for the role of the translator's preface.
