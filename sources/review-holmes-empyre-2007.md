# Holmes, -empyre- list (2007)

This is a mailing-list post, not a review, and only one paragraph of it concerns the book. Read in full via fetch. It is an early English-language mention of the 2006 French edition by the art and activist critic Brian Holmes.

## Condensed account
1. Holmes names *Réenchanter le monde* and notes that it opens with a chapter called "What is to be done?", which matches EN Introduction p. 1.
2. He summarises Stiegler as arguing the following. "Writing machines", in a broad sense, should be redirected to protect Europeans, and perhaps others, from a threatened loss of singularity and so of any possible ethics. In advanced economies capitalism is now chiefly cultural, organised around devices that externalise memory and creativity into objects and traces. These machines are constitutive of human experience over time, but their production needs care so that individuals can go on individuating in creative tension with societies that are also individuating. Without such change, disenchantment with democracy may grow and desire may collapse into destructive violence.
3. Holmes uses this to put a challenge back to another list member: if that member's post is right, Stiegler's approach may already be obsolete, so what does the member propose?

## Notes for evaluation
- A good, compact paraphrase of the Introduction's and Ch.1's argument (grammatization as "writing machines", the cultural or hyperindustrial capitalism of EN Ch.1, desire turning to violence, "becoming-barbaric" in EN Ch.1 p. 39). It flags the "European" framing, cf. EN Ch.1 "a European way of life", p. 53.
- Stance: autonomist and activist. He is mildly sceptical about Stiegler's Europe-centred and care-based reformism, but the post is not a critique.
- Low weight. No page references.
