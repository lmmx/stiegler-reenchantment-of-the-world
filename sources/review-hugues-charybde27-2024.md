# Hugues, Charybde 27 (2024)

A bookseller's blog reading note. Read in full via fetch. It quotes several long passages from the book without page numbers. I have not reproduced those passages here.

## Condensed account
1. The book is a "philosophical and political manifesto" against the hold of the capitalist imaginary on real and digital life. Valéry's 1939 "baisse de la valeur esprit" is extended to reality TV, the selling of audiences, and consumers treated as conditioned nervous systems.
2. Biographical context: Stiegler's late turn to philosophy, his prison years, Derrida's support, Ars Industrialis, the IRI, and the Centre Pompidou.
3. **Occasion of the book:** the Medef's 2005 summer university, which used the slogan "Réenchanter le monde". The book takes the slogan at its word and asks whether employers want a revival of spirit or "knowledge without spirit".
4. The diagnosis is set within Weber's disenchantment thesis.
5. The opening section quotes employer and media figures: Ernest-Antoine Seillère, Denis Kessler, and Patrick Le Lay's "temps de cerveau disponible". It also has the Medef's "knowledge economy" talk and Benjamin and Freud material. Hugues locates all of this in the introductory part of the book, which corresponds to EN Introduction "What Is to Be Done?", p. 1.
6. On ecological and social crisis, the book cites Laurence Tubiana ("Toubiana" in the note) and Robert Lyon. It calls for "qualitative growth" instead of endless consumption and for a new framework to stop the fall of the value of spirit.
7. A closing passage built on the anaphora "Nous savons…" lists what "we know" about the need to raise consciousness and intelligence. Hugues locates it at the end of the book's opening part, but he gives no page.
8. Verdict: a "rare and invigorating" reading of contemporary imaginaries. Its concepts are tools ("munitions") against capitalist manipulation, and it was early in seeing the climate emergency.

## Short quotations

- "une lecture rare et tonique de ce qui est à l'œuvre dans nos imaginaires"

## Notes for evaluation
- Valuable for the **Introduction's** content: the Medef/Seillère/Kessler/Le Lay framing, the Valéry opening, Tubiana and Lyon, and the "Nous savons" passage. It agrees with de Beer, who also cites Robert Lyon. There are no page numbers, and where in the book the quoted passages fall is inferred.
- Stance: enthusiastic, left-ecological and bookseller-activist. Uncritical.
- Reliability: accurate paraphrase as far as can be checked. Read 18 years after publication, it projects later climate concerns onto the book, though Tubiana is in fact cited.
