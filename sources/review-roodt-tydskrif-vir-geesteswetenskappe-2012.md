# Roodt, Tydskrif vir Geesteswetenskappe (2012)

This is a critical article on the **Ars Industrialis manifestos**, which appear in the book as the "Ars Industrialis 2005 Manifesto" (EN p. 11) and the "2010 Manifesto" (EN p. 17). It does **not** cite *Réenchanter le monde* itself: it uses the web versions of the manifestos (2008 English translation and 2010). It is included because it is the most sustained critique found of the manifestos' key concept. I read the full open-access PDF.

## Condensed account
1. **The claim she examines.** The founding manifesto commits members to an "industrial politics of spirit". Its opening makes clear that "spirit" refers to Hannah Arendt's "life of the mind", which it says is under worldwide threat. Stiegler also calls his own project "a struggle for the life of the spirit" (citing *Mécréance et discrédit* 3, p. 120).
2. **Her reconstruction of Stiegler and the manifestos.** Digital media deliver spirit over to global capitalism by fabricating desire, or "libidinal energy", and directing it toward consumer goods. Because singularisation happens through libidinal energy, fabricated desire yields false singularities and narrows the possibilities of existence. Technology is also the means of liberation: new techniques for making objects of desire outside the market would free libido and open new forms of individual and social life, which is the new politics of spirit.
3. **Arendt's distinctions.** Arendt separates mind from psyche or soul: the passions are undergone and belong to necessity, whereas thinking, willing and judging are self-reflexive and free. She also separates the free activity of mind from political freedom, which arises only in collective action.
4. **Three disagreements.** (a) Stiegler equates mind with libido, collapsing Arendt's mind/psyche distinction. (b) He treats the freedom of mind, or its absence, as a function of economic forces. (c) He assumes a direct path from liberated mind to new forms of collective existence and political action, whereas for Arendt action is never the necessary outcome of mental operations.
5. **Style of thought.** Stiegler is a *synthetic* thinker who links technology, politics, economy, culture, psyche and spirit, and therefore calls for resistance on every front. Arendt is a thinker of *distinctions*, and her hope lies in the ever-present human capacity to begin something new together, not in schemes to redeem spirit.
6. **Verdict.** Stiegler's analysis of technics, his critique of consumption and his call for care for the world should not be dismissed. But the manifesto's appeal to Arendt does not hold, and the Ars Industrialis project cannot be derived from Arendt's view of mind or of action.

## Notes for evaluation
- **Coverage.** Manifestos only (2005 and 2010), via their web versions. Useful for the section on the 2005 Manifesto's Arendt reference and on the 2010 Manifesto's libido and pharmacology vocabulary.
- **Stance.** Arendtian and political-theoretical, and analytically careful. She concedes common ground (critique of consumer society, care for the world).
- **Possible oversimplification.** "Stiegler equates spirit with libido" compresses his more layered account (libido as the energy of sublimation, spirit as the noetic and transindividual). Supporters would say she does not engage his Freud and Simondon apparatus. She is explicit, though, that she judges only the manifesto's presentation.
- **Disagreement.** Directly contrary to de Beer in the same issue, who embraces the spirit vocabulary.
