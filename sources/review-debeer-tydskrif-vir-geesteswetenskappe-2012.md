# de Beer, Tydskrif vir Geesteswetenskappe (2012)

This is a review-article, not a short book review. It takes *Réenchanter le monde* (2006), rendered in Afrikaans as *Heropluistering van die wêreld: geestelike waarde teenoor industriële populisme*, as its "guiding publication". It reads the book together with the two Ars Industrialis manifestos and Stiegler's other books of 2004–2010. I read the full open-access PDF.

## Condensed account
1. **Starting point.** The core message is to recover spirit, spirituality and the value of spirit. Disenchantment amounts to a *sociopathology* that calls for a *sociotherapy*, and that therapy depends on the activity of spirit.
2. **What "spirit" means.** It does not mean religious spirit. It is the defining human trait, a translation of Greek *nous*, meaning the living body as minded, knowing and relational. De Beer stresses that Stiegler leans on Valéry ("La crise de l'esprit", "La liberté de l'esprit") and places the book in the French *philosophie de l'esprit* tradition (Blondel, Lavelle, Le Senne, Bergson), alongside Malabou's materialist Hegel.
3. **Technologies of spirit (book pp. 27–28, within the Manifesto).** Culture industries, programme industries, media, telecommunications and cognitive technologies are converging into "technologies of spirit". These require an "industrial ecology of spirit" that takes in new social practices as well as calculation, and could found a "new epoch of civilisation" (p. 28).
4. **Industrial populism (book p. 9).** The authors point to factors that speed up humanity's decline. De Beer groups these as industrial populism: hyperindustrialism, hyperconsumerism, the dismissal of knowledge as a waste of time, the "whatever / so-what" attitude, Lacan's "will to ignorance", manipulation in place of noetic commitment, and "hyper-technicism".
5. **Two chapters.** The book gives two chapters to (a) the thorough *re-foundation of society* and (b) the *struggle against the reign of ignorance*. This matches EN Ch.1 and Ch.2.
6. **Knowledge without spirit? (book pp. 13–14).** Is knowledge produced through industrialised savoir-vivre, savoir-faire and savoir-penser, and through cognitive technologies, really individuation of the contemporary episteme, given "cognitive saturation" and an "entropy" of hypotheses, axioms and methods? Can knowledge without spirit be knowledge? This page range is probably in the Introduction. The cognitive-saturation theme is developed in EN Ch.2 at p. 83.
7. **Being vs having.** The book quotes Robert Lyon (*Le Monde*, 7 July 2006) on the "century of less": humanity can come out of this only by siding with *being* rather than *having*. De Beer gives no page.
8. **Pharmakon (book p. 82).** Cited together with Manifesto 2010 point 4 for the claim that digital technics is a pharmakon and that "nootechnics" or technologies of spirit can be turned toward cure.
9. **Manifestos.** He quotes Manifesto 2005 point 7 (English 2008 web version): an industrial politics of spirit needs a new form of public power that brings together economic actors, institutions, researchers, artists, philosophers and investors. He quotes Manifesto 2010 point 6 for the definition of proletarianisation as loss of formalised knowledge. His bibliography places the "Manifeste d'Ars Industrialis" at **pp. 27–35 of the French edition**, and says the same journal issue carries an Afrikaans translation of the 2005 manifesto.
10. **Conclusion.** A "polemology" (struggle) for spirit leads to a sociotherapy or "nootherapy": re-founding a true "we", fighting ignorance as a fight for intelligence, reinventing care and love, re-enchanting the world, and recovering "nearness of being".

## Short quotations

## Notes for evaluation
- **Usefulness.** This is the only source found that gives **page references to the French edition**: pp. 9, 13–14, 27–28, 82, and the Manifesto at pp. 27–35. French pagination does not match EN. FR Manifesto pp. 27–35 corresponds to EN pp. 11–16.
- **Caution.** Many of de Beer's other page references (2006a, 2006b, 2006c) are to *Mécréance et discrédit* 2 and 3 and *La télécratie contre la démocratie*, not this book. A bare "(p. 93)" quotation about consumers "dispossessed of all knowledge" is ambiguous and should not be attributed to *Réenchanter*.
- **Stance.** Strongly sympathetic and somewhat homiletic. It reads Stiegler through a quasi-spiritualist and Heideggerian lens (being, nearness of being, Fromm's *To Have or To Be*) and adds Ubuntu-adjacent communal language. It plays down the political-economy and libidinal-economy material and does not treat the Tunis motion.
- **Contrast.** Roodt's article in the same issue (see review-roodt-…) disputes the Arendtian lineage of "spirit" that the Manifesto claims, whereas de Beer accepts the spirit vocabulary uncritically.
- **Reliability.** Good for structure and for FR page anchors. Its interpretation adds a good deal of de Beer's own material.
