# "Souffrance et consommation" (25 Feb 2006)

**Nature of source.** The session page: argument, list of speakers and documents. The talks were posted as **WMA audio downloads**, with no transcript.

## Condensed account

The argument: if libido is capitalism's fundamental energy, capitalism is an epoch of libidinal economy, and 20th-century consumption is its typical organisation, then consumption now tends to become **addictive** and so generates psychic and social suffering. The session examined both the addictive dimension inherent in desire and the conditions in which it becomes destructive of desire, with a view to a new organisation of desire. It referred to the trial of Emmanuel and Patricia Cartier, accused of infanticide, framing them and their children as victims of extreme addictive consumption. That is the argument's hypothesis, not established fact. Speakers: Stiegler (president), **Marc Valleur** (psychiatrist, head of the addiction service at the Marmottan centre) and **Jean-Pierre Klein** (psychiatrist, art therapy). Preparatory reading: an extract "L'individu désaffecté" from Stiegler's *Mécréance et discrédit* t. 2, which Galilée was due to publish in March 2006. The working group included George Collins, Caroline Stiegler (listed as "juriste"), Danielle Rapoport and others.

## Short quotations

- "la consommation tend à devenir essentiellement addictive et comme telle à engendrer souffrances et désordres psychiques et sociaux."

## Notes for evaluation

This shows that the "consumer" and "capitalism of drives" themes of ch. 1 were being worked out in public with clinicians eight months before the book (October 2006). It also gives independent confirmation that Caroline Stiegler was a jurist.
