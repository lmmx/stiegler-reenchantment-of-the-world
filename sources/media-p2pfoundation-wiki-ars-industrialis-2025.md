# P2P Foundation wiki: "Ars Industrialis"

## Condensed account

The page defines Ars Industrialis as a "cultural and political international association" for a new "industrial politics of the spirit", inspired by Stiegler. Its "Definition" section is labelled **"Via Deepseek"** and its bibliography **"Suggested by DeepSeek"**. Claims made:

- Founded **18 June 2005** on Stiegler's initiative by a group of "philosophers and jurists".
- Stiegler is described as "then director of the Department of Cultural Development at the Centre Georges Pompidou and former director of IRCAM".
- The founding manifesto draws on Arendt's "life of the mind", glossed in brackets as "(vita activa)".
- Technologies of spirit capture libidinal energy for consumption. They are explained through Foucault's biopower and Stiegler's "psychopower".
- Pharmakon; proletarianisation as loss of savoir-faire and savoir-vivre; disindividuation; economy of contribution, with **Wikipedia** as the model of contributor-consumers; care; nootechnologies and hypomnemata.
- A second manifesto appeared in 2010.

Bibliography: Mingant (InMedia 2012), Roodt (2012, Arendt/Stiegler), Abbinnett (2017), Bradley (boundary 2, 2015), Duchesne Winter (2019, Sumak Kawsay), and Stiegler (2014). I did not check these here.

## Notes for evaluation

- **AI-generated content, openly flagged.** No inline sources for individual claims.
- **Anachronism or error:** in June 2005 Stiegler was still **director of IRCAM** (2002 until 1 Jan 2006, per French Wikipedia). He became director of cultural development at the Centre Pompidou on **1 January 2006**. So "then director of the Department of Cultural Development … former director of IRCAM" is wrong for June 2005.
- **Conceptual slip:** "life of the mind (vita activa)". Arendt's *The Life of the Mind* and her *vita activa* (in *The Human Condition*) are different, even contrasting, notions. The manifesto's "vie de l'esprit" alludes to the former.
- "18 June 2005" matches the inaugural session date on arsindustrialis.org. That date is the first public meeting, which may differ from the legal registration date.
- The conceptual summary (psychopower, pharmakon, contribution) is broadly accurate for Ars Industrialis *as a whole* (2005–2015). But some of it, especially "nootechnologies" and the developed "economy of contribution", belongs mainly to the association's later work (2008 onwards). It should be checked against the 2006 book before being attributed to it. The Wikipedia example does appear in ch. 2 (cf. English edition p. 87, per the Lotz-based note in the Colline file).
