# Rossouw, "Bernard Stiegler's aestheology and the new otium of the people" (2015)

An article in LitNet Akademies, the English version of an Afrikaans text.

## Condensed account
- **Thesis.** Stiegler's political ideal, a "new *otium* of the people", is read as "the pursuit of Christianity through means other than Christian". Art and the aesthetic stand in for religion, a move Rossouw compares to Schiller's turn to art after the French Revolution. For Stiegler, the central crisis of Western modernity is "of a theological nature": a loss of faith in the social order and its future.
- **Otium / negotium.** Negotium is business and subsistence; otium is education, art, intellectual work and contemplation. The industrialisation of leisure lets negotium dominate otium. Rossouw sketches a history of otium: Cicero's otium for the common good, Augustine's prayer, and medieval clerical and aristocratic privilege. Stiegler's new otium is cultivated through disciplines and practices, forms attention, creates community, commemorates tradition, offers shared time, and is open to all.
- **Subsistence, existence, consistence.** In this triad, the "consistent that does not exist" plays God's role in Christianity.
- **Proletariat / hyper-industrial.** The proletariat extends "from producers in the industrial society to consumers in the hyper-industrial society". Disindividuation and loss of participation lead to symbolic misery. The keywords include "re-enchantment" and "new proletariat".
- **Critique.** Rossouw names two questionable moves: "Christianity is used to authorise art", and "the aesthetical is interpreted as the ground of politics".

## Notes for evaluation
- **Stance:** a theological-political critique by a South African philosopher who knows the French corpus well (Stiegler's student milieu). It offers a distinctive reading of *re-enchantment* as a secularised Christian project, which is relevant to the book's Weberian and Valéryan register of "spirit" and "re-enchantment".
