# Cherrier, lelitteraire.com (2012)

A short French web review of the 2006 Flammarion edition (172 pp., €12).

## Condensed account
1. Opens with Valéry's 1939 remark, made as Nazism advanced, on a "baisse de la valeur esprit".
2. The book's question is what place spirit and reflection have in industrial capitalism and in a consumer society saturated by communication technologies.
3. Innovation serves mass communication, which raises the question of whether spirit serves technology or technology serves spirit. The reviewer links this to loss of individuation and gives reality TV (*Star Academy*) as an illustration.
4. Nanotechnologies, RFID, the web, ADSL and Wi-Fi produce constant information. The reviewer paraphrases the book's claim that the result is a paradoxically "disinformed" society bathed in ignorance, which echoes Ch.2's information-versus-knowledge theme.
5. The book examines spirit's role in the economy and in a "European way of life" (cf. EN Ch.1 §"a European way of life", p. 53). Through Ars Industrialis it invites reflection with Arendt's "life of the mind" in view.
6. Its vocabulary (*hypomnémata*, *épokhal*, *pharmaka*) is an obstacle.
7. Verdict: relevant but hard going. Suited to philosophy students and specialists, and general readers should learn the terminology first.

## Short quotations
- "Un essai pertinent mais difficile d'accès"
- "une société finalement désinformée, baignant dans l'ignorance"

## Notes for evaluation
- Accurate but brief and impressionistic. There are no page references and no structural description. The *Star Ac* example may be the reviewer's own rather than the book's.
- Stance: a neutral general reader. Its complaint about difficulty agrees with Lyons, the SensCritique reader and Lotz.
- Low weight. Useful only as a sign of French lay reception.
