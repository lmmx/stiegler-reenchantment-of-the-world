# Ross, Introduction to Stiegler's *The Neganthropocene* (2018)

Ross's Introduction to the open-access volume, with a note of how Stiegler's essays in it cite *The Re-Enchantment of the World*. Page numbers follow the printed book.

## Condensed account (Ross, Introduction)
- **Phases of the work (pp. 7–24).** Ross divides Stiegler's work into three "conversions of the gaze":
  1. a *technological* phase: the existential, carceral origin and the *Technics and Time* project of exteriorisation and the default of origin;
  2. an *organological and pharmacological* phase, prompted by 11 September 2001 and the rise of the National Front, in which Stiegler decides to write in direct relation to "questions of political economy";
  3. a *neganthropological* phase (entropy, the Anthropocene, platform capitalism).
  *Réenchanter le monde* (2006) belongs to the second phase. Ross notes that Anglophone philosophy rarely reads beyond *Technics and Time* 1–3, whereas media-studies readers take up the "direct, visible, legible" second-phase works without their philosophical underpinnings (p. 22).
- **General organology (pp. 19–20).** It has three strands: psychic and somatic organs, social organisations, and technical organs. Because we are neotenic and "perpetually unfinished", we must produce artificial organs, learn to practise them, and institute intergenerational social organisations that metastabilise "forms of knowledge that are these practices and these cares".
- **Grammatization (p. 20).** It is "the primary analytical concept" of the second phase. Stiegler takes it from Sylvain Auroux and extends it from the discretisation of speech into alphabetic writing to every spatialisation that makes temporal or perceptual flows discrete and reproducible: from Palaeolithic "arche-cinema", through workers' gestures in machines and radio and cinema, to binary code. It links Socrates' pharmakon of writing (*Phaedrus*) to Marx's *Grundrisse* (knowledge absorbed into fixed capital).
- **Proletarianization (pp. 20–21).** Ross calls it "perhaps his fundamental political concept": the deprivation of knowledge, first thought by Socrates. In the 19th century work-knowledge is lost, in the 20th life-knowledge (through the culture industry and marketing, which target youthful attention and intergenerational transmission), and in the 21st rational and conceptual knowledge (through computation).
- **Protention (pp. 21–22).** Marketing and cultural tertiary retentions aim to control protention (expectations, desires, beliefs), which decides behaviour where it is not reduced to drives. The libidinal economy harnessed to production depletes libidinal energy, unbinds drives and makes the productive economy insolvent. This leads from control societies to "hypercontrol" and then "uncontrollability", with *passages à l'acte* (acting out).
- **Care (p. 24).** Culture, education and knowledge are "practices of care" that metastabilise transindividuation.
- **Contemporary challenges (pp. 9–12).** Ross's list includes the financial crisis as a symptom of increasingly short-term and speculative investment, and "industrially-generated populism" leading into the "post-truth" age and the "Trumpocene". He presents these as continuations of the diagnosis of industrial populism.

## Stiegler's own citations of the book in this volume
- Note 271 (endnotes section) refers readers to *The Re-Enchantment of the World* (Bloomsbury 2014), with *Automatic Society 1*, for "the recent evolution" of Simondon's **associated technogeographic milieus**, "which Simondon never had the opportunity to analyse". Note 449 likewise lists the book among his commentaries on Simondon's associated milieu. **This is an authorial pointer that the book is a key place where Stiegler develops the associated/dissociated milieu concept.**
- In the body (p. 100; and pp. 208–9 in the chapter "What is Called Caring?"), Stiegler partly revises the 2006 hope. He says that for ten years he had stressed, via free software, that digital networks could form "networks of associated milieus". He now argues that the algorithmic web has become "a systemically dissociating milieu, because it is hyper-proletarianizing" (p. 100). The web associated milieu (since 1993) is traversed by two contrary tendencies: contributory de-proletarianisation, and extreme proletarianisation that turns contributors into functions of the system (pp. 144–146).

## Short quotations
- Ross, p. 20: grammatization is "the primary analytical concept emerging from this general organology, in this second phase of Stiegler's work".
- Ross, p. 21: proletarianization is "perhaps his fundamental political concept".
- Stiegler, p. 100: the reticulated milieu "becomes a systemically dissociating milieu, because it is hyper-proletarianizing."

## Notes for evaluation
- **Reliability:** high. Ross is Stiegler's principal translator and a close collaborator, and the reconstruction is lucid and well documented. Stance: insider and sympathetic, Derridean and Simondonian, with no external critique.
- **Value for the book:** it places *Réenchanter* within the "second conversion" (post-2001, post–Le Pen 2002). It provides authorial confirmation that the book is a reference text on the associated milieu, and it documents Stiegler's later self-correction about the internet as an associated milieu. This matters when glossing the 2005/2010 manifestos' optimism about digital networks.
