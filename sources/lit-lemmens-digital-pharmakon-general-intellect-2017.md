## Condensed account
- **Cognitive capitalism and the general intellect (sections 1–3).** Lemmens surveys the post-operaist reading of Marx's "Fragment on Machines" (Negri, Hardt, Moulier-Boutang, Lazzarato), in which the general intellect has migrated into living labour and digital networks favour the autonomy of the multitude. Against this he sets Berardi's gloomy "semiocapitalism", with libidinal exhaustion and psychopathologies of the "cognitariat", which "only seems to perceive the negative side".
- **Grammatization (section "Pharmacology of the Digital").** For Stiegler, digital network technologies are a mnemotechnological transmutation as decisive as writing or printing, a "third phase" of grammatization (Auroux).
- **General organology and libidinal economy.** There are three organ systems: psychosomatic, social and technical. Their relations are transductive and form "circuits of desire": "every organology constitutes a libidinal economy". Technical organs can bind drives (sublimation) or unbind them (desublimation).
- **Pharmakon.** Technologies compensate for the *défaut originaire* and have both autonomising and heteronomising potential. Lemmens notes the affinity with Feenberg's "technical ambivalence". Autonomy exists only in relation to technical heteronomy, adopted "with care", which requires a therapy, a "sociotherapy", in short a politics.
- **Associated vs dissociated milieu.** Digital networks can create long circuits and new social autonomy if they are collectively appropriated so that they become "an associated milieu instead of a dissociated milieu, the latter being the dominant situation today". They are better suited to this than radio and TV because they are multilinear and allow reading and writing.
- **Proletarianization.** Against Hardt and Negri, and closer to Berardi, Stiegler holds that the general intellect is being proletarianised, producing "systemic stupidity". Cognitive capitalism destroys cognition. Yet the situation is pharmacological: Free Software, P2P, Wikipedia and similar projects are first steps of **deproletarianisation**, carried by the hacker ethic (Himanen).
- **Critique (section "Class-Based Pharmacology").** Stiegler lacks "a class-based analysis". It is "unrealistic if not totally illusory" to trust governments and public institutions to promote alternatives (citing *La télécratie contre la démocratie*, 2006). Lemmens calls for a "compositionalist" pharmacology aimed at recomposing the general intellect.

## Notes for evaluation
- **Stance:** sympathetic Stieglerian combined with post-autonomist Marxism. It sits between Ross (exposition) and Manche and Hutnyk (Marxist critique): it accepts the pharmacological framework but criticises Stiegler's statism. This bears directly on the book's 2005/2010 manifestos, which appeal to public power and European industrial policy.
- **Reliability:** high on exposition and peer-reviewed. It is good for the associated-milieu, organology and libidinal-economy slugs, and it places Stiegler clearly within the cognitive-capitalism debate.
