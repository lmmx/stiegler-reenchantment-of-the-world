A short web review of the Bloomsbury 2014 translation (136 pp.). I read the full online text through a fetch-and-summarise tool, so the paraphrase below rests on that tool's rendering of the page. The review has no page references.

## Condensed account
- **Overview and structure.** Lyons calls the book a "critical taking-of-stock". It is the first English-language presentation of Ars Industrialis's collective work: two manifestos (2005, updated 2010), two long Stiegler essays, and the Tunis WSIS motion. He notes the association was founded in 2005 by Stiegler, Caroline Stiegler and colleagues.
- **Spirit.** The core diagnosis is a decline in the value of spirit, glossed as mind, intelligence, attention, care for self and others, and communication. Lyons credits the **translator's preface** (Trevor Arthur) with explaining *esprit*'s several meanings: individuation, personal and collective development, memory, and critical reflection. Valéry's pre-war reflections are named as the parallel source.
- **Industrial populism.** He reads it as the era produced by communicational and consumerist technologies (social media, television) that favour instant gratification.
- **Libidinal economy.** In his account it is manipulated so that instant gratification replaces desire and the sublimation of the drives.
- **Disenchantment / re-enchantment.** He links the title to Weber's disenchantment. Stiegler describes a later stage, a "poisonous enchantment" produced by neo-capitalist consumption.
- **Pharmakon.** He describes technology as poison and remedy, so harmful uses can be redirected toward community. He stresses that Stiegler is not technophobic.

## Short quotations
- "The value of spirit in the Western capitalist world is dwindling, fading, nearly lost." (Lyons)
- "Technology is read through a double lens, as both poison and remedy, blessing and curse" (Lyons)
- "The Re-enchantment of the World does not, of course, hold many concrete answers." (Lyons)

## Notes for evaluation
- **Scope:** one of the few English reviews devoted specifically to this book, which makes it useful for reception history and for the claim about the translator's preface on *esprit*.
- **Depth:** shallow. It does not discuss proletarianization, symbolic misery, otium/negotium, the economy of contribution or associated milieus.
- **Criticism:** Lyons finds the prose associative and full of reference and shorthand. He says newcomers will struggle with Simondon, Freud and Marx, and that the essays "barely graze the surface". This matches the book's character as an occasional, manifesto-type text.
- **Possible slips:** Lyons's "Weberian" framing of re-enchantment is plausible but asserted rather than argued. Check against the text whether Stiegler's foil is primarily Weber or Medef's 2005 summer university slogan. Lyons does not mention Medef.
- **Reliability:** moderate. It is accurate at the level of general orientation but thin.
