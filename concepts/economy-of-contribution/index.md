# Economy of contribution (*économie de la contribution*)

[Home](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/index.md>) › [Glossary](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/GLOSSARY/index.md>) › **economy of contribution**

## Definition (the book's usage)

The economy of contribution is the industrial model proposed to replace the **consumerist** model. Its traits:

1. **No separation between producers and consumers.** There are only *contributors*, as in free/open-source software, Wikipedia, smart grids and community-supported agriculture (AMAP).
2. Its value takes the form of **positive externalities**: care for self and others, and Sen's **capabilities**, much of which cannot be monetized.
3. Its milieus are **associated** (Simondon), so participants individuate themselves by individuating the milieu.
4. It aims at **de-proletarianization**: the reconstitution of *savoir-faire*, *savoir-vivre* and theoretical knowledge.

## Where it appears

- [2010 Manifesto](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/chapters/03-manifesto-2010/index.md>) §3 (named and defined), §6 (free software as de-proletarianization), §7 (generalization: smart grids, AMAP, contributory businesses), §9.3 (fiscal policy for positive externalities; work as distinct from employment). N. 9: the concept was suggested by Franck Cormerais. P. 25: contributory businesses must learn from the failure of the cooperative movements of Charles Gide and Marcel Mauss.
- **Already explicit in 2006:** [Ch. 1](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/chapters/04-ch1-re-foundation-of-society/index.md>) pp. 36–37 names "the **economy of contribution** typical of open-source software" (Linux; users as practitioners), grounded in the IP network as an associated milieu since 1992. This economy furnishes "the concepts of a new industrial model" called to replace that of dissociated milieus (p. 36). The internet as associated milieu enables an industrial model resting on the association of addressees and addressors, which produces "a new form of sociality and a new spirit of capitalism (while waiting for the replacement of the latter by another form of society)" (p. 37).
- [Ch. 2](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/chapters/05-ch2-against-the-reign-of-ignorance/index.md>) p. 87: **Wikipedia** is "an extraordinary success" showing that the participatory logic of associated milieus is "an extremely new and powerful dynamic". P. 91 and pp. 92–93: hypertextual "**societies of authors and readers**", with Web 2.0 as infrastructure anticipating "a new industrial model".
- [Motion](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/chapters/06-tunis-motion/index.md>) p. 95: knowledge as a common good, with only temporary property limits.

## How secondary sources gloss it

- **Ars Industrialis *Vocabulaire*:** three traits: no producer/consumer split; non-monetizable value as positive externality; an economy of **existences** as much as of subsistences. The contributor is neither consumer, nor taxpayer, nor gift-giver in Mauss's sense ([glossary](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/glossary-arsindustrialis-vocabulaire-2013/index.md>)).
- **Stiegler–Lemmens interview (2010):** a "new industrial model". Stiegler explicitly brackets whether it will still be capitalism. Lemmens presses him on the marginality of free software and on the neoliberal state ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/primary-parallel-lemmens-stiegler-interview-krisis-2011/index.md>)).
- **Hui &amp; Halpin (2013):** a Simondonian design programme for social networks along contributory lines ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/lit-hui-halpin-collective-individuation-2013/index.md>)).
- **Abbinnett (2015):** frees labour from "ratio" so that otium becomes a universal good ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/lit-abbinnett-politics-of-spirit-2015/index.md>)).

## Disagreements

- **Manche (2021):** the most rigorous critique. Contribution rests on a teleological, operaist reading of automation. It sidelines profit and accumulation, yields "good vs bad capitalism" reformism, and risks **self-exploitation and co-optation** by corporate partners ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/lit-manche-proletarianisation-not-capitalism-2021/index.md>)).
- **Lemmens (2017):** accepts the pharmacological frame but criticizes Stiegler's **statism** ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/lit-lemmens-digital-pharmakon-general-intellect-2017/index.md>)).
- **Lotz (2015):** the book's only example is Wikipedia (p. 87); Benkler's *Wealth of Networks* says more. Lotz overstates this: Ch. 1 also cites open-source software and Linux (p. 36), and the 2010 Manifesto cites free software, smart grids and AMAP ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/review-lotz-marx-and-philosophy-2015/index.md>)).
- **Stiegler's own revision:** by *The Neganthropocene* (2018) he judged the algorithmic web "a systemically dissociating milieu", which qualifies the 2010 optimism ([Ross](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/lit-ross-neganthropocene-introduction-2018/index.md>)).
