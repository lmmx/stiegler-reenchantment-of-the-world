# Tertiary retention (*rétention tertiaire*)

[README](../README.md) › [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) › **tertiary retention**

## Definition

Tertiary retention is memory **retained outside the living being, in artefacts**: tools, writing, recordings, databases. Husserl distinguished *primary* retention (the just-past held in the present of perception, as when a note's sound lingers in a melody) from *secondary* retention (recollection). Stiegler adds **tertiary** retention, the technical, "epiphylogenetic" memory that both stores the past and **conditions** the other two. What we can perceive and remember is overdetermined by the recording and selection technologies of an epoch. Marketing and the program industries operate by controlling tertiary retentions, and through them **protentions** (expectations and desires).

## Where it appears

The term itself does not appear in the parts we could read. The idea runs through the book under other names:

- [Ch. 2](../chapters/05-ch2-against-the-reign-of-ignorance.md) "Knowledge (*Savoir*) and information" (p. 77) and "Knowledge and memory, or reign of ignorance?" (p. 79). Memory is externalized from the human origin; living memory must project outside itself to overcome its "retentional finitude"; the **mnemotechnical → mnemotechnological** transition. "Instruments of knowledge and the criteria of selection" (p. 87): memory as retention is **selection**, and industries of memory impose selection criteria (Lotz, p. 88: memory and imagination become objects of investment).
- [2005 Manifesto](../chapters/02-manifesto-2005.md) and [2010 Manifesto](../chapters/03-manifesto-2010.md): **hypomnemata** and "mnemotechnologies".

## How secondary sources gloss it

- **Ars Industrialis *Vocabulaire* ("Attention, rétention, protention"):** tertiary retentions are hypomnesic sediments in artefacts that **overdetermine** secondary retentions, which in turn overdetermine primary ones ([glossary](../sources/glossary-arsindustrialis-vocabulaire-2013.md)).
- **Ross (2018):** marketing and cultural tertiary retentions aim to control **protention**, which leads to "hypercontrol" and then "uncontrollability" ([source](../sources/lit-ross-neganthropocene-introduction-2018.md)).
- **Stiegler, "Pharmacology of Desire" (2011):** money is itself a tertiary retention and protention ([source](../sources/primary-parallel-stiegler-pharmacology-of-desire-2011.md)).
- **Manche (2021):** a clear standard exposition; the concept is introduced in *Technics and Time* 3 ([source](../sources/lit-manche-proletarianisation-not-capitalism-2021.md)).

## Disagreements

- No disagreement over the definition was found.
- **Labelled interpretation:** Ch. 2's anti-Platonic argument (writing is memory's condition, not its death) is the clearest application in the book. Its target is Michel Serres's view that externalizing memory to machines frees us for pure knowledge. For Stiegler, there is no knowledge outside its retentional supports.
