# Symbolic misery (*misère symbolique*)

[Home](../README.md) › [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) › **symbolic misery**

## Definition (the book's usage)

Symbolic misery is the loss of individuation that results from losing **participation in the production of symbols**: meanings, works, expressions and affects. The culture industries split producers of symbols from mere consumers. The consumer stops expressing and only receives, and the circuit of symbolic exchange (expression answering expression) breaks. The term comes from Stiegler's *De la misère symbolique* 1–2 (2004–05; English *Symbolic Misery*). In this book it works mostly in the background, as the premise behind **spiritual misery** and the "drop in the value of spirit".

## Where it appears

- Implicit throughout. The [2005 Manifesto](../chapters/02-manifesto-2005.md) on the destruction of singular objects of desire and symbolic practices.
- [Ch. 1](../chapters/04-ch1-re-foundation-of-society.md) §3: malaise, self-disgust and the insipid world (the *sapere*/*sapide* pun). P. 39: "absolute spiritual misery" (the Nice case, end of 2005). P. 58: a century of aesthetics in the service of production and consumption yields "profound symbolic and spiritual misery".
- [Tunis motion](../chapters/06-tunis-motion.md): "**spiritual misery**" (*misère spirituelle*) striking the industrial world.
- Stiegler's *Libération* diary (June 2005): the ruin of symbolic exchange as "the great symbolic misery in which desire in general collapses" ([source](../sources/primary-parallel-stiegler-liberation-diary-2005.md)).

## How secondary sources gloss it

- **Ars Industrialis *Vocabulaire*:** the loss of individuation through losing participation in symbol production, increasing since the "machinic turn" of culture and politics. Fighting the Front National means fighting symbolic misery, and so industrial populism ([glossary](../sources/glossary-arsindustrialis-vocabulaire-2013.md)).
- **Stiegler, "Uncontrollable Societies" (2006):** symbolic misery "leads irresistibly to spiritual misery" ([source](../sources/primary-parallel-stiegler-uncontrollable-societies-nja-2013.md)).
- **Stiegler, "Pharmacology of Desire" (2011):** the producer/consumer split leaves consumers "de-symbolized" ([source](../sources/primary-parallel-stiegler-pharmacology-of-desire-2011.md)).
- **Ross (2018):** places the *Symbolic Misery* volumes at the start of the political "second phase" to which this book belongs ([source](../sources/lit-ross-neganthropocene-introduction-2018.md)).

## Disagreements

- **Labelled interpretation:** the term is used more than it is explained in this book. A reader new to Stiegler will need *Symbolic Misery* vol. 1, ch. 1–2, to see why aesthetic participation is the model for all participation. The *Libération* diary cites Leroi-Gourhan's "one must participate in order to feel".
