# Hyperindustrial society (*société hyperindustrielle*)

[Home](../README.md) › [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) › **hyperindustrial society**

## Definition (the book's usage)

Against theories of a "post-industrial" society, Stiegler argues that we live in a **hyper-industrial** epoch. Industrialization now extends beyond material production to **services, culture, cognition and everyday life**: "life itself", including attention and knowledge, is produced and managed industrially. In Ch. 1 this takes the form of **service capitalism**. Services take over activities people once performed themselves and **control the processes by which ways of life are adopted**. The term comes from *De la misère symbolique 1: L'époque hyperindustrielle* (2004).

## Where it appears

- [Introduction](../chapters/01-introduction-what-is-to-be-done.md): "cultural" or "cognitive" capitalism.
- [Ch. 1](../chapters/04-ch1-re-foundation-of-society.md) "The hyperindustrial epoch as service capitalism" (p. 29): there are "no alternatives" to an ever more industrial world. In hyperindustrial society all forms of life become objects of rationalization, investment and service; "de-industrialization" is only a new division of labour. N. 1 refers to *De la misère symbolique 1. L'époque hyperindustrielle* (2004). "Hyperindustrial service societies as the destruction of processes of individuation through the control of processes of adoption" (p. 31).
- [Ch. 2](../chapters/05-ch2-against-the-reign-of-ignorance.md) "The re-instrumentation of forms of knowledge and the future of hyperindustrial society" (p. 73): hyperindustrialization is inevitable but has **self-destructive limits**, and calls for a "renaissance of knowledge" and a "sustainable development of spirit".

## How secondary sources gloss it

- **Ars Industrialis *Vocabulaire*:** the **digital** stage of grammatization corresponds to "hyperindustrial society" ([glossary](../sources/glossary-arsindustrialis-vocabulaire-2013.md)).
- **Stiegler–Lemmens interview (2010/2011):** the industrialization of the life of the mind; a "new industrial model" rather than an exit from industry ([source](../sources/primary-parallel-lemmens-stiegler-interview-krisis-2011.md)).
- **Ross (2018):** the setting of the "second phase" of the work ([source](../sources/lit-ross-neganthropocene-introduction-2018.md)).
- **Hutnyk (2012):** places it in the cognitive-capitalism debate and doubts its novelty ([source](../sources/lit-hutnyk-proletarianisation-2012.md)).

## Disagreements

- Against post-industrial theorists (Bell, Touraine), Stiegler's point is that services are *more* industrial, not less. Lotz reads the corollary, that "the future is still to be sought in an industrial mode of production", as a weakness: industrial production is never put in question ([source](../sources/review-lotz-marx-and-philosophy-2015.md)).
- **Labelled interpretation:** the 2006 notion of "service capitalism" predates platform capitalism, but it describes the same *mechanism* (control of adoption through services). Readers often find it prescient for that reason ([Goodreads](../sources/media-goodreads-reader-reviews-2026.md)).
