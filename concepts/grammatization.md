# Grammatization (*grammatisation*)

[Home](../README.md) › [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) › **grammatization**

## Definition

Grammatization is the process by which temporal **flows** (speech, gesture, perception, calculation) are **discretized** into reproducible spatial elements, "grammes". The examples are the alphabet's letters, the machine-tool's encoded gestures, the phonograph groove and binary code. Stiegler takes the term from linguist **Sylvain Auroux** (*La révolution technologique de la grammatisation*, 1994), who used it for the alphabet's precedence over grammar and logic. He generalizes it to the whole of technical history and links it to Derrida's grammatology. Each stage of grammatization creates new possibilities of knowledge and new risks of **proletarianization**. It goes with a **general organology**: the co-evolution of physiological organs, technical organs and social organizations.

## How secondary sources gloss it

- **Ross (2018):** "the primary analytical concept" of the second phase of Stiegler's work. It extends from Palaeolithic "arche-cinema" through gestures in machines and analogue media to binary code, and connects Socrates' pharmakon of writing to Marx's *Grundrisse* ([source](../sources/lit-ross-neganthropocene-introduction-2018.md)).
- **Ars Industrialis *Vocabulaire*:** three stages (literal, analogue, digital). The digital is that of hyperindustrial society. The term is borrowed from and "diverted" from Auroux ([glossary](../sources/glossary-arsindustrialis-vocabulaire-2013.md)).
- **Lemmens (2017):** grammatization within general organology; the digital as a new *pharmakon* ([source](../sources/lit-lemmens-digital-pharmakon-general-intellect-2017.md)).

## Disagreements

- No disagreement over the concept was found among the sources consulted.
- **Labelled interpretation:** the conceptual hinge of the book is that grammatization is *neither* simply loss (Plato, the Frankfurt School) *nor* simply gain (Serres, the "knowledge society" boosters). It is the condition of both, and that is why Ch. 1 says critics of the culture industry lack it.

## In the printed text (owner's copy, read Nov 2026)

- [Ch. 1](../chapters/04-ch1-re-foundation-of-society.md) n. 2 defines grammatization as a process of description, formation and discretization of human behaviours that makes their reproducibility possible. pp. 47–51 give the deep history: hydraulic empires' writing as control; Phoenician to Greek literal writing as the citizen's associated milieu; Nora and Minc's comparison of informatics to Sumerian writing (n. 37); the nineteenth-century grammatization of gesture (phonograph, photography, Taylor, n. 38).
- [Ch. 2](../chapters/05-ch2-against-the-reign-of-ignorance.md) p. 62: the internet, like the alphabet, printing, machine-tool and TV, is a stage of grammatization that outstrips its actors' intentions ("epokhal double redoubling", n. 4). p. 87, n. 40: Auroux. The English bibliography wrongly lists Auroux's book under "Christian Fauré".
