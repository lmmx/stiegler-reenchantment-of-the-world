# Libidinal economy (*économie libidinale*)

[README](../README.md) › [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) › **libidinal economy**

## Definition (the book's usage)

The libidinal economy is the organization and production of **desire**: the channelling of libidinal energy, in Freud's sense of drive energy socialized through **sublimation**, onto objects. Stiegler's thesis is that twentieth-century capitalism made libido its main energy source. Marketing and the culture industries (Bernays) channel desire towards commodities so as to absorb overproduction. That capture ends by **destroying** desire. Desire degrades into **drives** (*pulsions*), attention is short-circuited, and investment in every sense collapses. The Introduction calls this episode of libidinal economy "a destruction of desire" and "a chapter" of Weber's disenchantment (p. 2). The term echoes Lyotard's *Économie libidinale* (1974), but Stiegler's use is Freudian and normative: desire, unlike drive, attaches to singular objects and sustains long-term investment.

## How secondary sources gloss it

- **Ars Industrialis *Vocabulaire*:** libido is "socialized and desexualized" drive energy, invested through artefacts (fetishism), which is why it can be industrialized. Unlimited exploitation exhausts it, so a **"renewable" libidinal energy** is needed, an *ecological* problem. *Besetzung* means both libidinal and financial investment ([glossary](../sources/glossary-arsindustrialis-vocabulaire-2013.md)).
- **Stiegler, "Pharmacology of Desire" (2011):** "libidinal dis-economy"; drive-based capitalism; Le Lay; investment against speculation ([source](../sources/primary-parallel-stiegler-pharmacology-of-desire-2011.md)).
- **Hansen (2014):** reads the programme as "saving libidinal capitalism", a "short-term economy of the drive" to be replaced by a long-term economy of desire. He calls the project curious, even perverse, for seeking to save capitalism ([source](../sources/lit-hansen-saving-libidinal-capitalism-2014.md)).
- **Abbinnett (2015):** the fall in libidinal energy is "the determining limit of consumer capitalism"; global inequality results from "pathologically disordered forms of libido" ([source](../sources/lit-abbinnett-politics-of-spirit-2015.md)).
- **Lemmens (2017):** accepts the libidinal-exhaustion thesis within a post-autonomist frame ([source](../sources/lit-lemmens-digital-pharmakon-general-intellect-2017.md)).

## Disagreements

- **Marxist objection** (Lotz; Hutnyk; Manche): putting desire and consumption at the centre makes **labour, capital accumulation and class** "evaporate" from the analysis ([Lotz](../sources/review-lotz-marx-and-philosophy-2015.md), [Hutnyk](../sources/lit-hutnyk-proletarianisation-2012.md), [Manche](../sources/lit-manche-proletarianisation-not-capitalism-2021.md)).
- **Arendtian objection** (Roodt): reducing the life of the mind to libido loses what Arendt meant ([source](../sources/review-roodt-tydskrif-vir-geesteswetenskappe-2012.md)).
- **Labelled interpretation:** the empirical claim that libidinal energy is being "exhausted" works as a premise in the book rather than a demonstrated finding. Supporters treat it as a structural diagnosis; critics read it as a decline narrative.
