# Technologies of spirit (*technologies de l'esprit*); psychotechnologies vs nootechnologies

[README](../README.md) › [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) › **technologies of spirit**

## Definition (the book's usage)

The **2005 Manifesto** coined "technologies of spirit" for the sector formed when digitization makes the **culture industries, program industries, media, telecommunications and cognitive technologies converge**. These technologies are *spiritual* because they work on attention, memory, imagination and desire. They are also ***hypomnemata***. Ars Industrialis is defined as an association "for an industrial politics of technologies of spirit". The 2010 Manifesto sharpens the point into a normative pair. **Psychotechnologies** serve behavioural control (marketing, audience capture). **Nootechnologies** serve the life of the mind and de-proletarianization. The same device can be either.

## How secondary sources gloss it

- **Ars Industrialis *Vocabulaire* ("Psychotechnique / nootechnique"):** the opposition is "normative, assumed as such". Psychotechnics act on or against the mind to control it, and nootechnics foster its individuation. The criterion is **reciprocity** (the reader can also write) and long circuits of transindividuation ([glossary](../sources/glossary-arsindustrialis-vocabulaire-2013.md)).
- **Stiegler, "Within the limits of capitalism…" (c. 2008):** calls for a European industrial politics of technologies of spirit ([source](../sources/primary-parallel-stiegler-economizing-taking-care-2008.md)).
- **Stiegler, 5 Nov 2005 session** on cognitive technologies, knowledge management and design methodology ([source](../sources/media-stiegler-technologies-cognitives-2005.md)).

## Disagreements

- **Labelled interpretation:** critics rarely address the term directly. It inherits the debates on [pharmakon](pharmakon.md) and on statism ([reception.md](../reception.md)).

## In the printed text (owner's copy, read Nov 2026)

- **"R technologies" = relational technologies** ([Ch. 1](../chapters/04-ch1-re-foundation-of-society.md) p. 29; n. 5 cites Rifkin, *The Age of Access*): telecom and broadcast networks, barcodes, chip cards, RFID, wi-fi, Bluetooth, biometrics, nanotechnologies. In dissociated milieus they serve control. They remain technologies of spirit and could support individuation (pp. 37–38). Nora and Minc foresaw the convergence of audiovisual, telecom and computing but not an "industrial politics of technologies of spirit" (pp. 50–51).
