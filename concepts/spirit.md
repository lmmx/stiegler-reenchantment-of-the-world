# Spirit (*esprit*; *la valeur esprit*)

[Home](../README.md) › [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) › **spirit**

## Definition (the book's usage)

*Esprit* is the noetic life of human beings: intelligence, attention, memory, judgement and desire as sublimated. Stiegler treats it as **both psychic and collective**, and as dependent on technical supports. The phrase **"la valeur esprit"**, "the value [called] spirit", comes from **Paul Valéry** (1939). Valéry compared spirit to a commodity value, like oil or wheat, whose price can fall, and he saw a "drop in the value of spirit" before the war. The book's wager is that spirit is now the main industrial resource ("after matter, spirit has become the first industrial resource", Tunis motion) *and* the thing being depleted. Spirit in this sense is not religious. The Tunis motion warns against locating spirit only in religion. Nor is it the brain: the Introduction contrasts consciousness, the seat of spirit, with "a brain reduced to an ensemble of neurons".

## Where it appears

- Title and subtitle; [Translator's Preface](../chapters/00-translators-preface.md) (pp. viii–ix: *esprit* versus "spirit").
- [Introduction](../chapters/01-introduction-what-is-to-be-done.md) pp. 1–3: Valéry; is "knowledge without spirit" possible? P. 7: spirit, which always presupposes technologies of spirit, is "a modality of… psychic and collective individuation" (Simondon). Knowledge is "the treasure bequeathed by those who are dead".
- [2005 Manifesto](../chapters/02-manifesto-2005.md): "technologies of spirit", "industrial ecology of spirit", the "life of the mind/spirit" (Arendt). P. 11: "spirit" is meant also in the sense of the English word "mind", despite its metaphysical and theological baggage.
- [Ch. 1](../chapters/04-ch1-re-foundation-of-society.md): the **"enslaved spirit… servile spirit, which serves the service societies"** (p. 33; cited by Lotz); capitalism "needs a spirit" (p. 35); "new spiritual devices" (p. 37); capitalism become barbarous is "denuded of spirit" (p. 43); a "debilitated", "rotten" or "servile" spirit cannot found enterprises (p. 46); Weber's spirit of capitalism has been lost through the control of affects (p. 55).
- [Ch. 2](../chapters/05-ch2-against-the-reign-of-ignorance.md) title; p. 67 (demotivation as the "drop in the value of spirit", the loss of the "spirit of capitalism"); p. 68 (spirit as the capacity to imagine and concretize alternatives, per Lotz); p. 69 (spirit cannot be adapted; one learns a theorem by re-living its genesis, *methodos*).
- [Tunis motion](../chapters/06-tunis-motion.md): spirit as both "available brain time" and "works of the mind". P. 96: spirit must not be situated "exclusively on the side of the religious"; underinvestment in the value of spirit has fed "regressive and radical spiritualisms". P. 100: reason as "universal *motif* of spirit".

## How secondary sources gloss it

- **Ars Industrialis *Vocabulaire*:** spirit is what brain activity becomes when it turns external things into memory supports. It is the dynamic of technically mediated **transindividuation**, "the 'and'" in Simondon's "psychic *and* collective individuation". When people can no longer *use* the technologies imposed on them, spirit loses its capital and **capitalism loses its spirit** ([glossary](../sources/glossary-arsindustrialis-vocabulaire-2013.md)).
- **Stiegler, "Uncontrollable Societies" (2006 text):** symbolic misery leads to **spiritual misery** and the fall of the value of spirit. Ross's translator's note on *esprit* as mind and spirit is a useful comparison with Arthur's choice of "spirit" ([source](../sources/primary-parallel-stiegler-uncontrollable-societies-nja-2013.md)).
- **Trevor Arthur:** *esprit* as a synthesis of psychic, social, intellectual and historical life ([source](../sources/primary-bloomsbury-preview-2014.md)). **Lyons** credits this preface as the best key to the term ([source](../sources/review-lyons-critical-inquiry-2014.md)).
- **Cohen (2017):** the best gloss of the Valéry genealogy. She links spirit to Stiegler's subsistence/existence/consistence scheme ([source](../sources/lit-cohen-dare-to-care-2017.md)).
- **Abbinnett (2015):** reads spirit through Derrida's *Of Spirit*. Stiegler shares Derrida's suspicion of Heidegger's *Geist*, but in Abbinnett's reading spirit must pass through epiphylogenetic, written memory ([source](../sources/lit-abbinnett-politics-of-spirit-2015.md)).

## Disagreements

- **Futural versus memorial.** Abbinnett contests Beardsworth's Nietzschean, "remorselessly futural" reading of Stieglerian spirit and ties it to inherited, technically retained memory.
- **Empty or full?** Lotz calls the positive notion an empty placeholder ("battle against regression", p. 71) ([source](../sources/review-lotz-marx-and-philosophy-2015.md)).
- **Spirit = libido?** Roodt (Arendtian) argues the manifestos reduce Arendt's "life of the mind" to libido, as a function of economic forces ([source](../sources/review-roodt-tydskrif-vir-geesteswetenskappe-2012.md)). De Beer, in the same journal issue, embraces the vocabulary in a quasi-spiritual register ([source](../sources/review-debeer-tydskrif-vir-geesteswetenskappe-2012.md)). Rossouw reads re-enchantment as Christianity by other means ([source](../sources/lit-rossouw-aestheology-new-otium-2015.md)).
- **Labelled interpretation:** the English "spirit" carries religious connotations that *esprit* (also "mind", "wit") does not. This may explain why some English readers hear the book as more spiritualist than French readers do.
