# Industrial populism (*populisme industriel*)

[Home](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/index.md>) › [Glossary](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/GLOSSARY/index.md>) › **industrial populism**

## Definition (the book's usage)

Industrial populism is a populism produced **by the organization of the economy**, specifically the culture and program industries and marketing, rather than by political movements. It levels and flatters the lowest common denominator of drives in order to capture audiences and sell their attention. Its emblem is TF1 chief Patrick Le Lay's claim (2004) that what the channel sells Coca-Cola is "available human brain time". The book's subtitle sets the **value of spirit** *against* it. The Introduction defines contemporary "cultural" or "cognitive" capitalism as "before all else the destructive organization of an industrial populism" that uses each technical evolution to turn consciousness into a reflex organ (p. 1).

## How secondary sources gloss it

- **Ars Industrialis *Vocabulaire*, "Populisme industriel et télécratie":** a populism "of a new kind" whose causes are economic. Consumerist societies reach their limits, life becomes drive-based and addictive, and **political populism follows secondarily**. "Telecracy" is mass audiovisual media short-circuiting political transindividuation ([glossary](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/glossary-arsindustrialis-vocabulaire-2013/index.md>)). In 2013 the association used the concept to analyse the Front National (*Pharmacologie du Front National*).
- **Stiegler, "Uncontrollable Societies" (2006):** ties industrial populism to "available brain time" and to the loss of shame (*aidos*) ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/primary-parallel-stiegler-uncontrollable-societies-nja-2013/index.md>)).
- **Ross (2018):** reads later "industrially-generated populism" and "post-truth" politics as the continuation of this diagnosis ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/lit-ross-neganthropocene-introduction-2018/index.md>)).
- **Abbinnett (2015):** uses the term as shorthand for the "disorientated life" of consumer culture ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/lit-abbinnett-politics-of-spirit-2015/index.md>)).
- **Context:** the exact wording and source of Le Lay's statement is in [context-le-lay-temps-de-cerveau-2004.md](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/context-le-lay-temps-de-cerveau-2004/index.md>).

## Disagreements

- The secondary literature **uses the term but rarely analyses it**.
- **Labelled interpretation:** in 2005–06 the concept is *television-centred* (Le Lay, *Star Academy*). Readers such as a Goodreads reviewer note that the remedies are correspondingly TV-focused ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/sources/media-goodreads-reader-reviews-2026/index.md>)). Its extension to platforms and social media is a later move by Stiegler (2010 Manifesto §4; *The Neganthropocene*).
- The French term differs from the Anglo-American political-science use of "populism". Stiegler's claim is causal: economic organization comes first, political populism second.
