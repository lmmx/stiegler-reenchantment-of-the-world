# Economy of contribution (*économie de la contribution*)

[README](../README.md) › [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) › **economy of contribution**

## Definition (the book's usage)

The economy of contribution is the industrial model proposed to replace the **consumerist** model. Its traits:

1. **No separation between producers and consumers.** There are only *contributors*, as in free/open-source software, Wikipedia, smart grids and community-supported agriculture (AMAP).
2. Its value takes the form of **positive externalities**: care for self and others, and Sen's **capabilities**, much of which cannot be monetized.
3. Its milieus are **associated** (Simondon), so participants individuate themselves by individuating the milieu.
4. It aims at **de-proletarianization**: the reconstitution of *savoir-faire*, *savoir-vivre* and theoretical knowledge.

## Where it appears

- [2010 Manifesto](../chapters/03-manifesto-2010.md) §3 (named and defined), §6 (free software as de-proletarianization), §7 (generalization: smart grids, AMAP, contributory businesses), §9.3 (fiscal policy for positive externalities; work as distinct from employment).
- **Proto-forms in 2005–06:** [Ch. 2](../chapters/05-ch2-against-the-reign-of-ignorance.md) p. 91 calls for hypertextual "**societies of authors and readers**", contrary to dominant business models and so "insolvent". Lotz, pp. 87 and 92: **Wikipedia** and "cooperative instruments of forms of knowledge". The term itself is not attested in the 2006 texts we could read. Ars Industrialis's founding minutes (June 2005) record no settled position yet on free software ([source](../sources/media-arsindustrialis-seance-inaugurale-2005.md)).

## How secondary sources gloss it

- **Ars Industrialis *Vocabulaire*:** three traits: no producer/consumer split; non-monetizable value as positive externality; an economy of **existences** as much as of subsistences. The contributor is neither consumer, nor taxpayer, nor gift-giver in Mauss's sense ([glossary](../sources/glossary-arsindustrialis-vocabulaire-2013.md)).
- **Stiegler–Lemmens interview (2010):** a "new industrial model". Stiegler explicitly brackets whether it will still be capitalism. Lemmens presses him on the marginality of free software and on the neoliberal state ([source](../sources/primary-parallel-lemmens-stiegler-interview-krisis-2011.md)).
- **Hui & Halpin (2013):** a Simondonian design programme for social networks along contributory lines ([source](../sources/lit-hui-halpin-collective-individuation-2013.md)).
- **Abbinnett (2015):** frees labour from "ratio" so that otium becomes a universal good ([source](../sources/lit-abbinnett-politics-of-spirit-2015.md)).

## Disagreements

- **Manche (2021):** the most rigorous critique. Contribution rests on a teleological, operaist reading of automation. It sidelines profit and accumulation, yields "good vs bad capitalism" reformism, and risks **self-exploitation and co-optation** by corporate partners ([source](../sources/lit-manche-proletarianisation-not-capitalism-2021.md)).
- **Lemmens (2017):** accepts the pharmacological frame but criticizes Stiegler's **statism** ([source](../sources/lit-lemmens-digital-pharmakon-general-intellect-2017.md)).
- **Lotz (2015):** the book's only example is Wikipedia (p. 87). Benkler's *Wealth of Networks* says more ([source](../sources/review-lotz-marx-and-philosophy-2015.md)).
- **Stiegler's own revision:** by *The Neganthropocene* (2018) he judged the algorithmic web "a systemically dissociating milieu", which qualifies the 2010 optimism ([Ross](../sources/lit-ross-neganthropocene-introduction-2018.md)).
