# Ars Industrialis: 2010 Manifesto (pp. 17–28)

[Home](../README.md) › [Overview](../overview.md) › [Structure](../STRUCTURE.md) › **2010 Manifesto** · prev: [← 2005 Manifesto](02-manifesto-2005.md) · next: [Chapter 1 →](04-ch1-re-foundation-of-society.md)

> **The text.** pp. 17–28, with notes on pp. 102–104. The Introduction's n. 17 (p. 102) says this text is **absent from the 2006 French book** and was added for the English edition. The association also published it on its website ([source](../sources/primary-arsindustrialis-manifesto-2010.md)). Sections start on p. 17 (§1), p. 18 (§2–3), p. 19 (§4), p. 22 (§5), p. 23 (§6), p. 25 (§7), p. 26 (§8–9) and p. 28 (§10).

## Abstract (L2)

Written five years after the founding and three years into the global financial crisis, this manifesto claims the 2005 diagnosis is vindicated. The crisis is that of the **consumerist model** as a whole. That model instrumentalized desire (Bernays) and so destroyed it, together with every form of **investment**. Speculator and consumer share a **drive-based**, **[short-termist](../concepts/short-termism.md)** behaviour. Disposability produces an **addictogenic** society of **carelessness** (*incurie*), so "to economize means first of all to take **[care](../concepts/care.md)**". The alternative industrial model is the **[economy of contribution](../concepts/economy-of-contribution.md)**: free software, contributors rather than producers and consumers, **[associated milieus](../concepts/associated-milieu.md)**. Digital technologies are ***[pharmaka](../concepts/pharmakon.md)*** and can either deepen or end generalized **[proletarianization](../concepts/proletarianization.md)**. Public power must act through seven national policies (industrial, financial, educational, fiscal, cultural, health and media) and through territorial politics.

## Core claims

1. The 2007– crisis is a crisis of consumerism, not only of finance. Destroyed desire means **disinvestment** and "systemic stupidity".
2. Disposable objects make subjects feel **disposable**. Consumer society is toxic environmentally and psychically, **addictogenic**, and a society of **carelessness**.
3. **Care** must be at the heart of political economy: economizing *is* taking care.
4. The consumerist model is obsolete. It must give way to the **economy of contribution**, which produces positive externalities and capabilities (Sen). On digital networks, contributors form an **associated milieu** (Simondon).
5. Digital networks also breed hyper-consumerist behaviour because all **hypomnemata** are **pharmaka** (Plato). Without a therapeutic (care, technique of the self) a pharmakon turns toxic. **Politics is a system of care**, and culture cultivates a caring relation to pharmaka.
6. Culture industries (new hypomnemata) competed with **program institutions** (schools) and replaced reason with **rationalization**. Marketing seized digital relational technologies. Public power has abdicated since Thatcher and Reagan.
7. Politics must secure the short term *in order to* reverse it in the long term, moving from **disapprenticeship** to the development of capabilities.
8. **Proletarianization** is defined as the loss of knowledge formalized in an apparatus. It struck workers (*savoir-faire*), then consumers (*savoir-vivre*), and now cognitive workers (theoretical knowledge; Greenspan). Free software shows **de-proletarianization**. **Psychotechnologies** must become **nootechnologies**.
9. The model generalizes: smart grids, community-supported agriculture (AMAP), contributory firms. "Light time" replaces "carbon time". Relational technologies **re-territorialize** and lead to post-globalization.
10. National policy has seven planks in the book (the web version has six; the book adds the rebuilding of financial systems against speculation), and Ars Industrialis adopts a programme to match.

## Argumentative moves

- **Vindication.** The 2005 prediction is presented as fulfilled.
- **Psychoanalytic economics generalized.** Drives against desire; disinvestment; addiction.
- **Pharmacological turn.** Technics is neither good nor bad but needs therapeutics. This replaces the 2005 opposition of control to spirit with a single ambivalent object.
- **Positive model.** Free software and peer production become the paradigm of a whole industrial model, the "economy of contribution".
- **Programmatic close.** A list of policies, as in a party platform.

## Notes

- **Chronology (corrected).** This text was added in 2014 but written in 2010. *Pharmakon*, associated milieu and the economy of contribution are **not** new here: Ch. 1 (2006) already develops them (pp. 36–37, 47–51). What is new in 2010 is their systematic use: care as the axiom of political economy, Sen's capabilities, territories and smart grids, and the seven policy planks. Earlier versions of this file said these terms were "at most embryonic" in the chapters; that was wrong.
- **Labelled interpretation:** this manifesto is the book's most concrete answer to the charge (Lotz) that Stiegler offers no positive programme. Reviewers who stress abstraction tend to under-weight it.
