## Abstract (L2)

Written five years after the founding and three years into the global financial crisis, this manifesto claims the 2005 diagnosis is vindicated. The crisis is that of the **consumerist model** as a whole. That model instrumentalized desire (Bernays) and so destroyed it, together with every form of **investment**. Speculator and consumer share a **drive-based**, **[short-termist](../concepts/short-termism.md)** behaviour. Disposability produces an **addictogenic** society of **carelessness** (*incurie*), so "to economize means first of all to take **[care](../concepts/care.md)**". The alternative industrial model is the **[economy of contribution](../concepts/economy-of-contribution.md)**: free software, contributors rather than producers and consumers, **[associated milieus](../concepts/associated-milieu.md)**. Digital technologies are ***[pharmaka](../concepts/pharmakon.md)*** and can either deepen or end generalized **[proletarianization](../concepts/proletarianization.md)**. Public power must act through six national policies (industrial, educational, fiscal, cultural, health and media) and through territorial politics.

## Core claims

1. The 2007– crisis is a crisis of consumerism, not only of finance. Destroyed desire means **disinvestment** and "systemic stupidity".
2. Disposable objects make subjects feel **disposable**. Consumer society is toxic environmentally and psychically, **addictogenic**, and a society of **carelessness**.
3. **Care** must be at the heart of political economy: economizing *is* taking care.
4. The consumerist model is obsolete. It must give way to the **economy of contribution**, which produces positive externalities and capabilities (Sen). On digital networks, contributors form an **associated milieu** (Simondon).
5. Digital networks also breed hyper-consumerist behaviour because all **hypomnemata** are **pharmaka** (Plato). Without a therapeutic (care, technique of the self) a pharmakon turns toxic. **Politics is a system of care**, and culture cultivates a caring relation to pharmaka.
6. Culture industries (new hypomnemata) competed with **program institutions** (schools) and replaced reason with **rationalization**. Marketing seized digital relational technologies. Public power has abdicated since Thatcher and Reagan.
7. Politics must secure the short term *in order to* reverse it in the long term, moving from **disapprenticeship** to the development of capabilities.
8. **Proletarianization** is defined as the loss of knowledge formalized in an apparatus. It struck workers (*savoir-faire*), then consumers (*savoir-vivre*), and now cognitive workers (theoretical knowledge; Greenspan). Free software shows **de-proletarianization**. **Psychotechnologies** must become **nootechnologies**.
9. The model generalizes: smart grids, community-supported agriculture (AMAP), contributory firms. "Light time" replaces "carbon time". Relational technologies **re-territorialize** and lead to post-globalization.
10. National policy has six planks, and Ars Industrialis adopts a programme to match.

## Argumentative moves

- **Vindication.** The 2005 prediction is presented as fulfilled.
- **Psychoanalytic economics generalized.** Drives against desire; disinvestment; addiction.
- **Pharmacological turn.** Technics is neither good nor bad but needs therapeutics. This replaces the 2005 opposition of control to spirit with a single ambivalent object.
- **Positive model.** Free software and peer production become the paradigm of a whole industrial model, the "economy of contribution".
- **Programmatic close.** A list of policies, as in a party platform.
