## Abstract (L2)

Chapter 1 is Stiegler's 2006 diagnosis of **[hyperindustrial](../concepts/hyperindustrial-society.md) service capitalism**. Its programme is that society must be **re-founded**, which means re-founding capitalism itself. In the hyperindustrial epoch, services take over activities people once did for themselves, and the **consumer is "discharged" of their own existence**. Individuation proceeds by **adopting** ways of life, and service capitalism takes **control of adoption** at every level through ICTs used as control technologies. This generalizes **[proletarianization](../concepts/proletarianization.md)**: producers lose *savoir-faire*, consumers lose *savoir-vivre*, and the world loses its savour. The resulting **enslaved spirit** shows up as malaise, self-disgust and hyperconsumption. Against this "unhappy destiny of consumption" and the **capitalism of drives** with its slide toward **barbarism**, Stiegler calls for a **jumpstart (*sursaut*)**. It would be grounded in a long view of **[grammatization](../concepts/grammatization.md) and individuation**, aimed at a **revolution *of* capitalism** rather than against it, and at a specifically **European way of life**. The closing section ("The plane that enchants me") returns to enchantment. Per Lotz, its last pages describe hyper-solicited attention short-circuiting **[transindividuation](../concepts/transindividuation.md)** and a youth that has "lost the feeling of existing".

## Core claims (confidence-weighted)

1. **(high)** Individuation is essentially **adoption** of ways of life, and civilizations live by adopting and being adopted.
2. **(high)** Adoption carries a future only if it lets adopters **individuate themselves**, intensifying singularity, which is **negentropy**.
3. **(high)** Hyperindustrial **service capitalism** controls adoption at all levels, starting with primary and secondary **identification**. This destroys psychic and collective individuation.
4. **(high)** This generalizes **proletarianization**. A proletarian is one who loses their knowledge: the producer becomes labour power, the consumer becomes purchasing power, and *savoir-vivre* becomes a user manual.
5. **(high)** Cognitive capitalism and the "knowledge society" are announced just as capitalism **liquidates knowledge**, producing entropy, disgust and an insipid world.
6. **(high)** What critiques of the culture industry lack is a **[grammatological](../concepts/grammatization.md)/organological** perspective. That perspective shows both why this stage is necessary and that alternatives are open.
7. **(medium, Lotz p. 33)** The result is an **"enslaved spirit"**: generalized malaise in which hyperconsumption is a symptom.
8. **(low–medium, titles + parallels)** The **capitalism of drives** tends to **become barbaric**. Overcoming it needs a **jumpstart**, a **revolution of capitalism**, and a **European** way of life.
9. **(medium, Lotz pp. 57–58)** Hyper-solicitation of attention short-circuits **transindividuation**, causing **disidentification and disindividuation**, loss of attention, "urbanity", "politeness" and civility. The young have "lost the feeling of existing".

## Argumentative moves

- **Ontology of adoption** (from *Technics and Time 3*). Society is a process of adopting ways of life, so whoever controls adoption controls individuation.
- **Pun as argument.** *savoir* / *sapere* / *sapide*: knowledge gives the world its taste. Losing knowledge makes the world insipid, which is disenchantment in the literal sense of a world without flavour.
- **Extended proletarianization.** Marx's worker and the new consumer are analysed together as losers of knowledge.
- **Correcting the left critique** (Marc Jimenez, Adorno's line). Agreement on "mass mystification", but the critique lacks **[general organology](../concepts/grammatization.md)**.
- **Reformist turn** (titles: "revolution of capitalism", "jumpstart"). The target is capitalism's *current* organization, not capitalism as such. Critics seize on this; see [reception.md](../reception.md).

| 31 | **Hyperindustrial service societies as the destruction of processes of individuation through the control of processes of adoption** | We do not choose how we live; we adopt ways of life. **Individuation = adoption** (*Technics and Time 3*). Examples: China, formed over 3,000 years, with characters as Asian **hypomnemata**, and possibly exporting its way of life through services; Alexander's reciprocal religious adoptions in Egypt. Adoption has a future only if it **strengthens or creates individuation** (singularity, negentropy). Hyperindustrial capitalism **controls adoption** at every level (primary and secondary identification), using ICTs as **control** rather than individuation technologies. This yields generalized **proletarianization** (*savoir-faire*, *savoir-vivre*) and a world without savour (*sapere*/*sapide*). The proletarian is one who loses their knowledge. Cognitive capitalism (Negri to Seillière) and knowledge society (Blair, UNESCO, Serres) are announced as knowledge is liquidated. Jimenez on "mass mystification" lacks **general organology**, defined in a note as the transductive co-evolution of physiological organs, technical organs and social organizations. | French text (pp. 42–47) · **high** |
| 33 | **Re-enchanting the world faced with the unhappy destiny of consumption** | The **enslaved spirit** serving service societies produces malaise and self/other-disgust. Hyperconsumption is only an apparent paradox (against Lipovetsky's *Le Bonheur paradoxal*, 2006). "Malaise in civilization" has become **malaise of consumption**, which leads to a generalized refusal of change, "especially in France". The section title frames re-enchantment as the answer to consumption's "unhappy destiny". | end of French §3 + Lotz (p. 33) · **medium** |

Thematically: new devices for spirit (networked digital technologies) as possible supports of individuation rather than control. | title only · **low** |
