## Abstract (L2)

This is the founding text of **Ars Industrialis** (spring 2005), signed by George Collins, Marc Crépon, Catherine Perret, Bernard Stiegler and Caroline Stiegler. Digitization is merging the culture industries, media, telecoms and cognitive technologies into one sector the manifesto names **[technologies of spirit](../concepts/technologies-of-spirit.md)**. Submitted only to market criteria, these serve **control**. Their control of desire destroys **[libidinal energy](../concepts/libidinal-economy.md)**, the very energy twentieth-century capitalism ran on. "This capitalism is self-destructive" and heads for an unprecedented crisis. The remedy is an **industrial politics** that is also an **industrial ecology of spirit**. It would be carried by a **new form of public power** and would invent new practices of these technologies understood as ***[hypomnemata](../concepts/hypomnemata.md)***. The manifesto ends with ten "motifs and motives" and a programme of meetings, including one on the **Tunis WSIS**.

## Core claims

1. The "life of the mind/spirit" (Arendt) faces total subjection to market imperatives through **converging** culture, program, media, telecom and cognitive industries.
2. These technologies of spirit could found **a new age of spirit**. Under market criteria alone they serve **societies of control** (Burroughs/Deleuze) and short-term profit.
3. Control of desire destroys **singularity**, and with it existence. Capitalism harnessed **libido** to absorb surplus production. That capture now *destroys* libido, which "leads, inevitably, to an unprecedented global crisis".
4. The ruin of desire is the ruin of **sublimation** and of the super-ego, and has psychic, social, political and geopolitical consequences.
5. Desire's objects are **singular** (incalculable). Industrial fabrication of desire **categorizes** singularities. ICTs are **hypomnemata** (Foucault, Plato) and so call for new practices and social organizations, not just user manuals and marketing.
6. The task is to "give a boost to desire", not consumption. That requires a **new public power** uniting all kinds of competence.

## Argumentative moves

- **Thesis by naming.** Coining "technologies of spirit" makes media, telecom and IT one political object.
- **Freud applied to macro-economics.** Libido as capitalism's energy source; its exhaustion as systemic risk.
- **Hypomnemata argument.** Placing ICTs in the lineage of ancient memory supports (Stoic/Epicurean/Christian *hypomnemata*) shows they *can* support care of the self. What is at stake is practice, not the technology itself.
- **Prediction.** "Unprecedented global crisis" is a falsifiable claim that the 2010 Manifesto later presents as confirmed.

## Notes

- **Absent here:** *pharmakon*, care (*soin*), economy of contribution, proletarianization. All appear in the **2010** Manifesto, and the first three are already developed in Ch. 1 (2006, pp. 36–37, 47). ***Otium***, by contrast, *is* named in the printed 2005 text (p. 13). That contrast is the clearest measure of how Ars Industrialis's vocabulary developed between 2005 and 2010.
- **Labelled interpretation:** the manifesto's politics is reformist and Europeanist ("new public power", partnership with investors). Marxist reviewers such as Lotz criticize exactly this. See [reception.md](../reception.md).
