# A Motion Adopted by Ars Industrialis on the Eve of the Tunis Summit (pp. 95–100)

## Abstract (L2)

Two days before the second phase of the UN **World Summit on the Information Society** (Tunis, 16–18 Nov 2005), the five founders of Ars Industrialis declared it a "missed appointment". The real stake of the "information society" is UNESCO's **knowledge societies**: raising intelligence, with knowledge essentially shared. The summit would not ask how information, knowledge, technology, industry and society must be governed together to transform a **consumption capitalism** of **desublimation** and regression. The motion calls for **another summit** built around an **industrial politics of [technologies of spirit](../concepts/technologies-of-spirit.md)**, led by a new **public power** with industry. Television should become an instrument of knowledge, not of **[industrial populism](../concepts/industrial-populism.md)**. Europe should rebuild itself on its singular tradition of a **public space of knowledge**.

## Core claims

1. Knowledge is essentially **shared and shareable**. Digital cognitive and cultural technologies are restructuring the entire global public space, which must be **regulated** to serve the capacity to discern, criticize, create and transmit.
2. Tunis will not address the relation between information, knowledge, technology, industry and society as the stake of transforming capitalism.
3. Against **spiritual misery** people feel a need for spirit. That need should not be met **only by religion**, because the life of spirit happens in work, relationships and communications media.
4. "Spirit" means both the **"available brain time"** sold by TV and the **works of the mind**.
5. A new public power plus economic actors should build a new industrial model aimed at **raising individual and collective intelligence**. TV must become an instrument of knowledge.
6. **Europe**: long-term public and private research funding, with no demand for short-term returns. "After matter, spirit has become the first industrial resource." New solvencies and fields of externalities.
7. A **"new spirit of capitalism"**. Living standards are measured by the quality of spiritual life, "not … the quantity of protein consumed".
8. Critique of policy: the **Beffa report** (industrial policy) ignores technologies of spirit; Barroso's **Lisbon strategy** goes the wrong way; "cultural diversity" is not enough.
9. The stake is a Europe Europeans believe in again: a public space of knowledge that is no longer only written but **industrial-cognitive-cultural**.

## Notes

- **Context.** WSIS's second phase was widely judged in the press to have achieved little beyond the internet-governance compromise (creation of the IGF). Holding it in Ben Ali's Tunisia was criticized by press-freedom groups (RSF). The motion ignores the location issue entirely and argues at the level of the knowledge/information distinction.
- **Labelled interpretation:** this is the most "official" register in the book, addressed to policy-makers (Élysée, the European Commission). Its continuity with Ch. 2 is exact: Ch. 2 is the September talk given to prepare it.
