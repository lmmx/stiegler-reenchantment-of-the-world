## Abstract (L2)

The Introduction gives the book its title and its problem. In 2005 the French employers' federation, **Medef**, held its summer university under the slogan *"le réenchantement du monde"* to promote the "knowledge economy". The book "takes Medef at its word". To re-enchant the world through technologies of consciousness requires revaluing **[spirit](../concepts/spirit.md)** in the economy. The obstacle is that the same milieu (Seillière's preface to a book in which TF1's Patrick Le Lay boasts of selling "available brain time") organizes the **"drop in the value of spirit"** (Valéry, 1939) through an **[industrial populism](../concepts/industrial-populism.md)**. Twentieth-century capitalism is a **[libidinal economy](../concepts/libidinal-economy.md)** that, in capturing desire, destroys it. This is a chapter of Weber's **[disenchantment](../concepts/disenchantment.md)**. The ecological and demographic stakes of the coming decades demand "a new human consciousness". In short, humanity must overcome the **age of consumption** through a "new, qualitative growth", a "new industrial model" and a new **spirit of capitalism**. The title's echo of Lenin (and Chernyshevsky) is not glossed in the pages we could read.

## Core claims

1. The capitalism called "cultural" or "cognitive" is above all an **industrial populism** that uses every technical evolution to turn consciousness, "the seat of spirit", into a **reflex organ**. A brain without consciousness becomes a commodity on the audience market whose value keeps falling (p. 1).
2. Benjamin and Freud saw that communication technologies began a new history of consciousness and the unconscious. They did not see the organization of capitalism around the **consumer** as a **libidinal economy**, a channelling of desire. Benjamin saw the aestheticization of politics under fascism but not the **aestheticization of the economy** through control technologies and the manufacture of consumers (pp. 1–2).
3. This libidinal economy, by destroying consciousness, **destroys desire**. That is a chapter of Weber's **disenchantment** of the world (p. 2).
4. Medef's 2005 "re-enchantment" (Denis Kessler; Tony Blair's proposal to move EU agricultural funds to cognitive-technology R&D) is ambiguous. It could be a genuine "jumpstart" of spirit, or the industrial production of **knowledge without spirit**, as one now produces "brains without consciousness" (pp. 2–3).
5. Ars Industrialis is "an international association for an industrial politics of technologies of spirit" (p. 3).
6. Humanity faces challenges (ecology, scarcity; Laurence Tubiana, Robert Lyon's "age of less") that require a new human consciousness and **overcoming the age of consumption** by "new, qualitative growth" (p. 3).
7. (per Lotz) Contemporary capitalism replaces intelligence and responsibility with "the time of gregarious audiences", brains without consciousness, nervous systems turned into **drive** systems (p. 4). A "new spirit of capitalism" and a "new industrial model" are needed (p. 6), and with them a political economy (p. 7).

## Argumentative moves

- **Taking the adversary at its word.** The book does not reject the business slogan "re-enchantment". It radicalizes it, and turns it against those who coined it.
- **Juxtaposition as proof.** Seillière's knowledge-economy rhetoric and Le Lay's "brain time" appear in the *same* book (*Les Dirigeants face au changement*, 2004). Stiegler treats this co-presence as revealing the system's contradiction.
- **Genealogy by great names.** Valéry (value of spirit), Benjamin and Freud (technology and the psyche), Weber (disenchantment) and Derrida (*Echographies of Television*, 1993) locate the present as an unforeseen stage.
- **From culture critique to political economy.** The question "what is to be done?" is answered by founding an association and calling for an industrial politics.

## Context you need

- **Medef**: Mouvement des entreprises de France. Ernest-Antoine Seillière was president until July 2005, succeeded by Laurence Parisot (to whom the French edition is dedicated). Denis Kessler was a leading Medef ideologue. See the context files in [sources/](../sources/EVALUATION.md).
- **Patrick Le Lay**: TF1 chief executive. His statement, in *Les Dirigeants face au changement* (Éditions du Huitième jour, 2004), that what TF1 sells Coca-Cola is "temps de cerveau humain disponible" became proverbial in France.
