## Abstract (L2)

Chapter 2 is a philosophical policy paper on the **"information society"**, written to steer Ars Industrialis's intervention at the 2005 Tunis summit. Its claim is that the **knowledge society** proclaimed by the UN, the EU (Lisbon) and Medef is turning into its opposite, a **reign of ignorance**. **Information**, as a commodity, is **entropic**: its value decays with time. **Knowledge** is **negentropic**: it lasts, intensifies and constitutes time. Firms practising "continuous adaptation" and media practising **[industrial populism](../concepts/industrial-populism.md)** (Le Lay's "available brain time") control both production and consumption knowledge. The result is **demotivation**, losses of *savoir-faire* and *savoir-vivre*, and **disindividuation**. Stiegler rejects Plato's opposition of living memory to written memory and Michel Serres's optimism that outsourcing memory frees us for knowledge. **Memory has always been externalized** ([grammatization](../concepts/grammatization.md), [hypomnemata](../concepts/hypomnemata.md), [tertiary retention](../concepts/tertiary-retention.md)). What matters is whether societies organize its **re-interiorization**. Historically the school did this for the alphabet. Today schools are helpless before program industries. The remedy is an **industrial ecology of spirit**: public investment in **new digital instruments of knowledge**, built for scholarly practice, not repurposed office software or knowledge-management tools, together with new editorial functions and "societies of authors and readers". This **"investment in an increase in the value of spirit"** is the European policy the chapter's title calls for.

## Core claims

1. The "information society" grew from network deregulation and digitization. It realizes **convergence** into **[technologies of spirit](../concepts/technologies-of-spirit.md)**, which so far *lower* the value of spirit (Valéry) through **desublimation**.
2. The struggle is to **reverse** this, so that the vector of decline becomes one of increase.
3. Enterprises are **not** the right agent. Adaptationism is entropic and demotivating. A **long-term new public power** is required.
4. Le Lay's "available brain time" exemplifies **control society** and **industrial populism**. The **axioms of the industrial paradigm** must change.
5. The economy is **[libidinal](../concepts/libidinal-economy.md)**. Spirit concerns *motives* (motivation), hence **psychic and collective individuation**. The cognitive capitalism of the present is a stage of **grammatization** and of Weberian **[disenchantment](../concepts/disenchantment.md)**. It controls knowledge, which leads to entropy, losses of *savoir-faire* and *savoir-vivre*, and loss of singularity and desire.
6. **Instrumentation** of knowledge is necessary and good. Today's **instrumentalization** is a fact, and a *right* must be opposed to it. Control technologies can become **individuation technologies**, as Mesopotamian bookkeeping became Greek citizenship.
7. **Knowledge is negentropic; information-as-commodity is entropic.** Knowledge societies built on ICT contain this contradiction.
8. Against Plato's *Phaedrus* and Serres: externalization is originary. The danger is externalization **without correlative interiorization**, which produces a **reign of ignorance**.
9. Individuation is psychic *and* collective. Knowledge exists only as shared and public. The risk is **[disindividuation](../concepts/transindividuation.md)**, an entropic collapse of the psychic/collective/machinic relation.
10. **Cognitive saturation** and managerial **knowledge management** do not answer it. Knowledge markets are "insolvent" and public authorities have withdrawn.
11. Memory is **selection**. Industrializing memory means imposing new **selection criteria** (Google). True instruments of knowledge generate their own criteria as practical rules.
12. **Technoscience** explores becoming, not being. Transmission breaks down, which produces a **crisis of education**. Program institutions are helpless before program industries.
13. **Practical consequences:** specific instruments for research and higher education; a new publishing industry; computer-assisted intellectual work; delinearized audiovisual objects; hypertextual societies of authors and readers. These are presently "insolvent", so public policy must create the conditions for them.

## Argumentative moves

- **Immanent critique of a slogan.** "Knowledge society" is judged by its own standard. If knowledge is what it claims to value, then the actual information economy contradicts it.
- **Thermodynamic metaphor** (entropy/negentropy) to distinguish knowledge from information. This becomes central to Stiegler's late work (*neganthropology*).
- **Anti-Platonic pharmacology avant la lettre.** Writing is not memory's death but its condition. The question is *practice* (appropriation). This is the seed of the later **[pharmakon](../concepts/pharmakon.md)** doctrine.
- **Transcendental argument.** Kant's deduction ignores a "prosthetic" synthesis of imagination, so education systems built on Kantian or Enlightenment self-understanding cannot think their own instruments (*Technics and Time 3*).
- **Policy deduction.** From philosophy of memory to industrial specifications: specific, hierarchized, reversible instruments.

## Section-by-section (L3)

| p. | Section | Content (paraphrase; precursor talk unless marked) |
|---|---|---|
| 61 | **From the "informatization of society" to the "society of information"** | The information society began with e-commerce, disintermediation, tax avoidance and globalization's remote control (Boltanski & Chiapello). It is called knowledge society (UNESCO) or cognitive capitalism. Origins: civilian TCP/IP from 1992, the Clinton–Gore "information superhighways", generalized digitization, far beyond the 1978 **Nora–Minc** report (*L'informatisation de la société*; the French edition is dedicated to Simon Nora). The 1990s **convergence** of audiovisual, IT and telecom produces the **technologies of spirit**, which for now drive Valéry's drop in value: **desublimation** (not Marcuse's sense). |
| 64 | **From the "information society" to "societies of knowledge"—or, on the possibility of "re-enchanting the world"** | (Inferred mapping to talk §3.) The stake of Tunis is **spirit as the power to sublimate**, socialization as sublimation within a **libidinal economy**, always through **hypomnemata**. A **reversal** is possible, as Kessler's gloss on Medef's "re-enchantment" hoped. The question is geopolitical: what would a **European** political economy of technologies of spirit be, and could it re-enchant the European project? (Lotz cites p. 63 for a "politics of decentralization", just before this section.) |
| 66 | **Enterprises, public power, and industrial populism** | Against Medef's claim that the firm is the agent of change. (a) **Continuous adaptation** abandons grand strategy and is an **entropic vicious circle** that produces **demotivation**; long-term action by a new international public power is needed. (b) The present organization is generalized debasement, a true **industrial populism**, evidenced by Le Lay's "available human brain time" sold to Coca-Cola in the very book Seillière prefaced. (Lotz, p. 67: demotivation of producers and consumers is the concrete form of the "drop in the value of spirit", the loss of the **spirit of capitalism**. Lotz, p. 68: a "total absence of thought"; spirit as the capacity "of imagining and concretizing alternatives".) |
| 70 | **Changing the industrial paradigm** | "Brain time" is the worst case of **control society** (*Fahrenheit 451*). Le Lay boasts of next-day audience knowledge and *Star Academy* as "controlled reactivity". A real knowledge society raises everyone toward refined forms of spirit through **works of sublimation**. The Seillière/Le Lay contradiction requires a change of **axioms**, the agenda for Tunis and Lisbon. (Lotz, pp. 70–72: one's **own judgement** and **critical judgement** become difficult; "battle against regression", "conquest of the best over the worst".) |
| 71 | **Information society, disenchantment, demotivation, and the control of forms of knowledge** | Enchantment talk is possible because the economy is **libidinal**. Spirit is a question of *motives* and individuation. Cultural and cognitive capitalism peaks Weber's **disenchantment** and is a stage of **grammatization**. It controls knowledge on both sides through R&D, design and marketing. Theses: entropy versus knowledge's negentropy; losses of *savoir-faire* and *savoir-vivre*; loss of individuation, singularity and desire; need for an **industrial ecology of spirit**. |
| 73 | **The re-instrumentation of forms of knowledge and the future of hyperindustrial society** | (Talk §§7–8.) **Instrumentation ≠ instrumentalization**: all knowledge has a "techno-logic". Instrumentalization is a fact to be opposed by a right. **Control technologies are the condition of future individuation** (Mesopotamian/Egyptian records → Greek citizen). **[Hyperindustrialization](../concepts/hyperindustrial-society.md)** is inevitable but has self-destructive limits. A **renaissance of knowledge**, "sustainable development of spirit", needs true **technologies of intellect** that current innovation models cannot deliver. (Lotz, p. 73: new forms of "instrumentation"; p. 75: "collective intelligence".) |
| 77 | **Knowledge (*Savoir*) and information** | Information-as-commodity is **entropic**; knowledge is **negentropic** and constitutes time (intensive magnitudes: Kant, Thom). Lyotard (*Postmodern Condition*): knowledge produced for sale loses use-value, "a doing without knowing". The internal contradiction of knowledge societies. No spirit without a **material milieu** (Husserl, Leroi-Gourhan, Goody). **Against Serres**: externalization is the origin of the human and of knowledge; memory is "collective and objective" from the start, hence **grammatization**. |
| 79 | **Knowledge and memory, or reign of ignorance?** | Plato's *Phaedrus* opposes *anamnesis* to *hypomnesis*, but living memory must project outside itself to overcome its retentional finitude and be transmitted. The real threat is **externalization without interiorization**. The **school** appropriates the alphabetic hypomnesis. A knowledge society that does not organize the social appropriation of new **mnemotechnologies** (a European politics of technologies of spirit) becomes a **reign of ignorance**. "Anamnesic capacity" = knowledge's negentropy (Husserl's reactivation; Euclid in Lobachevsky). |
| 81 | **The risk of disindividuation as the growth of ignorance rather than knowledge** | Informationalization is threat and chance. Convergence merges conception, production and consumption. Children's brain time is captured during the educational years, so the knowledge-society question is first a **television** question requiring public policy. Individuation is psychic *and* collective; knowledge exists only as shared and public. The risk is not memory loss but **disindividuation** (entropic collapse from unresolved psychic/collective/machinic conflict) through faulty appropriation. (Lotz, p. 82: a "new industrial world".) |
| 83 | **Cognitive saturation and "knowledge management"** | "Cognitive overflow syndrome": data pile up without becoming knowledge. **KM** tools are managerial (cognitive = decision-supporting). Instruments of teaching and research are spin-offs of the tertiary sector; e-learning is disappointing or regressive. Knowledge markets are "insolvent", and public underwriting has been withdrawn. |
| 87 | **Instruments of knowledge and the criteria of selection that they produce** | Auroux: the alphabet as **grammatization** preceding all science. The third industrial revolution moves from **mnemotechnical** to **mnemotechnological** societies, where cognitive functions are delegated to machines and production absorbs the clerks. **Industries of memory and imagination** separate producers from consumers (Lotz, p. 88: memory and imagination as objects of investment). Memory = retention = **selection**; industrialization imposes selection criteria (Google). Knowledge instruments generate their own criteria. Spirit is originally externalized, therefore exploitable, so an **ecological crisis of spirit** is possible. Subordinate information to knowledge. (Lotz, p. 87: **Wikipedia** as the example of a cooperative knowledge instrument; this is not in the 2005 talk.) |
| 89 | **Being and becoming in "technoscience"** | Technoscience questions being and abandons knowledge's ontological project. Schools teach that knowledge formalizes *being* while knowledge explores *becoming*. Axiomatic and axiological crises make teachers lag structurally behind pupils' daily experience. Condorcet, Guizot and Ferry did not think from originary externalization. |
| 90 | **The crisis of education** | Kant ignored a **prosthetic synthesis** of imagination (*Technics and Time 3*). Education systems (**program institutions**) are therefore helpless before **program industries**. The ecological crisis of spirit appears first as an **education crisis**, and also as a crisis of research institutions. A new **digital instrumentality** can and must be oriented by public policy. Socialization as sublimation is threatened at its core. |
| 91 | **Practical consequences** | Knowledge instruments must be **specific, hierarchized, reversible**, not office, document-management or KM sub-applications. Production tools are fitted to qualified users. Dissemination tools share the basic functions so recipients can retrace a statement's genesis. A publishing industry should serve prescribing institutions. Priorities: (1) instruments for research and higher education; (2) new editorial functions for schools. Feasible now: computer-assisted intellectual work (annotation, critique, argument, indexing, synthesis); **delinearized audiovisual temporal objects** that de-massify audiences (INA 1997, IRCAM); **hypertextual societies of authors and readers** that run against dominant business models and are deemed "insolvent". (Lotz, p. 92: "cooperative instruments of forms of knowledge".) The chapter ends, per the p. 93 preview, by calling for a new summit preceded by debate, and on "the spirit of Europe in the age of technologies of spirit". |
