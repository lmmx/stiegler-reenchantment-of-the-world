# French back cover, dedication, and Ch. 1 §3 (French edition)

A primary text, transcribed by an amateur reader with interpolated comments marked "hcq". Character encoding is damaged (accents dropped). It gives the French edition's paratext and Ch. 1 §3. **No continuous text is reproduced here.**

## What it establishes

- **French back cover (4e de couverture).** It is essentially a compressed version of the Introduction's opening: Valéry 1939; 45% radio listenership in 1939, no TV; communicating objects chasing "available brain time" from waking to sleeping; "cultural"/"cognitive" capitalism as the ravaging organization of **industrial populism** reducing the seat of spirit to a reflex organ, "a brain without consciousness"; Medef's 2005 summer university under the banner of re-enchanting the world; "this book proposes to take it at its word". It names **Georges Collins, Marc Crépon, Catherine Perret and Caroline Stiegler** as co-founders of Ars Industrialis with Stiegler.
- **Dedication (French ed.).** "To the memory of Simon Nora. To Laurence Parisot." Nora co-wrote the 1978 Nora–Minc report *L'informatisation de la société*, which Ch. 2's first section title echoes. Parisot became Medef president in July 2005, succeeding Seillière.
- **French pagination anchor:** Ch. 1 §3 runs at **pp. 42–47** of the French first edition, against English pp. 31–33.

## Condensed account of Ch. 1 §3 (our paraphrase)

1. No one decides how they live. We reproduce ways of life inherited from family, adopted through education, or taken from foreign cultures through trade, migration, evangelization, new techniques, ideologies and culture industries. **Individuation is essentially a process of adoption** (cross-reference to *Technics and Time 3*, ch. 3).
2. Civilizations form by inventing ways of life that others adopt and by adopting others'. Examples: China, unified over about three millennia with written characters as an Asian form of **hypomnemata**, now itself a new capitalism that may export its way of life through service industries; Alexander adopting Egyptian religion and having Egyptians adopt his, as shown by an Alexandrian tomb combining Persephone/Hades with Isis/Osiris.
3. A society's future lies in adopting new ways of life, including new technologies and above all hypomnemata. But adoption carries a future only if it strengthens or creates a process of **psychic and collective individuation**, so that adopters can individuate *themselves* and intensify their singularity, their **negentropic** potential.
4. **Hyperindustrial capitalism**, as now organized, does the reverse. It **takes control of adoption at every level**, starting with primary and secondary **identification** (cross-reference to *Mécréance et discrédit 2*, *La Télécratie contre la démocratie*). This **destroys individuation**, psychic and collective. As a **service capitalism** using ICTs as technologies of **control** rather than individuation, it generalizes **proletarianization**: producers lose *savoir-faire*, consumers lose *savoir-vivre*. Life loses its taste. There is a play on *savoir*/*sapere*/*sapide*: knowledge makes the world savoury, and a world needs *savoir-vivre*, an art of living, civility, *politesse*.
5. Definition: the proletarian is one who loses their knowledge. The producer becomes pure labour power; the consumer, whose *savoir-vivre* has become a user manual, becomes pure purchasing power. Just as "cognitive capitalism" (Negri to Seillière) and "knowledge society" (Blair, UNESCO, Serres) are announced, capitalism liquidates knowledge, produces **entropy** and disgust, and makes the world insipid. Cognitive industries do to knowledge what culture industries did to the arts, turning them into entertainment or "mass mystification" (citing Marc Jimenez, 2006 seminar).
6. Jimenez's analysis lacks a projection into the dynamics of **grammatization** and a **general organology** (defined in a note: the joint evolution of physiological organs, artificial organs and social organizations, in Simondon's transductive relations). Such an approach would show both the necessity of this stage and its alternatives. Still, an **enslaved spirit** (*esprit asservi*), a servile spirit serving service societies, produces generalized malaise and self-disgust. Hyperconsumption is only a seemingly paradoxical symptom (notes cite the Ars Industrialis session "Souffrance et consommation", 25 Feb 2006, and attack Lipovetsky's *Le Bonheur paradoxal*). The malaise in civilization has become the malaise of **consumption**, leading to a generalized refusal of the ongoing transformation, "especially in France".
