# Hui and Halpin, "Collective Individuation" (2013)

A chapter in the *Unlike Us Reader*. Page numbers follow the reader's pagination. The work came out of the authors' research at IRI (Institut de recherche et d'innovation, Centre Pompidou), which Stiegler directed.

## Condensed account

- **Social networks as atomism (pp. 104–108).** The "social graph" ontology of nodes and links goes back to Moreno's sociometry in the 1930s. Digital platforms such as Facebook industrialise social relationships by treating users as "social atoms" optimised for marketing data. Time and attention are "chopped into smaller pieces". No group is formed through a common project that would invest "attention, libidinal energy, and time". Following Stiegler (*États de choc*), such atoms are "disindividuals" (p. 108).
- **Pharmakon (p. 110).** Decentralisation alone (Diaspora, StatusNet) does not escape the atomist ontology. With Stiegler (*Ce qui fait que la vie vaut la peine d'être vécue*), the technology of decentralisation is "always a pharmacon": both democratic (2011 protests) and an instrument of control.
- **Collective individuation (pp. 111–113).** With Simondon (*L'individuation psychique et collective*), individuation is always psychic *and* collective, and each individual is both agent and milieu. The alternative is to start from the collective: groups formed around a *common project*, as in Ushahidi in Haiti or tsunami mapping in Japan, where individuals individuate through the collective. They propose platforms built around collective projects (goals, tasks, merging and splitting groups). "A project is also a projection", that is, protention.
- **Conclusion (pp. 115–116).** Social networking may play the role the novel played for early capitalism (Lukács). It carries an "unredeemed promise" of global friendship.

## Short quotations (Hui &amp; Halpin)

- "these constructed social atoms are not actually 'individuals', but disindividuals" (p. 108)
- "the technology of decentralization is always a pharmacon, something that is simultaneously positive and negative" (p. 110)
- "Decentralization is not, and never will be, enough." (p. 110)

## Notes for evaluation

- **Stance:** Simondonian and Stieglerian, with an engineering bent (Halpin worked at the W3C). It applies the concepts to platform design rather than explicating them.
- **Value:** a concrete gloss on how the *associated milieu* and contribution ideas of the Ars Industrialis manifestos translate into a design programme. The term "associated milieu" itself is not used, but the "collective project" plays that role. It also illustrates the pharmacological ambivalence of networks that the later Stiegler emphasises (see lit-ross-neganthropocene-introduction-2018.md).
- **Limits:** short, programmatic, and not exegetical. It does not mention *Réenchanter*.
