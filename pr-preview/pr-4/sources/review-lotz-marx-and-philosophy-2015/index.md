# Lotz, Marx &amp; Philosophy Review of Books (2015)

## Condensed account

1. **Framing.** The book is a short presentation of the motives and assumptions of Ars Industrialis, the group Stiegler co-founded in Paris about a decade earlier. It aims at solutions to capitalism's "dead end". Lotz says Stiegler "no longer" thinks this in Marxist language and instead grounds it in the co-belonging of technics and humanity (the "prosthetic"). Ars Industrialis wants "technologies of spirit" and a politics drawn from analysing consciousness industries. **Knowledge** is the central concept.
2. **Contents.** Manifestos of 2005 and the 2010 update; two articles by Stiegler; the brief Tunis motion. The **Introduction** is "the most telling and most transparent text", outlining the central claims.
3. **Core thesis as Lotz reads it.** Contemporary capitalism centres on the control, discipline and production of **"brain time"** by industries that take over the whole mental apparatus. Psychic desires are channelled to consumption (p. 1) and the consumer is manufactured (p. 2). This produces stupidity and short-circuiting, a "total absence of thought" (p. 68), and destroys knowledge and memory as mental life becomes **proletarian** through externalized memory. Knowledge is reduced to calculable information (p. 23). Memory and imagination become objects of financial investment (p. 88). Own judgement (p. 70) and critical judgement (p. 72) become harder. Lotz likens this to extending Arendt's Eichmann analysis to a whole culture. Capitalism replaces intelligence with "brains without consciousness" and reflex/drive systems (p. 4). Hyper-solicitation of attention short-circuits **transindividuation** and causes **disindividuation**, loss of attention, civility and "politeness" (p. 57). Youth have "lost the feeling of existing" (p. 58). The result is **demotivation** of producers and consumers, the "drop in the value of spirit" and the loss of the **spirit of capitalism** (p. 67).
4. **Remedy as Lotz reads it.** Spirit = the capacity "of imagining and concretizing alternatives" (p. 68). Lotz finds this content empty ("battle against regression", "conquest of the best over the worst", p. 71). The new culture rests on **collective intelligence** (p. 75) and new **instrumentation** (p. 73), so the future is still industrial (p. 82). The only example given of cooperative knowledge instruments is **Wikipedia** (pp. 87, 92). Consumerism cannot last, "in contradiction with the future" (p. 23).
5. **Critique.**
6. Abstraction: little concrete analysis of social relations, power, political opportunities, or the **capital/labour relation**.
7. He calls for political economy (p. 7) without linking it to his concept of technology.
8. **Labour control "evaporates"** into control of consumer behaviour, and the production/consumption dialectic and the role of money and capital are not analysed.
9. The frame is a subtle return to **Kant/Enlightenment** and **reformist social democracy**: idealist and ethical (responsibility, care, spirit, education).
10. No account of ecological transformation or of a **political movement**.
11. "New spirit of capitalism" (p. 6) and "new industrial model" (p. 6) veil the real restructuring needed. "Politics of decentralization" (p. 63) and socialization of technology (p. 24) are noted. Benkler's *Wealth of Networks* tells us more.
12. **Verdict.** Post-*Technics and Time* writings are "at times repetitive". This volume works well as an **introduction to Stiegler's last decade**. Its value lies in extending culture-industry critique to technological and media systems.

## Short quotations

- "the focus of his book seems to be located in his claim that the center of our contemporary capitalist world should be seen in the control, discipline, and production of 'brain time'"
- "Overall, Stiegler's reflections remain extremely abstract and empty of any concrete content."

## Notes for evaluation

- **Accuracy:** good on the diagnosis, and the page references are consistent with the verified contents (e.g. pp. 57–58 fall in Ch. 1's last sections, pp. 66–92 in Ch. 2). One slip in framing: Lotz calls the 2010 text an update of the 2005 Manifesto. It is a new manifesto, though it does revisit 2005.
- **Coverage:** whole book, with the most citations from Ch. 2.
- **Depth:** medium. It is a \~1,200-word review.
- **Stance/bias:** **Marxist** (journal and reviewer). It judges Stiegler by the absence of a capital/labour analysis and a revolutionary subject. This is fair as external critique, but it underplays Stiegler's own claim that proletarianization is *about* knowledge (savoir-faire/vivre/theoretical). That claim is his explicit rewriting of Marx's concept. It also does not discuss the economy of contribution, which the 2010 Manifesto (pp. 17–28) puts forward as the concrete alternative.
- **Reliability:** high as a record of page-located claims. Its critical judgements are partisan but well-argued. See [EVALUATION.md](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-4/sources/EVALUATION/index.md>).
