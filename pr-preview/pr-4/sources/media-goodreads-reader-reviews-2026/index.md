# Goodreads reader reception (English edition)

## Condensed account

When read: **3.56 average from 34 ratings, 6 written reviews**. Distribution: 5★ 5, 4★ 13, 3★ 12, 2★ 4, 1★ 0. (A second Goodreads ID, 19255433, belongs to an unrelated teacher-training book. Don't confuse them.)

- **James Davies (2023):** short but hard, with long, digressive sentences. The argument is repeated but evolves: technology shapes minds and social life. Optimistic and pragmatic. The Tunis-motion proposals about **television** feel dated in the internet era.
- **Solal (2026, in French):** the author is "not anticapitalist" and is Eurocentric, but many analyses are worth keeping, especially on TV, internet, phones and games. Recommended despite the opening jargon.
- **Attasit Sittidumrong (2023, in Thai):** reads the book as a critique of a "consciousness industry" eroding critical thought and links it to Arendt's Eichmann-type functionary. "Re-enchantment" is the remedy, but the solutions are too abstract.
- **melancholinary (2019):** first Stiegler. The vocabulary is impenetrable but the core ideas are simple. Values the *pharmakon* and the libidinal critique of neoliberalism.
- **Michael (2025):** "timely" for the AI moment. Worth reading aloud in a group.
- **Peter Zomer (2024):** "gorgeously difficult". Prescient on screen addiction and on citizens becoming consumers. Part I's psychoanalytic analysis is strong, Part II's solutions abstract. Notes that Ars Industrialis never got the traction hoped for.

## Short quotations

- Davies: "Stiegler doesn't slow down much to flesh out his concepts."
- Zomer: "Gorgeously difficult. This guy has something to say."
- Michael: "it could not be more timely concerning our AI present".

## Notes for evaluation

Reader reception is consistent: difficulty and jargon, strong diagnosis, weak or abstract remedies, and a sense of prescience about attention and AI. The Thai reviewer's Arendt link is a reader's association. The Arendt reference is more explicit in other Ars Industrialis texts and in the Roodt (2012) article cited on the P2P wiki. Zomer's "Part I / Part II" probably maps to ch. 1 and ch. 2. Davies's point about TV-centred proposals is fair: the 2005 motion is explicitly about television. These are low-authority, self-selected reviews and should be used only as reception evidence.
