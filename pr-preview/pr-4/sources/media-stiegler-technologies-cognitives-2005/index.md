# Stiegler, "Cognitive technologies" (Ars Industrialis, 5 Nov 2005)

**Nature of source.** A written contribution (c. 2,600 words) posted for the session. The session page (node/1461) also lists contributions by Philippe Aigrain, Jean-Max Noyer and Alain Giffard, and links the Tunis motion.

## Condensed account

- **Origin of the concept.** Stiegler says he placed "cognitive technologies" at the basis of the Costech laboratory he founded at the Université de technologie de Compiègne in 1993. The aim was to distance it from cognitive science, which modelled cognition on an idealised Turing machine with infinite memory and ignored the role of tools and prostheses. His axioms are that every memory is finite and every machine is concrete. He compares this with Californian "situated cognition".
- **Memory theory.** As in *Technics and Time* 1, human knowledge appears with a third, external and technical memory ("epiphylogenetic") alongside genetic and somatic memory. "Tertiary retention" makes possible mnemotechnics, such as alphabetic writing as the condition of geometry, law and philosophy. Cognitive technologies are the latest **hypomnémata**. Together with the "cultural" technologies of communication, which are now merging with them through digitisation, they form the "technologies of spirit" of the Ars Industrialis manifesto. The example given is Mesopotamian catalogues as early "directories".
- **Practical experience.** He cites projects he worked on: a computer-assisted reading station for the Bibliothèque nationale de France, which was not completed because the BnF withdrew; an audiovisual reading station for the INA legal deposit; hypermedia documentary formats for digital TV; and aids for blind users.
- **Method.** Today "use" is prescribed by user manuals and marketing so as to maximise return on investment, which is almost the opposite of epistemic activity. Design should instead rely on **circles of practitioners**, whose knowledge forms a Simondonian "preindividual fund". It should treat feedback as the co-evolution of the instrument within a whole cognitive arrangement. This requires public power to accompany the time needed to build such arrangements.
- **Axioms.** "Lire, c'est écrire": perception requires motor action. Cognitive technologies are hypomnémata produced by **grammatisation** (discretisation of the continuous), and every grammatisation also produces **disindividuation**. "Désindividuations et nouvelles formes d'individuation" are the political stake, "l'objet d'une lutte politique et économique".

## Notes for evaluation

- It is useful background for ch. 2's sections on knowledge management, instrumentation vs instrumentalisation, and the call for a "knowledge editorial industry". The practical "specific, hierarchical, reversible" design principles of the 24 Sept talk are grounded here in Stiegler's own engineering projects.
- It is one of the few places where Stiegler explains the concepts behind the book in a talk setting and in a practitioner register (design methodology). It is self-presentational: the claims about Costech's priority and the BnF project come from Stiegler himself.
