# Hyperindustrial society (*société hyperindustrielle*)

[Home](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/index.md>) › [Glossary](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/GLOSSARY/index.md>) › **hyperindustrial society**

## Definition (the book's usage)

Against theories of a "post-industrial" society, Stiegler argues that we live in a **hyper-industrial** epoch. Industrialization now extends beyond material production to **services, culture, cognition and everyday life**: "life itself", including attention and knowledge, is produced and managed industrially. In Ch. 1 this takes the form of **service capitalism**. Services take over activities people once performed themselves and **control the processes by which ways of life are adopted**. The term comes from *De la misère symbolique 1: L'époque hyperindustrielle* (2004).

## Where it appears

- [Introduction](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/chapters/01-introduction-what-is-to-be-done/index.md>): "cultural" or "cognitive" capitalism.
- [Ch. 1](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/chapters/04-ch1-re-foundation-of-society/index.md>) "The hyperindustrial epoch as service capitalism" (p. 29): there are "no alternatives" to an ever more industrial world. In hyperindustrial society all forms of life become objects of rationalization, investment and service; "de-industrialization" is only a new division of labour. N. 1 refers to *De la misère symbolique 1. L'époque hyperindustrielle* (2004). "Hyperindustrial service societies as the destruction of processes of individuation through the control of processes of adoption" (p. 31).
- [Ch. 2](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/chapters/05-ch2-against-the-reign-of-ignorance/index.md>) "The re-instrumentation of forms of knowledge and the future of hyperindustrial society" (p. 73): hyperindustrialization is inevitable but has **self-destructive limits**, and calls for a "renaissance of knowledge" and a "sustainable development of spirit".

## Disagreements

- Against post-industrial theorists (Bell, Touraine), Stiegler's point is that services are *more* industrial, not less. Lotz reads the corollary, that "the future is still to be sought in an industrial mode of production", as a weakness: industrial production is never put in question ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/review-lotz-marx-and-philosophy-2015/index.md>)).
- **Labelled interpretation:** the 2006 notion of "service capitalism" predates platform capitalism, but it describes the same *mechanism* (control of adoption through services). Readers often find it prescient for that reason ([Goodreads](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/media-goodreads-reader-reviews-2026/index.md>)).
