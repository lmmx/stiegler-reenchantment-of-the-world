# Disenchantment / re-enchantment (*Entzauberung*; *désenchantement* / *réenchantement*)

[Home](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/index.md>) › [Glossary](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/GLOSSARY/index.md>) › **disenchantment**

## Definition (the book's usage)

**Max Weber's** "disenchantment of the world" (*Entzauberung der Welt*, 1917–19) names the rationalization of modern life. Magic and mystery recede before calculation, bureaucracy and science. Weber also wrote that the **spirit of capitalism** (*The Protestant Ethic*, 1904–05) outlives its religious motives. Stiegler's use has three layers:

1. **Disenchantment as libidinal destruction.** The industrial capture and destruction of desire is "a chapter of what Max Weber has described much more generally as the disenchantment of the world characteristic of capitalism" (Introduction, p. 2).
2. **Capitalism losing its spirit.** Demotivation of producers and consumers "is the concretization of the 'drop in the value of spirit'… the loss of the spirit of capitalism" (Ch. 2, p. 67, per Lotz).
3. **Re-enchantment as a contested slogan.** Medef's 2005 summer university used "le réenchantement du monde" to sell the knowledge economy. The book takes the slogan "at its word": real re-enchantment means raising the value of spirit, not producing "knowledge without spirit".

There is also a literal play: *savoir*/*sapere*/*sapide*. A world without knowledge is **insipid**, without savour (Ch. 1 §3).

## Where it appears

- Title. [Introduction](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/chapters/01-introduction-what-is-to-be-done/index.md>) pp. 2–3; epigraph by **Ulrich Beck** (what capitalism disenchants are pre-modern traditions, which stopped regenerating after the 1950s). P. 5: Medef's slogan refers to Weber. Capitalism unleashes disenchantment "with a stroke of enchantment": the Protestant spirit, tied to the printing press as a "techno-logy" of spirit. Rationalization (reason as *ratio*) then lowers the value of spirit, and the present low calls for a **sursaut**.
- [2010 Manifesto](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/chapters/03-manifesto-2010/index.md>) §4.4: reason replaced by **rationalization** (Weber, Adorno, Marcuse, Habermas).
- [Ch. 1](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/chapters/04-ch1-re-foundation-of-society/index.md>) "Re-enchanting the world faced with the unhappy destiny of consumption" (p. 33); "The plane that enchants me" (p. 58). The "plane of motifs" is "the plane of enchantment" (p. 58). Enchantment is motivation, which requires incalculable singularities (pp. 58–60).
- [Ch. 2](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/chapters/05-ch2-against-the-reign-of-ignorance/index.md>) p. 64 ("on the possibility of 're-enchanting the world'"); p. 71 ("Information society, disenchantment, demotivation…"). Pp. 71–72: talk of "enchantment" is possible because the economy is libidinal; motives are projected onto "the plane that enchants me" (n. 17); cognitive capitalism brings Weber's disenchantment to its climax.
- [Motion](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/chapters/06-tunis-motion/index.md>) p. 96: spiritual misery and Weber's disenchantment.

## How secondary sources gloss it

- **Stiegler, "Uncontrollable Societies" (2006):** extreme disenchantment means capitalism loses its spirit. He rejects Boltanski and Chiapello's "new spirit of capitalism" thesis, and Medef's "re-enchantment" is a symptom ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/primary-parallel-stiegler-uncontrollable-societies-nja-2013/index.md>)).
- **Stiegler, "Pharmacology of Desire" (2011):** "extreme disenchantment" as three combined limits of capitalism ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/primary-parallel-stiegler-pharmacology-of-desire-2011/index.md>)).
- **Lyons (2014)** and **Hansen (2014):** frame the title in Weberian terms. Hansen calls the programme a "re-enchantment *of capitalism*" ([Lyons](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/review-lyons-critical-inquiry-2014/index.md>), [Hansen](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/lit-hansen-saving-libidinal-capitalism-2014/index.md>)).
- **Context:** [context-medef-universite-ete-2005.md](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/context-medef-universite-ete-2005/index.md>) gives the 2005 Medef theme. It also warns against confusing the book with Maffesoli's *Le Réenchantement du monde* (2007) or Landy and Saler's *The Re-Enchantment of the World* (2009).

## Disagreements

- **Rossouw (2015):** re-enchantment as a secularized Christian (theological) project ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/lit-rossouw-aestheology-new-otium-2015/index.md>)).
- **Lotz (2015):** "new spirit of capitalism" talk veils the real restructuring needed ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/review-lotz-marx-and-philosophy-2015/index.md>)).
- **Labelled interpretation:** the book does *not* call for re-sacralization. The Tunis motion explicitly warns against locating spirit only in religion. Its "enchantment" is closer to motivation, desire and meaning sustained by knowledge.
