# Pharmakon / pharmacology

[Home](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/index.md>) › [Glossary](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/GLOSSARY/index.md>) › **pharmakon**

## Definition

In Plato's *Phaedrus*, writing is offered as a *pharmakon*, a drug that is at once **remedy and poison**. Derrida's "Plato's Pharmacy" (1968) made the ambivalence central. Stiegler generalizes it: **every technics is pharmacological**. It can support or destroy individuation, and which one depends on the practices, institutions and care that surround it. Without a **therapeutic** (*melete*, *epimeleia*: discipline, care, techniques of the self), a pharmakon becomes toxic. "Pharmacology" is the knowledge and politics of this ambivalence.

## Where it appears

- **Explicitly in 2006:** [Ch. 1](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/chapters/04-ch1-re-foundation-of-society/index.md>) pp. 47–51. Technologies of spirit are "poison and remedy". The *pharmakon* (Plato; Derrida, n. 35), reanalysed in *Mécréance et discrédit 3*, is a question of **socio-therapy**, of care for individuation. Writing in the *Phaedrus* is the citizen's *tekhnē tou biou* and the sophists' weapon. Digital technology is "the *hypomnématon* (the *pharmakon*)" that can reconstitute associated milieus (p. 51). Also, implicitly, [Ch. 2](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/chapters/05-ch2-against-the-reign-of-ignorance/index.md>) p. 79 (anamnesis/hypomnesis).
- **Systematically:** the [2010 Manifesto](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/chapters/03-manifesto-2010/index.md>) §4.1–4.5 and §6 ("hypomnemata are, as pharmaka, remedies as well as poisons"; psychotechnologies must become nootechnologies). De Beer cites French p. 82 for the pharmakon and technologies of spirit ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/review-debeer-tydskrif-vir-geesteswetenskappe-2012/index.md>)).
- **Chronology:** the *systematic* vocabulary of pharmacology is developed in Stiegler's 2007–2010 work (*What Makes Life Worth Living*, 2010). But the term and the argument are already explicit in Ch. 1 (2006), drawing on *Mécréance et discrédit 3* (2006).

## How secondary sources gloss it

- **Ars Industrialis *Vocabulaire*:** remedy, poison *and scapegoat* (the third sense is Derrida's). The pharmakon is both what enables care and what must be cared for. "Positive pharmacology" may still prohibit some pharmaka; the example given is nuclear power ([glossary](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/glossary-arsindustrialis-vocabulaire-2013/index.md>)).
- **Abbinnett (2015):** Stiegler extends Derrida's pharmakon beyond writing to all human–technical interfaces. It "sharpens the aporia" of technological society, between capitalized desire and new cathexes of love and spirit ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/lit-abbinnett-politics-of-spirit-2015/index.md>)).
- **Stiegler–Lemmens interview (2010):** a technology is first poisonous and becomes curative only after a second "epochal redoubling" ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/primary-parallel-lemmens-stiegler-interview-krisis-2011/index.md>)).
- **Lyons (2014):** foregrounds the pharmakon as the root of the book's "philosophy of hope" ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/review-lyons-critical-inquiry-2014/index.md>)).

## Disagreements

- **Hutnyk (2012)** turns it back on Stiegler: "stupidity too can be pharmacological", so Stiegler's own decline narrative should be read pharmacologically ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/lit-hutnyk-proletarianisation-2012/index.md>)).
- **Manche (2021):** pharmacology combined with an operaist reading of Marx's "Fragment on Machines" yields a teleological view of technology ([source](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/lit-manche-proletarianisation-not-capitalism-2021/index.md>)).
- **Labelled interpretation:** Lyons reads the 2014 *book* through the pharmakon. This fits the 2006 chapters too (pp. 47–51), not only the 2010 Manifesto.
