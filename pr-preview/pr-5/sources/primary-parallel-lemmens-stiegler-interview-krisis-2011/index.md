# Lemmens, interview with Stiegler, *Krisis* (2011)

Published in *Krisis*; a copy is at edepot.wur.nl/194315. The piece has two parts: Lemmens's introduction (secondary, pp. 33–35) and the interview itself (primary-parallel). Lemmens's introduction says Ars Industrialis "has published two manifestos and two books". *Réenchanter le monde* (2006) is one of those books, but the introduction does not name it.

## Condensed account

**Lemmens's introduction (pp. 33–35)** - **Two strands.** Lemmens places Stiegler in two lines: a Heideggerian rethinking of philosophy's forgotten technical condition (the *défaut d'origine*), and a continuation of critical theory, namely the critique of political economy and of the culture industry. He calls the second the "Marxist strand". - **Proletarianization.** Following Simondon and Marx, it is the loss of knowledge and know-how. Nineteenth-century capitalism proletarianised workers and twentieth-century capitalism proletarianised consumers, replacing their ways of life with marketed "lifestyles". Service-economy consumers are "discharged" of responsibility for their lives and lose savoir-vivre and joie de vivre. The claim that we live in a "knowledge society" is called a lie. - **Hyperindustrial society.** The systematic industrialisation of memory and cognition through digital technologies amounts to "hyperindustrial" societies, in which the life of the mind is industrialised. - **Psychopower.** On the analogy with Foucault's biopower, psychopower captures attention and desire. It ends in the destruction of libido and of sublimatory capacity, and it yields "systemic carelessness" and "systemic stupidity". Lemmens ties this to neoliberal, short-termist speculative finance. - **Contribution.** Digital networks may catalyse the passage from consumer society to a "society of contribution". Ars Industrialis is glossed as an "association for the promotion of an industrial politics of spirit".

**Interview (pp. 35–41)** - **No technological determinism.** There is a "technological condition" and a "general organology" of the psychic, technical and social. Technics is always in excess of society, which produces "disadjustment". - **Pharmakon.** It is poison at first. It becomes curative only through a second "epochal redoubling", in which society appropriates the new technical system (p. 36). - **Proletarianization.** It begins with technical exteriorisation itself, and Plato is its "first thinker". The question is whether exteriorisation produces autonomy or heteronomy. Kant's "minority" amounts to self-proletarianisation through reading without thinking (pp. 37–38). - **Neoliberal turn.** Thatcher and Reagan (1979–80) handed the socialisation of technics, previously done by state, school and church, to marketing. This produced speculative rather than investment capitalism ("mafiazation"), pp. 38–39. - **Libidinal economy / addiction.** "No pleasure produced anymore by the system". Consumers are addicts who know consumption is bad for them. Juliet Schor's survey is cited (p. 40). - **Contribution.** The question is not "the end of capitalism" but "a new industrial model". Free software, peer-to-peer and changes to property law break the 20th-century opposition between production and consumption. Stiegler is pragmatic about Open Source compromises (pp. 39–41).

## Short quotations

- Stiegler: "there is no technological determinism. What there is is a technological condition." (p. 36)
- Stiegler: "the process of proletarianization marks the beginning of humankind" (p. 37)
- Stiegler: "Because there is no pleasure produced anymore by the system." (p. 40)
- Stiegler: "The question today for me is not the end of capitalism or the return of the communist horizon. Today we have to create a new industrial model." (p. 39)

## Notes for evaluation

- **Value:** a clear oral restatement of the core programme of *Réenchanter* and the manifestos, covering the industrial model, Ars Industrialis, the opposition between production and consumption, and libidinal exhaustion, in accessible English. The p. 39 statement is a useful primary answer to the Marxist critics (Hutnyk, Manche): Stiegler explicitly brackets the question of whether the new model will still be capitalism.
- **Lemmens's stance:** sympathetic, reading Stiegler through critical theory and Heidegger. Lemmens later pursues an eco-Marxist and Sloterdijkian line, but here he presses Stiegler on the neoliberal "strong state" and on the marginality of Free Software, which makes the interview usefully probing.
- **Caveat:** the interview postdates the book by four years and uses the post-2008 pharmacology vocabulary.
