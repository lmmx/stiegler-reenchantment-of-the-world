# Stiegler, "L'avenir du capitalisme" (Libération diary, June 2005)

A week of diary entries in *Libération*, written in the founding weeks of Ars Industrialis, just after the French "no" to the European Constitutional Treaty (29 May 2005).

## Condensed account (the opening of each entry)

- **Sat 11 June, "Beautiful Europe and its libido".** American reports of lost libido are set beside a former Jospin adviser, now Orange strategist, celebrating mobile-phone "coaching". We exist only by expressing ourselves to others: the gift/counter-gift circuit of **symbolic exchange**. The culture industry has ruined it, collapsing libido and motivation and destroying aesthetic participation (Leroi-Gourhan: "one must participate in order to feel"). Aesthetic participation contains political participation (Aristotle's *philia*). The 29 May "no" is read as a sudden protest by the unheard against this **symbolic misery**.
- **Sun 12 June, "Prayer for newspapers".** The journal was first a private diary supporting **otium**, then a public organ through which a group individuates as opinion. Reduced to information it now denies opinion and destroys individuation. Richard Durn (the 2002 Nanterre shooter) wrote a diary about needing to do harm to feel he existed. Freedom passes essentially through *otium*.
- **Mon 13 June, "Flux of artifices".** Abstention of 57% in June 2004 prompted *Mécréance et discrédit 1*. Turnout of 70% on 29 May. Participation is linked to **otium**, which Stiegler equates with **spirit** (sacred or profane): time for care of self, self-cultivation and writing (Plutarch's *tekhnè tou biou*).
- **Tue 14 June, "New critique".** One year after *Les dirigeants face au changement*, Le Lay revealed that **industrial populism** is the principle of "cultural capitalism". Seillière's preface on the knowledge economy is quoted. If the knowledge economy is not a hollow slogan, television should become an organ for **elevating minds** rather than making people stupid by diverting libido. This motivates an industrial politics of technologies of spirit.
- **Wed 15 June, "Error and power".** The Daewoo fraud and Juppé's plan to sell Thomson Multimedia for one franc illustrate an internalized failure to believe in public–private industrial strategy, which affected the French Socialists too. Hence the need for a **new form of public power**.
- **Thu 16 June, "Enlightened capitalism".** Strategy begins by identifying the enemy. It is not Islamism, sovereigntism, nationalism or political populisms as such, but **desublimation**, the result of **industrial populism** (Le Lay and "enemies of the beautiful", or with Kant and Lyotard, of the sublime). An **enlightened capitalism** must put an end to it.
- **Fri 17 June, "Europe, this young girl".** Returns to 29 May as a democratic question.

## Notes for evaluation

- It shows that by June 2005 the book's key vocabulary was already in place: industrial populism, desublimation, Le Lay/Seillière, new public power and "enlightened capitalism". The vocabulary was public before the French book (October 2006).
- It shares vocabulary with Ch. 1's European theme ("A European way of life", pp. 53–58) and the "jumpstart" (*sursaut*).
- The phrase "enlightened capitalism" supports critics (Lotz; Radical Philosophy's "technoreformism") who read Stiegler as reformist. See [reception.md](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/reception/index.md>).
