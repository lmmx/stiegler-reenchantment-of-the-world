# Bloomsbury preview of the English edition

Bloomsbury's online sample of the English edition (14 pp.): front matter, contents, Translator's Preface and the opening of the Introduction. Its contents page gives the page numbers used in [STRUCTURE.md](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/STRUCTURE/index.md>).

## What it establishes

- **Bibliographic facts.** Bloomsbury Academic, London/New York, 2014. The book is in the series *Philosophy, Aesthetics and Cultural Theory* (series editor Hugh J. Silverman). The French original is given as *Réenchanter le monde* © Flammarion 2006. The English title page credits **Bernard Stiegler alone**, unlike the French cover, which reads "Bernard Stiegler et Ars Industrialis". ISBNs: pb 978-1-4411-6925-9, hb 978-1-4411-0346-8, epub 978-1-4411-5056-1, PDF 978-1-4411-6734-7. LC classification HD60.S83713 2014. The subject headings are "Industries—Social aspects" and "Technology—Social aspects".
- **Full table of contents with page numbers.** See [STRUCTURE.md](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/STRUCTURE/index.md>).
- **Translator's Preface (pp. vii–ix, Brooklyn, June 2013).** Condensed account:
- The translator found the vocabulary easy, with philosophical and technical Latin cognates, but the style hard. French allows long nested clauses, and he kept them where he could.
- He leaves *savoir-faire* and *savoir-vivre* in French. *Savoir-faire* is the craftsman's knowledge tied to a *techne*. Its loss to the machine is **proletarianization**. *Savoir-vivre* is social grace, care for self and others, and *politesse* heard through *polis*. It is lost when the person becomes consumer as well as worker, which he calls a second proletarianization. He links *savoir-vivre* to Simondon's individuation and transindividuation, as taken up by Stiegler.
- In consumer society desire is reduced to **drives**, sublimation is blocked, and wants and needs are confused. Arthur names this the problem the book calls **industrial populism**, and its antidote is the **value of spirit**.
- *Valeur esprit*: the French drops the "of". *Esprit* is broader than English "spirit". It joins the psychic, the social, the intellectual and the historical. It is tied to individuation ("one becomes who he is") and transindividuation ("we become who we are"), and to memory and intelligence meeting in reflection.
- **Epigraph.** A passage from Ulrich Beck: what is "disenchanted" in industrial modernity is pre-industrial tradition, which keeps regenerating. A second line then says this held only until the 1950s.
- **Introduction, pp. 1–3 (of pp. 1–10).** See [chapters/01-introduction-what-is-to-be-done.md](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/chapters/01-introduction-what-is-to-be-done/index.md>) for the condensed account. Key moves: Valéry's 1939 "drop in the value of spirit". The 1993 Derrida–Stiegler television conversation (*Echographies of Television*), which did not foresee "libidinal television"/reality TV. Radio reached 45% of French people in 1939; by the 2000s communication objects chase "available brain-time" day and night. "Cultural"/"cognitive" capitalism is in fact an **industrial populism** that turns consciousness into a reflex organ, and brain time is sold on the audience market. Benjamin and Freud saw the new history of consciousness but not the organization of capitalism around the **consumer**, a **libidinal economy** that, by destroying consciousness, destroys desire. All this is a chapter of Weber's **disenchantment**. Medef's 2005 summer university was titled "The re-enchantment of the world" (Denis Kessler, Tony Blair's proposal to move CAP funds to cognitive-technology R&amp;D). Seillière's preface to *Les Dirigeants face au changement* introduced the volume in which Patrick Le Lay explains selling "available brain-time". The rhetorical question: is Medef's re-enchantment a "jumpstart" of spirit, or industrial knowledge *without* spirit? Ars Industrialis is defined as "an international association for an industrial politics of technologies of spirit". Ecological and civilizational stakes: Laurence Tubiana is quoted, though the translation spells her "Toubiana"; Robert Lyon's "age of less"; survival requires overcoming the "age of consumption" through "new, qualitative growth".

## Short quotations

- Arthur on *esprit*: it "expresses a synthesis of the psychic and social, as well as intellectual and historical life of man" (p. viii).
- Introduction: capitalism "called sometimes 'cultural,' sometimes 'cognitive,' but that is before all else the destructive organization of an industrial populism" (p. 1).

## Notes for evaluation

- **Accuracy:** primary text, authoritative for structure and pagination. Note small typos in the English: "contra" for *contre* and "le valeur" for *la valeur* in the preface; "Steigler"; "savior-faire"; "it's lowest point". These suggest light copy-editing. The French cover's attribution to Ars Industrialis is dropped from the English title page, which matters for questions of authorship (see [STRUCTURE.md](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/STRUCTURE/index.md>)).
- **Bias:** the translator's preface interprets as well as translates. Its framing in terms of drives versus desire and the "elixir" of *politesse* is Arthur's gloss.
- **Reliability:** high. The sample runs to about 12 pages of the book's text.
