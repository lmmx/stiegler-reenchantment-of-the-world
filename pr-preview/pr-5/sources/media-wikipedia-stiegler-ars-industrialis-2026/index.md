# Wikipedia coverage

**Is there a dedicated article?** No. Neither **Ars Industrialis** nor the **book** has a standalone Wikipedia article. Both are covered inside the Stiegler biographies.

## Condensed account

- **English Wikipedia, "Bernard Stiegler".** "In June 2005 Stiegler founded a political and cultural group, Ars Industrialis", whose manifesto calls for an "industrial politics of spirit". It names co-founders George Collins, Marc Crépon, Catherine Perret and Caroline Stiegler, and mentions an updated 2010 manifesto. **No inline citations** for these sentences. The bibliography lists *Réenchanter le monde* (2006) "with Marc Crépon, George Collins &amp; Catherine Perret" and the Bloomsbury 2014 translation. It does **not name the translator** and has no description of the book. IRCAM directorship is given without dates. Pompidou directorship of cultural development from 1 Jan 2006, and IRI created April 2006 (cited to the IRI site).
- **French Wikipedia, "Bernard Stiegler".** "En 2005, avec Marc Crépon, \[George Collins, Catherine Perret, Caroline Stiegler\] il fonde l'association Ars Industrialis", with a cited mission phrase "pour une politique industrielle des technologies de l'esprit". IRCAM director 2002 to 1 Jan 2006 (cited). The book is listed under "En collaboration" without a note. **No mention of the 2020 transformation** of the association, and no description of the book or of the Medef context.
- **French Wikipedia, "Temps de cerveau humain disponible".** A well-referenced article (L'Expansion, Nouvel Obs, Le Monde, Acrimed, *Communication &amp; langages*) on Le Lay's 2004 remark. It records Stiegler's reaction in *Le Monde diplomatique* (June 2005) and his role in the 2010 documentary. It is inconsistent about the source book's title (see [context-le-lay-temps-de-cerveau-2004.md](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/sources/context-le-lay-temps-de-cerveau-2004/index.md>)).

## Notes for evaluation

- **Authorship of the book.** EN Wikipedia lists three co-authors (Crépon, Collins, Perret) and omits Caroline Stiegler. The FR article lists four. Library catalogues (e.g. Philharmonie de Paris) credit "Bernard Stiegler et Ars Industrialis \[Marc Crépon, Georges Collins, Catherine Perret et Caroline Stiegler\]", and the Ars Industrialis site and the Tunis motion name the same five founders. **The EN bibliography's three-name list is an error or omission.** Mirrors of the 2006 Wikipedia text spread it further.
- "Founded June 2005" is consistent with the inaugural session of 18 June 2005, but it is uncited.
- The articles are adequate for basic facts and say nothing about the book's argument. Don't use them for content.
