> **Provenance (from the Introduction, p. 8).** Chapters 1 and 2 are "reformulations and elaborations" of papers Stiegler gave at the first two Ars Industrialis working sessions after the inaugural meeting of 18 June 2005. Chapter 1 was updated to spring 2006: it cites events and press from April 2006.

## Abstract (L2)

Chapter 1 argues that society must be **re-founded**, and that this requires a **revolution *of* capitalism**.

- **Diagnosis.** We now live in a **[hyperindustrial](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/concepts/hyperindustrial-society/index.md>)** society, with no way back from industry. Its form is **service capitalism**. Services, delivered through "R technologies, that is, relational technologies" (p. 29), take over the activities through which people once shaped their own lives. They **"discharge" the consumer of his existence**.
- **Mechanism.** Individuation proceeds by **adopting** ways of life. When service capitalism controls adoption, it destroys **[individuation](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/concepts/transindividuation/index.md>)** and generalizes **[proletarianization](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/concepts/proletarianization/index.md>)**. Producers lose *savoir-faire* and consumers lose *savoir-vivre*. The world becomes insipid and the spirit "servile".
- **Associated vs dissociated milieus.** The root problem is the **dissociated milieu**, in which producers and consumers are split. Stiegler draws on Simondon's **[associated milieu](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/concepts/associated-milieu/index.md>)** and argues that the internet is the first industrial technical milieu that is *structurally* associated. That makes possible an **[economy of contribution](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/concepts/economy-of-contribution/index.md>)** modelled on open-source software. This is the remedy, provided public power keeps the internet from being turned into an instrument of control.
- **Libido and the drives.** Capitalism is a **[libidinal economy](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/concepts/libidinal-economy/index.md>)**. Libido, not oil or coal, makes it run. By exhausting libido it becomes a **capitalism of drives**, heading toward **barbarism**.
- **The jumpstart.** Against this Stiegler calls for a **jumpstart (*le sursaut*)**, an "undertaking to found" (*entreprendre de fonder*). It is to be understood through the long history of **[grammatization](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/concepts/grammatization/index.md>)**: writing, like every **[pharmakon](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/concepts/pharmakon/index.md>)**, can found individuation (the Greek *polis*) or ruin it (the sophists).
- **Europe.** The programme is European: a **European public power** and a **European way of life** that put **consistencies** back at the centre of a new industrial model.
- **The plane that enchants me.** The closing section describes the **plane of consistencies or motifs**, the incalculable objects of desire. Stiegler calls it "the plane of enchantment" (p. 58). Capitalism must take care of it if it is to have any future.

## Core claims

1. There is no alternative to an ever more industrial world. "De-industrialization" is a new international division of labour, not an exit from industry (p. 29).
2. Hyperindustrial service capitalism controls attention and behaviour across every segment of existence through **relational (R) technologies**: telecoms and broadcast networks, barcodes, chip cards, RFID, wi-fi and Bluetooth, biometrics and nanotechnologies (p. 29).
3. Services **discharge** the consumer of productive tasks, and so of the chance to decide their own way of living (*façon de vivre*). This is a debilitating reversal of Hegel's master/slave dialectic (pp. 30–31).
4. **Individuation is adoption.** Adoption has a future only if adopters can individuate themselves "otherwise, and by themselves", which intensifies their singularity, their negentropy. Service capitalism controls adoption down to primary and secondary identification, and so destroys individuation (pp. 31–32).
5. **Generalized proletarianization.** Producers lose *savoir-faire* and become labour power. Consumers lose *savoir-vivre* and become purchasing power. Cognitive capitalism liquidates knowledge, producing entropy and distaste and an "enslaved", servile spirit (pp. 32–33).
6. Re-enchantment requires leaving **dissociated milieus** behind. These are milieus where marketing's "modes of employment" replace practices and design short-circuits practical time. Dissociation is "capitalism, which needs a spirit" yet destroys it (pp. 34–35).
7. The internet, unlike earlier industrial apparatuses, "structurally constitutes an associated milieu" (p. 37). Open source shows an **economy of contribution** that should replace the service economy. It can still be captured by user profiling. Whether it is depends on a new public power (pp. 36–37).
8. R technologies are themselves **hypomnemata**, so they are technologies of spirit. They currently serve disindividuation, but they could support individuation. Recorded music shows both sides: loss of listening skills on one hand, jazz on the other (pp. 37–38).
9. The first service is **leisure**: taking charge of people's time and distracting them, as the jester distracted the king. Patrick Le Lay and the "client-king" are the examples. Diverting children's primary identification toward television amounts to **desublimation** (pp. 38–39).
10. **Capitalism runs on libido.** Libido is sublimated drive energy that projects **consistencies** (love, knowledge, art). Industrial exploitation exhausts it. Motivation turns into de-motivation, *philia* and the "we" are destroyed, and capitalism becomes drive-based and barbarous, "denuded of spirit". This links 9/11, Richard Durn (26 March 2002), and the National Front vote of 21 April 2002 (pp. 39–43).
11. The jumpstart is the re-founding of the "human enterprise". It means maintaining the conditions of **meta-transformation**: the capacity to transform how we transform, against the temptation of the worst (Simondon). The American way of life is exhausted and toxic, even though America leads in associated milieus such as the web (pp. 44–47).
12. **Grammatization history.** Hydraulic empires used writing to control. The Greek *polis* turned literal writing into an associated milieu, and so into citizenship. Plato's *Phaedrus* stages the fight over this ***pharmakon***. Nora and Minc (1978) foresaw the convergence of audiovisual, telecom and computing, but missed "tele-cracy" (Berlusconi) and the industrial politics of technologies of spirit (pp. 47–51).
13. **Making the revolution of capitalism.** Re-founding must come from enterprising citizens supported by a new **public power**, on the model of the revolutionary French bourgeoisie of the late eighteenth century. The alternative is financier capitalism and a "tele-cracy" that restores autocracy (pp. 51–53).
14. **A European way of life.** Europe must become a European **public power**. The French "no" to the 2005 constitution expressed a feeling of public *powerlessness*. Saturation by automobiles, information and RFID chips shows where dissociated milieus lead. Young people who have "lost the feeling of existing" (Durn, Brunerie, the November 2005 rioters) mark the limit (pp. 53–58).
15. **The plane that enchants me.** Desire aims only at the incalculable and singular: works of art (Kant's reflective judgement), the beloved. These **consistencies** "do not exist" and must be cared for. A libidinal economy is an *oikos-nomos* of care. Without such care there is no future "of capitalism, or a beyond of capitalism" (pp. 58–60).

## Argumentative moves

- **Etymological arguments.** *Sapere* gives savour, so knowledge makes the world "sapid". *Service* recalls the serfs once charged with chores. *Entreprendre* combines *apprendre*, *comprendre* and *surprendre* (to learn, understand, surprise). Intelligence is *inter-legere*, "being-among". *Économie* is the *nomos* of the *oikos*.
- **Simondon applied to industry.** Individuation, transindividuation, the associated milieu (the Guimbal turbine) and the "temptation" toward a lower level of individuation are all carried over into a critique of the service economy.
- **Freud reread economically.** Libido is the energy of capitalism. Its exhaustion explains motivation crises and acting out. Freud's *Civilization and Its Discontents* is said to anticipate Nazism.
- **A deep history of technics as the frame for policy.** The chapter runs from Sumerian tablets to RFID implants, so that the digital appears as a new stage of grammatization: both poison and cure.
- **Dated case studies (2005–06).** A Nice rape filmed on a phone; Skyblogs; Applied Digital Solutions' implantable chip; INRIA/JCDecaux; Microsoft Research's "Intelligent Environments" symposium (Cambridge, 5–7 April 2006); the Béarn hunger strike; Seillière and Le Lay.
- **Reformist framing.** "Revolution *of* capitalism", led by "revolutionary capitalists". But Stiegler also writes that capitalism "is certainly not eternal" and speaks of a possible "beyond of capitalism" (pp. 37, 60).

| 29–30 | **The hyperindustrial epoch as service capitalism** | There is no future except in a more industrial world. In hyperindustrial society all forms of life become objects of rationalization, investment and service. This continues a process of **grammatization**, defined in note 2 as the discretization of human behaviours that makes them reproducible. Its new **hypomnemata** are both control technologies and potential supports of "a new age of spirit", a hope voiced by UNESCO (n. 4) and by business. De-industrialization relocates production; service capitalism is just as industrial. Cheap personal apparatuses make all of existence an object of permanent control of attention and behaviour through **"R technologies," that is, relational technologies** (p. 29; n. 5 cites Rifkin, *The Age of Access*): telecom and broadcast networks, barcode and chip-card readers, RFID (n. 6), wi-fi, Bluetooth, biometrics, nanotechnologies. Service enterprises thus become the main actors of public life, and steering how ways of life evolve becomes a matter of taking control. |
| 30–31 | **The consumer discharged of his existence** | Every epoch's social dynamism is the permanent transformation of ways of life, that is, psychic and collective **individuation** (n. 7: individuation is not individualization; Simondon). Humans cannot merely subsist; they "ex-sist" by transforming themselves. Societies are systems of cooperation and competition (Greek *eris*), now co-piloting their individuation at planetary scale. Service industries transform ways of life but **short-circuit transindividuation**: one's existence changes without one's participation. The consumer, relieved of productive tasks, is "relatively disaffected". The relief is sold as a "discharge" (as serfs were once "charged" with chores), but it divests the person of their existence and of the power to choose a way of living. Stiegler calls this a reversal and a debilitation of Hegel's master/slave dialectic (n. 9). |
| 31–33 | **Hyperindustrial service societies as the destruction of processes of individuation through the control of processes of adoption** | Nobody decides how they live; we reproduce or adopt ways of life (family, education, foreign cultures, merchandise, evangelism, culture industries). "The process of individuation is essentially a process of adoption" (n. 10: *Technics and Time 3*). Civilizations form by inventing and adopting: China, whose characters are an Asian *hypomnematon*; Alexander's mutual religious adoptions in Egypt. Adoption has a future only if adopters can individuate "otherwise, and by themselves", raising their singularity or **negentropy** (n. 12). By controlling adoption down to primary and secondary identification (n. 13: disidentification; *Télécratie*), hyperindustrial capitalism destroys individuation. Using information and communication technologies (ICTs) as control rather than individuation technologies, it generalizes **proletarianization**: producers lose *savoir-faire* and become labour power; consumers lose *savoir-vivre*, which becomes a "mode of employment", and are left as purchasing power. Life loses its savour (*sapere*/sapid). Cognitive capitalism (Negri to Seillière) and the knowledge society (Blair, UNESCO, Serres) are announced just as capitalism liquidates knowledge and produces entropy and distaste. Marc Jimenez's "mystification of the masses" (n. 14) lacks an account of grammatization and a **general organology** (n. 15: co-evolution of physiological, technical and social organs). The result is an **"enslaved spirit"** serving service societies (p. 33): general ill-being, with hyperconsumption as only one symptom (n. 16: the Ars Industrialis session "Suffering and Consumption", 25 Feb 2006; against Lipovetsky). Civilization's discontent has become the discontent of consumption, "particularly in France". |
| 33–37 | **Re-enchanting the world faced with the unhappy destiny of consumption** | There was a happy postwar age of consumption, though Debord in the 1950s already announced its unhappy destiny. That destiny arrived with the generalized service economy. The industrial economy supplied material goods (appliances); the **service economy destroys the social game itself**, i.e. individuation as a participatory process (Simondon, *IPC* p. 18). **Language** is the model of a participatory milieu: speakers individuate themselves and the language at once. One does not "use" a language, any more than one "uses" a piano (n. 19; Crépon). The service economy rests on designers controlling consumers' behaviour, so consumers find no material through which to individuate and become **disindividuated** ("life time value", n. 20). Re-enchantment requires leaving **dis-sociated milieus**: there, design upstream of production short-circuits practical time and marketing's uses replace practices. Consumers are proletarianized as producers were. Fordism generalizes this; when it reaches symbolic milieus it causes great suffering. Dissociation is capitalism, "which needs a spirit" and is destroying it (n. 21). **Associated milieus** (pp. 36–37): Simondon's concept, illustrated by the Guimbal turbine cooled by the very water that drives it. Digital hypomnemata form technical and industrial associated milieus in which addressees are in principle in the position of designers. The internet, an associated, participatory and dialogic milieu since 1992, makes possible "the **economy of contribution** typical of open-source software" (Linux; users as practitioners). This is the basis of a new industrial model to replace dissociated milieus, which are "ANTISOCIAL". Any associated milieu can be turned toward control (user profiling, n. 23). What is new is that the internet "structurally constitutes an associated milieu" (p. 37). That novelty must become the object of a public politics, so that the associated digital milieu is not "instrumentalized by R technologies" and so that a new spirit of capitalism can emerge, "while waiting for the replacement of the latter by another form of society". |
| 37–39 | **R technologies and the new spiritual devices** | Individuation needs cultivated, associated social milieus and develops all three forms of knowledge (*savoir-vivre*, *savoir-faire*, theoretical). Control devices are also **hypomnemata**. Cognitive and cultural industries currently use them in dissociated milieus as control or R technologies (n. 24), yet they remain **technologies of spirit** and could become supports of individuation. Music shows both sides: with Nicolas Donin, the "machinic revolving of the sensibility" (n. 25) lost listeners their musical knowledge, yet the same recording devices produced jazz as a new form of writing and individuation. The canalization of attention by culture industries was the first service: taking charge of people's time and distracting them, like the jester distracting the king. Patrick Le Lay confirms the point, and "to distract" also means to steal: the "client-king's" attention is hijacked for prescriptions of consumption, replacing free judgement with systematic conditioning. Children become helpless, herd-like, impulsive, overinformed and disaffected. Counter-tendencies (the web, blogs) are hijacked too (Skyrock's Skyblogs), leading toward "the war of all against all". **Spiritual misery**: a rape in Nice at the end of 2005 was filmed on a phone by the aggressor (n. 26). This tendency leads to the **capitalism of drives**. Television diverts the **primary identification** by which children inherit from parents. Dissociation becomes **desublimation**, liquidating the family and the psyche. |
| 39–44 | **Overcoming the capitalism of drives and fighting its becoming-barbaric** | The promised knowledge-based "cognitive" or "cultural" capitalism will not happen while the model of dissociated milieus endures. Firms profit from dissociation in the short term and are self-destructive in the long term. Capitalism has rested on controlling concepts and affects through R technologies, the "control" Deleuze named, with **television** as its principal instrument (n. 27: Deleuze, letter to Serge Daney). Control destroys public space and time and breeds **uncontrollable societies**. **Libido** is socialized, sublimated sexual energy; societies are apparatuses for canalizing it into roles, civilities and *savoir-vivre*. It projects **consistencies** (love, *sophia*, science, art), i.e. objects that "do not exist". The libidinal economy *economizes*: it defers satisfaction (Derrida's *différance*; pleasure is not *jouissance*) and binds life and death drives (Freud after 1920; *Civilization and Its Discontents* foreshadows Nazism). Libido, "neither oil nor coal" nor any other energy, makes capital function. **Motivation** (Bergson's "gods") is produced under conditions that now invert it into de-motivation: indifference, disaffection, disgust, hate (n. 30). Exhausting libido destroys the "we", *philia* and *affectio societatis*; financierization accelerates this. Capitalism becomes barbarous, "denuded of spirit", with no "desire for the future". This connects 9/11, Richard Durn (Nanterre, 26 Mar 2002) and the National Front vote (21 Apr 2002). Traditional societies took care of this energy under names such as *philia*, *eros*, *agape*, *caritas*, *mana*. The man of hyperindustrial **society of control** is infantilized and stripped of initiative. When "discharge" becomes capital's main revenue source it becomes **disindividuation**, eliminating experience (n. 31: the self-driving car whose freed time is sold as "available brain"; Rifkin's "marketization of experience"). |
| 44–47 | **The jumpstart (*le sursaut*): starting over again** | Opposing foreign ways of life is not the point; societies leapt forward by adopting them. The question is the conditions under which new ways of life form, i.e. **meta-transformation**: knowing how to transform how we transform. Transformation can lead to the best or the worst (dissociating, drive-based, barbarous). Simondon describes the "fundamental temptation" in individuation, a regression to a lower level (n. 32). Meta-transformation is the function of primary parental identification and of a **superego** produced through criticism of the inherited one. **America** invented a style of transforming ways of life ("the American way of life") that is now exhausted and toxic (n. 33): it substitutes the police society (*société policière*) for civil society (*société policée*) and provokes fundamentalisms. Yet America also revived associated milieus with the web, exploiting European public research while Europe imitated and subsidized it. The fashionable talk of "winners and losers" ignores that everyone loses from carelessness: Chernobyl's cloud "stopping" at the French border; a "lose/lose" tendency toward disindividuation. Hence the need for a **jumpstart**, a public good and a rupture. Enterprise needs a **spirit of enterprise**, not a "debilitated", "rotten" or "servile" spirit. Humans are spontaneously enterprising (*entreprendre*, *apprendre*, *comprendre*, *surprendre*), so the task is to "UNDERTAKE A FOUNDATION (ENTREPRENDRE DE FONDER)" (p. 46). When enterprises and societies hit a limit they must take a leap (*sauter un pas*): a jumpstart is a rupture (p. 47). |
| 47–51 | **Grammatization and individuation—yesterday, today, and tomorrow** | The **drop in the value of spirit** is caused by technologies of spirit reduced to control (n. 34), and it can be fought only with the same technologies. The poison is the remedy: the **pharmakon** (Plato, Derrida, n. 35), reanalysed in *Mécréance et discrédit 3* as a question of **socio-therapy**, of **care** for individuation, "the political question par excellence", which passes through industry. Technologies of spirit result from **grammatization** (n. 36), which over millennia lets civilizations re-found themselves when they reach their limits. Ancient Greece: the founders of cities (nomothetes: Thales, Solon) invented a new psychic and collective individuation, the *polis*. Hydraulic empires (Egypt, Mesopotamia) had used writing to control floods, merchandise and slaves under royal scribes. Via the Phoenicians, literal writing became in Greece a **new relation between the psychic and the collective**: the citizen. Nora and Minc (1978, n. 37) compared informatics to the Sumerian invention of writing. Remarks: (1) literal writing turned the **associated milieu** of language into a principle of association: the *logos* overturned the dynastic principle. (2) The *Phaedrus* makes writing a **pharmakon**: the *tekhnè tou biou* (art of living) of the citizen, and also the sophists' instrument for manipulating opinion, leading to *stasis* (civil war). (3) Today **industrial populism** and tele-cratic television form a process of disindividuation. Nora and Minc foresaw convergence but missed "tele-cracy" (Berlusconi, Giscard d'Estaing) and the alternatives opened by the ambiguity of these *pharmaka*. (4) The stakes are the same as Plato's: (a) founding and (b) caring for individuation against the disindividuation every pharmakon carries. Machines (n. 38: Taylor) and then appliances (n. 39) exteriorize knowledge into modes of employment, which is proletarianization. (5) The foremost task is the **re-foundation of the human enterprise**: digital technology is the *pharmakon* that opens the reconstitution of associated milieus, an **ecology of spirit**. |
| 51–53 | **Making the revolution of capitalism** | Political re-foundation rests on re-socializing an industrial technology and inventing a **new type of economic enterprise of services**. That can be done only by "enterprising citizens", aroused and supported by a public politics of industrial development of technologies of spirit, led by a new form of public strength (*puissance*) that associates public authorities, private economic powers and "the public". Television has been absorbed into a larger convergence with digital hypomnemata. It has become drive-based, "the secular arm" of a capitalism grown barbarous, irresponsible and financialized; industrial democracy risks collapsing into a **tele-cracy** that restores autocracy for deterritorialized financial oligarchies. To be enterprising today is to look after the possibility of being enterprising tomorrow. The Greek *polis* was also a new economy, arousing *eris* (rivalry) limited by ***dikē*** (justice) and ***aidōs*** (shame) (n. 41). Economy is never just subsistence; it is also a libidinal economy of existence that projects "planes of consistency". Hence the call to make, "along with revolutionary capitalists (like the French bourgeoisie of the end of the eighteenth century)", **the revolution of a capitalism "on the road to collapse"** (p. 53). |
| 53–58 | **A European way of life** | The *polis* opens public space and time where public power serves a public good on which interests converge. That convergence is itself an associated milieu. Europe must become a **European public power** and not a "space of mere police" where merchandise and consumers circulate under surveillance. The 2005 constitution was rejected because it seemed to offer public **powerlessness**: Béarn deputy Jean Lassalle's hunger strike against a Japanese firm while Chirac, Villepin and Sarkozy competed for attention (n. 42). Public power must fight tendencies toward dissociation, incivility and barbarism, and must be international, because grammatization is global. Only public action can resolve the contradiction in which Seillière praises cognitive capitalism while, in the book he prefaced, Le Lay sells "available brain time" (n. 43). The answer is a new industrial model, "a European way of life". Intelligence is social (*inter-legere*). Weber's spirit of capitalism has been lost to the **control of affects**: libido becomes drive, and affective saturation leads to disaffection. **Saturation** problems follow: cars, cognitive overload online, the senses, and soon the skin, through **RFID implants** (Applied Digital Solutions' rice-grain chip; INRIA/JCDecaux bus-shelter messaging; AFP, 11 Mar 2005, on Australia) and Amazon-style profiling that grammatizes singularities into calculable particularities. "Invasive informatics": Microsoft Research's "Intelligent Environments" (5–7 Apr 2006), transreality games, *otaku*. Hyper-solicitation **short-circuits transindividuation**: dis-identification, loss of attention, "incivility" (Finkielkraut), attention-deficit disorder. These are technologies of spirit put in the service of control; they breed the uncontrollable and the **reign of stupidity**, despite UNESCO's rhetoric at Tunis. Aesthetics has served production and consumption for a century, yielding symbolic and spiritual misery. People who have "lost the feeling of existing" (Durn; Maxime Brunerie, who attempted to assassinate the President on 14 July 2001; young people arrested in the November 2005 riots, per psychiatrist Cyril Canetti) show the stakes. A new European way of life must put back the **consistencies** that support existence, an industry that intensifies the incalculable: "the plane of motifs, which, like the horizon of the Muses, is the plane of enchantment" (p. 58). |
| 58–60 | **The plane that enchants me** | Capitalism needs **motivation**, a kind of enchantment; if nothing changes it will destroy that and us. Singularity alone is the object of libido. Since Newton everything can be calculated, but desire knows only the **incalculable**, "a plane other than that of the calculable". Mathematicians' idealities and axioms belong to it too. The work of art is singular: Kant's **reflective judgement** ("this is beautiful") cannot be proven, only experienced (*éprouvé*). Its universality is "by default". Singularity "does not exist" but *consists* in my existence and my participation in collective individuation. Likewise with the beloved: however profilable her behaviours (loyalty cards, RFID, Amazon), love is not drive. These consistencies are "the plane that enchants me", and they must be treated "with the greatest care". A libidinal *eco-nomy* (*nomos* of the *oikos*) is what takes care of them. Capitalism has left this care, called culture, in ruins while calling itself "cultural" and "cognitive". Hence the need to revolutionize it: there will be no future "of capitalism, or a beyond of capitalism, which is certainly not eternal" unless that future takes care of the future of the objects of desire (p. 60). |
