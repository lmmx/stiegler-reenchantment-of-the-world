## Abstract (L2)

A three-page note by the American translator. It explains two choices that shape how English readers meet the book: leaving ***savoir-faire*** and ***savoir-vivre*** untranslated, and the inadequacy of "spirit" for ***esprit***. On the way it gives a compact gloss of the book's problem: **industrial populism** proletarianizes us twice, as workers and as consumers. Its remedy is the **value of spirit**.

## Core claims

- The French is technically easy but syntactically intricate. Long nested clauses are kept where possible.
- ***Savoir-faire*** = the craftsman's, *techne*-bound knowledge. Losing it to machines is **[proletarianization](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/concepts/proletarianization/index.md>)**.
- ***Savoir-vivre*** = social grace and care for self and others (*politesse*, heard through *polis*). Losing it in the role of consumer is a second proletarianization. It is glossed through Simondon's **[individuation and transindividuation](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/concepts/transindividuation/index.md>)**.
- In consumer society desire is reduced to **drives**, sublimation is cancelled, and needs and wants are confused. This is the problem of **[industrial populism](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/concepts/industrial-populism/index.md>)**.
- ***Valeur esprit*** (no "of" in French). ***[Esprit](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/concepts/spirit/index.md>)*** joins the psychic, social, intellectual and historical. It is where memory and intelligence meet in reflection.

## Section-by-section (L3)

| pp. | Move |
| --- | --- |
| vii | Difficulty lies in style, not lexicon. Justifies leaving *savoir-faire* in French: its double sense of general practical wisdom and the specific skill of a *techne*. The worker "stripped of his savoir-faire" is proletarianized, and service industries now saturate personal life too. |
| viii | *Savoir-vivre*: the consumer relinquishes it and is "proletarianized all over again". Drives versus desire; sublimation; addiction ("addictive and toxic environment"). Introduces "industrial populism" and "the value of spirit" as the book's poles. |
| viii–ix | *Esprit* versus "spirit": common root *spiritus* (breath). *Esprit* as a synthesis tied to individuation ("one becomes who he is") and transindividuation ("we become who we are"). The closing image is of the world able to "sing again" through a revaluation of desire over drives and association over discord. |

## Notes

- **Interpretive (labelled):** the preface already frames the book in the *later* vocabulary of drives, addiction and care. That framing fits the 2010 Manifesto better than the 2006 chapters.
- The text has small typos ("contra", "le valeur", "Steigler", "savior-faire"). Reviewers such as Patrick Lyons (see [reception.md](<https://lmmx.github.io/stiegler-reenchantment-of-the-world/pr-preview/pr-5/reception/index.md>)) credit the preface with clarifying "spirit".
