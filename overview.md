## 1. What kind of book this is

*Réenchanter le monde* (Flammarion, 2006; English 2014) is not a treatise. It is the **founding dossier of Ars Industrialis**, the "international association for an industrial politics of technologies of spirit" that Bernard Stiegler set up in 2005 with George Collins, Marc Crépon, Catherine Perret and Caroline Stiegler. It contains:

- a polemical **Introduction** ("What Is to Be Done?", 2006);
- the association's **manifestos** (2005, and in the English edition only, 2010);
- two essays by Stiegler: **Ch. 1** "The Re-Foundation of Society" (2006) and **Ch. 2** "Against the Reign of Ignorance" (from a September 2005 talk);
- the **motion** the association published on the eve of the 2005 UN World Summit on the Information Society in Tunis.

Composition ran 2005 → 2006, plus a 2010 add-on, which is not the printed order. See [STRUCTURE.md](STRUCTURE.md).

## 2. The argument arc

1. **Provocation.** In 2005 the French employers' federation (Medef) took "the re-enchantment of the world" as the slogan of its summer university, to sell the "knowledge economy". Stiegler takes the slogan at its word. Re-enchantment would require raising the **[value of spirit](concepts/spirit.md)**, the faculty Valéry saw falling in 1939. The milieu that coined the slogan is the one lowering that value. Its emblem is TF1's boast of selling "available brain time" to Coca-Cola. Stiegler's name for this is **[industrial populism](concepts/industrial-populism.md)**.
2. **Diagnosis: libidinal economy and its exhaustion.** Twentieth-century capitalism organized consumption by capturing desire through the culture industries, a **[libidinal economy](concepts/libidinal-economy.md)**. That capture has turned into its destruction: desire degrades into **drives**, sublimation fails, and attention is "short-circuited". The outcome is a **[symbolic misery](concepts/symbolic-misery.md)**, and a **[disenchantment](concepts/disenchantment.md)** more radical than Weber's.
3. **Mechanism: control of adoption and proletarianization.** **[Hyperindustrial](concepts/hyperindustrial-society.md)** service capitalism controls how people *adopt* ways of life, and with it their **[individuation](concepts/transindividuation.md)**. Knowledge is formalized into apparatuses and services and lost by individuals. Producers lose *savoir-faire*, consumers lose *savoir-vivre*, and knowledge workers lose theoretical knowledge. This is generalized **[proletarianization](concepts/proletarianization.md)**.
4. **Ground: memory is technical.** Human memory and knowledge have always been externalized, through **[grammatization](concepts/grammatization.md)**, **[tertiary retention](concepts/tertiary-retention.md)** and ***[hypomnemata](concepts/hypomnemata.md)***. The problem is not technology as such but whether a society organizes the **re-interiorization** of its memory technologies. Schools did this for writing. Today the **program industries** out-compete the program institutions.
5. **Contradiction of the "knowledge society".** Commodified information is **entropic**. Knowledge is **negentropic**. An economy that reduces knowledge to information risks a **reign of ignorance** ([knowledge vs information](concepts/knowledge-vs-information.md)).
6. **Remedy: an industrial politics of spirit.** The same technologies are **[technologies of spirit](concepts/technologies-of-spirit.md)**. They could support a new age of individuation if a **new public power**, working with industry and taking a long view against **[short-termism](concepts/short-termism.md)**, invested in instruments of knowledge, reformed media and education, and championed a **European way of life**. Already in Ch. 1, and systematically in the 2010 Manifesto, this is framed as **[pharmacology](concepts/pharmakon.md)**: every technics is both poison and remedy. It calls for an **[economy of contribution](concepts/economy-of-contribution.md)** built on **[care](concepts/care.md)** and **[associated milieus](concepts/associated-milieu.md)**, and for reconquering free time as ***[otium](concepts/otium-negotium.md)***.

## 4. The Ars Industrialis project

- **Founded** spring 2005 in Paris (manifesto; the association was active roughly 2005–2020). Public sessions were held at the Théâtre de la Colline and Centre Pompidou. It later ran working groups ("techniques of the self", "relational technologies", "economy of contribution"), a *Vocabulaire*, and the summer school **pharmakon.fr** (from 2010). It worked closely with the IRI (Institut de recherche et d'innovation, Centre Pompidou), which built contributory annotation tools such as *Lignes de temps*.
- **Self-image**: a research collective modelled, by its own account, on the early Frankfurt School, aimed at **public power and industry**, not at party politics.
- **Political register**: European, reformist, interventionist. It calls for a "new spirit of capitalism", not its abolition. Whether that is a strength or a weakness is a matter for [reception.md](reception.md).
